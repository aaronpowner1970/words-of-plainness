    title: "Mastery of Humility"
    default: "reflect"
    reflect:
      prompt: "<p>Yehoshua spent thirty years in obscurity before three years in ministry. Where in your own life are you tempted to rush past the quiet seasons of preparation? What might God be forming in you during those seasons that cannot be formed any other way?</p><p>The chapter describes humility as making others feel safe: \"Sensing no competition, no threat, it allows me to let down my guard.\" Who in your life models this kind of humility—and what does it feel like to be near them?</p>"
    journal:
      prompt: "<p>The chapter says, \"One does not announce one's own humility.\" Write about a recent moment when you either practiced genuine humility quietly or when the desire for recognition crept in. What did you learn about yourself?</p>"
    witness:
      prompt: "<p>Yehoshua deflected glory to the Father even when directly praised. Has there been a moment when you set aside your own credit or comfort for someone else—and felt something unexpected in the act? Share what you witnessed in yourself.</p>"

  - id: "pause-obedience"
    title: "Mastery of Obedience"
    default: "reflect"
    reflect:
      prompt: "<p>The chapter presents a striking reframe: obedience to Christ is not the surrender of the true self—it is the rescue of the self. Does that ring true in your experience, or does it feel like a claim that still needs to be tested?</p><p>\"The Lord does not stand at the gate of discipleship as a foreman with a longer list of tasks. He stands there as the one who has already borne the heaviest load.\" What would it mean for you personally to stop carrying your burden alone?</p>"
    journal:
      prompt: "<p>The author describes paying tithes as a young father while afraid—and finding that \"each time I chose obedience He kept His promise.\" Write about a time when obedience cost you something. What happened after?</p>"
    witness:
      prompt: "<p>Yehoshua walked away from an easy crown because it was not the Father's will. Have you ever walked away from something good because you sensed it wasn't the right thing? What did that cost you, and what did it teach you?</p>"

  - id: "pause-compassion"
    title: "Mastery of Compassion"
    default: "reflect"
    reflect:
      prompt: "<p>\"Jesus wept\"—not from grief at Lazarus's death, but choosing to walk the full distance of human grief with a family in mourning. He didn't have to. Who in your life right now needs someone to enter their grief rather than fix it from a distance?</p><p>The chapter distinguishes between knowing about the suffering of others and entering into it. Where is that line for you—and what holds you back from crossing it?</p>"
    journal:
      prompt: "<p>The author names the hands that reached for him in his darkest season. Write about a pair of hands—real or metaphorical—that reached for you when you were in crisis. What did that act of Christlike compassion make possible in your life?</p>"
    witness:
      prompt: "<p>\"Grace at its most Christlike is compassion combined with the wisdom to know when and how to get involved.\" Share a moment when you witnessed or practiced this—when compassion moved past sympathy into real presence. What changed as a result?</p>"

  - id: "pause-closing"
    title: "Yehoshua the Man"
    default: "reflect"
    reflect:
      prompt: "<p>\"You are inside His prayer.\" Jesus prayed for the person reading this chapter by name in the eternal sense. Sit with that for a moment. What does it do to you to know that He saw you—not just your potential, but you, specifically, in this moment?</p><p>The chapter ends with a benediction: His heart still beats for you. Will yours begin to beat for Him? What is the most honest answer you can give to that question right now?</p>"
    journal:
      prompt: "<p>You have now sat with Yehoshua across eight dimensions of His character. Which one moved you most, and why? Write freely—not what you think the right answer is, but what actually landed in your heart as you read.</p>"
    witness:
      prompt: "<p>The chapter closes: \"Beholding transforms the beholder into the same image, from glory to glory.\" Has studying His character changed anything in how you see yourself, or what you believe is possible for you? This is the testimony this chapter was written to produce. Share what you witnessed.</p>"

<h2 id="invocation">{% paraspan 0 %}{% sentence 0 %}Invocation{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 1 %}{% sentence 1 %}By the time the first sandals scuff dust into the air above ancient dirt roads, every household is already in motion, each person drawn forward by the same quiet force that still moves us today in our modern age of shoes and pavement: the need to eat, to provide, to compete, to endure another day.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 2 %}The dust and duty of life cannot be ignored.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 3 %}And it can become all-consuming if we allow it.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 2 %}{% sentence 4 %}She did not think of herself as distracted.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 5 %}She thought of herself as responsible.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 6 %}The fire needed tending, the guests needed feeding, and no one else seemed to notice.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 7 %}She had no idea that one day the whole world would know her name.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 8 %}Martha, sister to Mary, both sisters of Lazarus of Bethany.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 9 %}Their home in Judea—conveniently near Jerusalem—was a favorite resting place for God who walked among humans.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 3 %}{% sentence 10 %}Mary and Martha labored daily within this pressing reality of "dust and duty," especially when He visited their home.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 11 %}They had heard the stories, considered the gospel message of the long awaited Messiah.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 12 %}They had prayed, experimented upon the word, embraced Him and His teachings, and bent their lives to His service.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 13 %}This is the question explored in chapter six: How would you know the truth of Him?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 14 %}If He were introduced to you as the Christ, how would you know it's true?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 4 %}{% sentence 15 %}This chapter presents a very different question from chapter six.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 16 %}How would you know Him personally?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 17 %}Not just the truth of His identity.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 18 %}How would you relate to Him socially?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 19 %}How would your soul respond to His personality and bearing?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 20 %}Would you be in the right frame of mind to feel His spiritual presence?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 21 %}Ready to see Him for who He is, rather than who you might expect based on two thousand year old records, traditions, and creeds?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 22 %}Would you be comfortable in His presence, or would you feel awkward?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 5 %}{% sentence 23 %}We know that children ran toward Him{% cite "ce-mark1013" %}{% cite "ce-3ne1721" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 24 %}Men untrained in theology responded instantly to His call{% cite "ce-matt418" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 25 %}Crowds listening to Him forgot to eat{% cite "ce-mark634" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 26 %}The powerful among Israel came to speak to Him in secret{% cite "ce-john31" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 27 %}Desperate souls pressed through walls of people and tore through rooftops to obtain His personal blessing{% cite "ce-mark525" %}{% cite "ce-luke517" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 28 %}His enemies were confused by Him and often failed to execute their own orders{% cite "ce-john746" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 29 %}A tax collector climbed a tree just to catch a glimpse of Him{% cite "ce-luke191" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 6 %}{% sentence 30 %}If He walked into a church meeting dressed as a parishioner, but by His will your eyes were kept from recognizing Him—like the disciples on the road to Emmaus{% cite "ce-luke2413" %}—would we be too distracted by hymns, and prayers, and wiggling children, and the normal business and gossip of congregational life?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 31 %}Would we know Him?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 32 %}How would we know Him?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 7 %}{% sentence 33 %}You have a Mary and Martha decision to make.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 34 %}You can be like Mary, recognizing the value of His character and kneeling at His feet to learn from Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 35 %}You can be like Martha, honorably busy serving the needs of others, but not recognizing the special moment for what it was{% cite "ce-luke1038" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 8 %}{% sentence 36 %}This isn't to say that we stop tending to the mortal essentials, but that we must be ready whenever He calls to stop, listen, and obey.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 37 %}We must also learn to be in the firm habit of setting down our cares and concerns regularly to make moments for Him.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 9 %}{% sentence 38 %}The prophet Joshua also presented to the world this same challenge: "Choose for yourselves this day whom you will serve"{% cite "ce-josh2415" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 10 %}{% sentence 39 %}This is not all—not for a disciple.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 40 %}This isn't a one-and-done choice.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 41 %}Choose today.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 42 %}And then tomorrow, choose again.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 43 %}If you ever fail to make the same choice, then get back up, shake the dust off of your sandals and make the choice once again to serve the Lord.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 11 %}{% sentence 44 %}What would it be like for Yehoshua to enter a room today or walk into a busy marketplace?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 45 %}Would He be noticed, or would He blend in?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 46 %}What would it be like to be near Him as just another member of a community?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 47 %}What would His presence say of Him to any that took notice?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 48 %}His attitude, His posture, His behavior?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 49 %}Before He ever opened His mouth, what would you learn of Him?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 12 %}{% sentence 50 %}The value of this chapter is in the sincere character study of Jesus Christ, the human personality of the Word Made Flesh.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 51 %}This topic is the centerpiece of our effort to respond to His command, "Learn of Me"{% cite "ce-matt1129" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 13 %}{% sentence 52 %}Let us feast upon the scriptures to learn of our God Made Flesh.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 53 %}Don't delegate this task to others—not even to preachers or teachers.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 54 %}It is as much your responsibility to learn of Jesus Christ as to personally confess Him as your Lord and Savior or to follow Him as an expression of your love for Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 55 %}Our purpose here is contemplative: sit with Yehoshua the Man, study Him honestly.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 56 %}Who is He?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 57 %}What kind of person is He?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 14 %}{% sentence 58 %}This chapter asks: "What is He like—what manner of man is this"{% cite "ce-matt827" %}?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 15 %}{% sentence 59 %}The portrait that follows is organized around a single claim: that grace and truth are the most defining character traits of the Son of God{% cite "ce-john114" %}—and that every other attribute, action, and reaction of Yehoshua flows from this foundation.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 16 %}{% sentence 60 %}His character is not the absence of human feeling but the perfection of it: every impulse governed by love, every response shaped by wisdom, every choice submitted to the Father.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 61 %}He is the mirror we hold up to see ourselves honestly.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-humility">{% paraspan 17 %}{% sentence 62 %}Mastery of Humility: Grace Embodied{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 18 %}{% sentence 63 %}Yehoshua spent roughly thirty years doing manual labor and only three years in ministry.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 64 %}That ratio itself is a testament to His humility.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 65 %}God in the flesh chose to quietly shape wood and stone in the privacy of an insignificant village before He ever shaped souls and performed miracles publicly.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 19 %}{% sentence 66 %}Shortly before His triumphant reception in the city, Yehoshua had left Galilee to walk the road to Jerusalem when a young man came running to meet Him along the way, falling to his knees before the Master.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 67 %}The young man said, "Good teacher, what must I do to inherit eternal life?"{% endsentence %} {% sentence 68 %}Of course, Yehoshua knew the answer better than anyone.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 69 %}He had taught the gospel of salvation in many forms to many people.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 70 %}Still, rather than answering directly, Yehoshua reacted with graceful humility.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 71 %}"Why do you call me good? No one is good—except God alone"{% cite "ce-mark1017" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 72 %}He did then teach the answer, but first redirected glory to the Father.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 20 %}{% sentence 73 %}From His early years, He could have enjoyed the same celebrity status that welcomed Him to Jerusalem on the day of His triumphal entry—when a vast throng of believers accepted Him as their Messiah and King of the Jews.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 74 %}Even on that glorious day, when all seemed to be going well, He rode into the adoring masses riding a humble donkey instead of a warhorse{% cite "ce-matt211" %}{% cite "ce-zech99" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 21 %}{% sentence 75 %}Paul taught us to emulate Yehoshua's humility in his letter to the people of Philippi.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 76 %}"In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus: Who, being in very nature God, did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage; rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant, being made in human likeness"{% cite "ce-phil25" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 22 %}{% sentence 77 %}Yehoshua's example is what grace looks like in the form of humility.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 78 %}He did not have to proclaim His mastery of humility.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 79 %}He lived it in His everyday relationships.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 23 %}{% sentence 80 %}My mother used to remind me that one does not announce one's own humility.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 81 %}Throughout my life, I have never felt safer than when I am with genuinely humble people.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 82 %}Sensing no competition, no threat, it allows me to let down my guard.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 83 %}Humility in others allows us to see deeper than the facade of ego to real depth of character.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 84 %}This is what it must have felt like to be near Yehoshua.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 85 %}To some it may have been frightening or suspicious; to disciples it feels like coming home.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-obedience">{% paraspan 24 %}{% sentence 86 %}Mastery of Obedience: Grace Received{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 25 %}{% sentence 87 %}Many Christians identify Christ's ultimate act of obedience to the Father by the words of surrender in Gethsemane: "Not my will, but thine, be done"{% cite "ce-luke2242" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 88 %}This moment will be studied in another chapter.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 89 %}Here, let us observe His daily examples of obedience as evidence of His character.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 26 %}{% sentence 90 %}As a dutiful son, Yehoshua obeyed His mortal parents and respected His elders.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 91 %}Both obedience and discipline were required to learn the artisan skills of carpentry and stonework from Joseph.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 92 %}Mary would have benefited from His obedience in performing chores around the house, and running errands to neighbors and the market.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 93 %}He willingly obeyed the laws and traditions of attending to regular studies and worship at synagogue.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 27 %}{% sentence 94 %}The concept of "roots before fruits" applies to this study of Christ's trait of obedience{% cite "ce-ps13" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 95 %}Yehoshua's mortal character was not whole and complete from birth.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 96 %}His development was molded by perfect submission to the Father{% cite "ce-john638" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 97 %}He received grace from the Father as He "learned obedience" from suffering, sacrifice, and the day-to-day choices needed to submit to the will of the Father{% cite "ce-heb58" %}{% cite "ce-john530" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 98 %}He grew in wisdom, stature, and favor with both God and man{% cite "ce-luke240" %}{% cite "ce-luke252" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 28 %}{% sentence 99 %}He learned mortal obedience the same way God teaches from heaven: "line upon line, precept upon precept"{% cite "ce-isa2810" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 100 %}His obedience was not automatic; it was chosen, costly, and deepened through mortal experience.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 101 %}His natural character was developed further by His mortal experience.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 102 %}This informs us about our own capacity to progress.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 29 %}{% sentence 103 %}After the miracle of feeding the five thousand from a single boy's lunch, the crowd was astonished to the point that they were ready to make Him ruler of Israel immediately.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 104 %}"When Jesus therefore perceived that they would come and take him by force, to make him a king, he departed again into a mountain himself alone"{% cite "ce-john615" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 105 %}He walked away from the easy path to kingship—offered freely.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 106 %}The people wanted Him for their earthly king.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 107 %}But the Father's plan was not a political throne in Galilee.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 108 %}The Son knew the path and obeyed.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 109 %}He traded the "easy crown" for Gethsemane, a betrayal, a trial, a cross, and a borrowed tomb in Jerusalem.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 110 %}He did not turn from the path.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 111 %}This is what obedience looks like in its highest form—not only the refusal of evil, but the refusal of good that is not God's will.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 30 %}{% sentence 112 %}I remember sitting with paychecks in hand while watching my young children play around me.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 113 %}For years as a young father, I worried about how it would affect them each time I chose to pay tithes and make charitable offerings.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 114 %}The widow's mite reminded me that God sees the cost of small obedience{% cite "ce-mark1241" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 115 %}And the Lord's challenge in Malachi 3:10 sustained my resolve: "Prove me now herewith, saith the Lord of hosts, if I will not open you the windows of heaven, and pour you out a blessing, that there shall not be room enough to receive it."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 116 %}I was not perfect in this, but each time I chose obedience He kept His promise.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 31 %}{% sentence 117 %}There are some who stand at the edge of this story and feel something other than inspiration.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 118 %}They hear of obedience and they hear constraint.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 119 %}They think of the covenant life of discipleship and they see doors closing—freedoms surrendered, individuality submerged, the self handed over to an institution or a set of rules that will define and diminish them.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 120 %}Rebellion is marketed as courage.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 121 %}Submission is marketed as weakness.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 122 %}And somewhere in the noise, the actual invitation of Yehoshua gets buried under the fears about whether it is safe to accept it.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 32 %}{% sentence 123 %}It is worth pausing here and saying plainly: that is not what obedience to the Lord is.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 124 %}It is not the surrender of the true self—it is the rescue of the self from everything that was diminishing it.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 125 %}The disciples who followed Him did not become less; they became more than they had imagined they could be.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 126 %}The fishermen became apostles.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 127 %}The tax collector became an evangelist.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 128 %}The greatest persecutor of disciples became the greatest missionary the early Church produced.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 129 %}Obedience to the invitation of Yehoshua does not erase the person.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 130 %}It reveals the person—the truest, deepest, most fully realized version of who they were always meant to become.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 33 %}{% sentence 131 %}The Lord does not stand at the gate of discipleship as a foreman with a longer list of tasks.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 132 %}He stands there as the one who has already borne the heaviest load in the history of creation—and who is offering, with open hands, to take yours.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 133 %}He is not asking you to carry what He has not already carried.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 134 %}He is asking you to stop carrying it alone.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 34 %}{% sentence 135 %}There is a freedom on the other side of that surrender that the world cannot manufacture and cannot explain.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 136 %}The freedom the Savior offers is the freedom of the person who knows what they are, and why they are here, and to whom they belong—a knowledge that settles in one's heart like ballast in a storm.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 35 %}{% sentence 137 %}And then God says within your heart, "Let there be light!"{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 36 %}{% sentence 138 %}The covenant life is lit from within in a way that the life of self-directed striving simply is not.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 139 %}There is a quality of joy available to the obedient disciple—not the surface happiness of favorable circumstances, but something deeper and steadier, a luminosity that persists even when circumstances are hard.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 37 %}{% sentence 140 %}The yoke of Yehoshua is miraculously light upon the shoulders—fitting far better than any device or philosophy the world can fashion without God.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 38 %}{% sentence 141 %}"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 142 %}Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me; for I am meek and lowly in heart: and ye shall find rest unto your souls.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 143 %}For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light"{% cite "ce-matt1128" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 39 %}{% sentence 144 %}Easy.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 145 %}Light.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 146 %}These are not the words of a taskmaster.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 147 %}They are the words of the One who designed the yoke so that it will not chafe, who knows exactly what it was made for, and who has been wearing it since before the world was formed.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 148 %}The burden is light because He is pulling at the same beam, and He does not tire.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 40 %}{% sentence 149 %}You do not lose yourself in Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 150 %}You find yourself—lit up, set free, and finally moving in the direction you were built to travel.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-compassion">{% paraspan 41 %}{% sentence 151 %}Mastery of Compassion: Grace Encountering{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 42 %}{% sentence 152 %}In Mark 1:40–42{% cite "ce-mark140" %}, Yehoshua's grace encountered a broken man.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 153 %}A leper—a man whose skin was rotting from a disfiguring disease—a man who searched for the Rabbi who could perform miracles.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 154 %}When he found the Master, he fell to his knees to confess, "If you are willing, you can make me clean".{% endsentence %} {% sentence 155 %}Yehoshua didn't hesitate in disgust.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 156 %}Instead, He was so moved with compassion for the man that He reached out His hand to touch him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 157 %}The contact made Yehoshua ritually unclean under the Law{% cite "ce-lev1345" %}{% cite "ce-num52" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 158 %}And then, He healed him.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 43 %}{% sentence 159 %}This is what grace looks like when it encounters the broken—not pity from a safe distance, but a compassionate Savior choosing contact.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 44 %}{% sentence 160 %}In the eleventh chapter of John, Yehoshua learned of the dire illness of His close personal friend, Lazarus.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 161 %}He didn't depart immediately to heal the friend, though He could have.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 162 %}He waited on purpose until after Lazarus had died.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 45 %}{% sentence 163 %}Learning that the Master approached their home in Bethany, Martha and Mary both ran to Him, in turns, to fall down at His feet to sob, "Lord, if You had been here, my brother would not have died."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 164 %}Surrounded by the mourning sisters and a crowd of grieving Jews, He "groaned in the spirit and was troubled"{% cite "ce-john1133" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 165 %}This is not the reaction of a distant and unrelatable deity.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 46 %}{% sentence 166 %}He then asked, "Where have you laid him?"—and that question is extraordinary, because He already knew what He was about to do.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 167 %}He was about to raise Lazarus.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 47 %}{% sentence 168 %}What happened next may be the most revealing moment in all of scripture.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 48 %}*{% sentence 169 %}Jesus wept.{% endsentence %}*{% endpara %}

{% para 49 %}{% sentence 170 %}This tender moment of compassion wasn't because Yehoshua felt sad about the death of His friend.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 171 %}Rather, it demonstrates His fully-developed compassion in the moment He chose to walk the full distance of human grief with a family in mourning, sharing in their pain.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 172 %}He didn't have to.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 173 %}He could have spoken the command at a distance as with the Roman centurion's dying servant.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 174 %}But He knew the value of entering the grief of the grieving—touch and presence—rather than healing from a distance.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 50 %}{% sentence 175 %}Throughout His mortal ministry, the Lord was filled with compassion for souls who "fainted, and were scattered abroad, as sheep having no shepherd"{% cite "ce-matt936" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 51 %}{% sentence 176 %}This trait did not lessen or end with His death.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 177 %}In the Eastern Hemisphere, the resurrected Master comforted the weeping Mary at His empty tomb{% cite "ce-john2011" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 178 %}He reassured the fearful disciples{% cite "ce-john2019" %}{% cite "ce-luke2436" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 179 %}He removed Peter's shame by allowing him to declare his love three times, once for each time he had previously denied Him{% cite "ce-john2115" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 180 %}He walked with the grieving disciples on the road to Emmaus{% cite "ce-luke2413b" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 181 %}He personally made a warm fire at a campsite and hand cooked a meal for His disciples who were returning from a frustrating day of fishing{% cite "ce-john211" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 52 %}{% sentence 182 %}The resurrected Yehoshua also gloriously appeared to a multitude of disciples in the Western Hemisphere who had been waiting for Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 183 %}After delivering His gospel message, He cast His eyes round about again on the multitude, and beheld they were in tears, and did look steadfastly upon Him as if they would ask Him to tarry a little longer with them.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 184 %}"Behold, my bowels are filled with compassion towards you"{% cite "ce-3ne175" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 185 %}He chose out of grace to stay for a while longer to visit and to heal the sick and afflicted.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 186 %}He blessed their children and prayed for them.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 53 %}{% sentence 187 %}The word "compassion" in the Bible is translated from the Greek *splagchnizomai* (σπλαγχνίζομαι).{% endsentence %} {% sentence 188 %}It signifies a profound "gut-feeling" of love and mercy that motivates actions to save and atone.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 189 %}It describes the visceral upheaval of seeing suffering—being shaken to the core by it, and then moving to act.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 54 %}{% sentence 190 %}I have known what it is to be broken and in need of compassion—the instability and isolation of homelessness, the devastation of a broken marriage and being a single parent to three teenage daughters, the helpless feeling of being on the verge of losing everything, and the dark night of the soul when God seemed distant.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 191 %}But I have also known the healing relief of Christlike compassion of others who reached out to me in those dark days.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 192 %}I can name one pair of hands that reached for me.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 193 %}They belong to my dear wife, Michelle, who was brave enough to love me, generous enough to be a mother to my three daughters.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 194 %}I also gained a precious new daughter in the blending, and I was healed in the process.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 55 %}{% sentence 195 %}When someone is in crisis beyond their own capacity to survive, sympathy is insufficient.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 196 %}The leper didn't need someone to quote Levitical laws.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 197 %}He needed someone to touch him with grace.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 198 %}There is a wide difference between knowing about the suffering of others and entering into it to support them.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 199 %}Grace at its most Christlike is compassion combined with the wisdom to know when and how to get involved.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-teaching">{% paraspan 56 %}{% sentence 200 %}Mastery of Teaching: Grace Communicated{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 57 %}{% sentence 201 %}A solitary Samaritan woman came to draw water at midday—an hour when no one else would be at the well—perhaps avoiding her peers.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 202 %}Yehoshua saw her and waited there.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 203 %}He greeted her with neither doctrine nor authority.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 204 %}He asked her for a drink{% cite "ce-john47" %}, which would have seemed inappropriate within that cultural setting.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 205 %}Beginning with that simple request, He led her step by step, at exactly the pace she could follow.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 206 %}Water turned to worship.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 207 %}He resolved her past and shaped her future.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 208 %}He led her from natural curiosity to a confession of faith.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 209 %}She recognized the promised Messiah.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 210 %}And what did she do?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 211 %}She left her water pot and ran to tell her village, "Come, see a man, which told me all things that ever I did: is not this the Christ?"{% cite "ce-john429" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 212 %}Grace received became grace shared in a matter of minutes.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 213 %}And it transformed her, from an isolated soul to student, and then from student to teacher.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 58 %}{% sentence 214 %}This is His pattern.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 215 %}How Yehoshua taught reveals who He was.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 216 %}He didn't simply pour out truth—He calibrated it to the hearer.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 217 %}Parables for crowds who needed to discover truth at their own pace{% cite "ce-matt1310" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 218 %}Direct doctrine for disciples ready to receive it{% cite "ce-john1625" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 219 %}Piercing questions for Pharisees hiding behind their authority{% cite "ce-matt2242" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 220 %}Silence as a teaching tool to instruct a Roman governor who had no interest in the answer{% cite "ce-matt2714" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 221 %}Every choice of method was an act of respectful grace for the person standing before Him.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 59 %}{% sentence 222 %}Why did He teach at all?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 223 %}Because "out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh"{% cite "ce-matt1234" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 224 %}A heart full of grace overflows.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 225 %}Restoration scripture confirms this in an account of Lehi, an elderly prophet.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 226 %}In a vision, he tasted the fruit of the tree of life and his first impulse was not to analyze it but to turn to his family.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 227 %}"I began to be desirous that my household should partake of it also"{% cite "ce-1ne812" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 228 %}The desire to share what is precious is the natural fruit of having received it.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 229 %}This is grace communicated.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 60 %}{% sentence 230 %}I remember a moment when a teenage special education student said something so disrespectful in my science laboratory that every instinct told me to respond by "matching his energy."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 231 %}Instead I bit my tongue, threw a desperate prayer heavenward, and waited.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 232 %}The Spirit softened my heart and opened my eyes—not just to find compassion for the student, but to see how to reach him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 233 %}The unmet need motivating his behavior became the focus for the teaching moment.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 234 %}I learned that day what the Master practiced perfectly: truth that reaches a soul must first be calibrated to the soul it is reaching.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 235 %}This is not just a teaching technique; it flows from habits of grace that we learn from Yehoshua the man.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-meekness-and-courage">{% paraspan 61 %}{% sentence 236 %}Mastery of Meekness and Courage: Grace and Truth in Tension{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 62 %}{% sentence 237 %}The same hands that overturned tables in the temple at Jerusalem also washed feet in the prepared upper room.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 238 %}Why?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 239 %}It bears repeating: Yehoshua was "full of grace and truth"{% cite "ce-john114b" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 63 %}{% sentence 240 %}On the evening before His crucifixion, Yehoshua rose from His place at the last supper table to perform a task that had been neglected by others.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 241 %}He laid aside His outer garment, wrapped a towel around His waist, and poured water into a basin.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 242 %}The Master then knelt before His servants{% cite "ce-john134" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 64 %}{% sentence 243 %}Washing of guests' feet was a degrading task assigned to the lowest household slave—work that Jewish law forbade compelling of a Hebrew servant.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 244 %}It was also an act of affectionate personal service that a wife would perform for her husband.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 245 %}The paradox is not that degradation equals affection, but that love can stoop to perform what status would despise.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 65 %}{% sentence 246 %}Peter's horrified reaction reveals the depth of meaning in watching his Master do this task{% cite "ce-john136" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 66 %}{% sentence 247 %}Yehoshua washed Peter's feet—who would deny Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 248 %}He washed the feet of Judas, who already had made plans to betray Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 249 %}The Master was not ignorant of these things, yet He knelt to serve, taking the posture and position of one that no one notices.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 67 %}{% sentence 250 %}Earlier that same Passover week, Yehoshua entered the temple and saw that the Court of the Gentiles—the only part of the temple where people of all faiths and nationalities could worship the God of Israel—had been converted into a bustling marketplace filled with vendors, moneychangers, animals and the chaos, filth, and greed that go with these things.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 251 %}He found a place where He could sit down and make a whip of cords{% cite "ce-john215" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 252 %}His reaction was not sudden.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 253 %}It was measured and premeditated.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 68 %}{% sentence 254 %}Those gentle hands—those that washed feet, broke bread with the hungry, healed lepers, shaped the world, and gestured to all to "come and see"—overturned the vendor tables, scattered their wares, cracked His whip in deliberate fury and His voice thundered quoted scriptures{% cite "ce-matt2113" %}{% cite "ce-isa567" %}{% cite "ce-jer711" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 69 %}{% sentence 255 %}His anger was not aimed solely at the merchants, but its scope included the corrupt priests that profited from the corruption of the "house of prayer."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 256 %}He courageously took a stand to confront an abusive system, not just functionaries.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 257 %}His anger focused on anything that came between souls and the Kingdom of Heaven.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 258 %}And no one could stop Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 259 %}The chief priests feared Him and could not act{% cite "ce-mark1118" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 70 %}{% sentence 260 %}Meekness is not weakness, and courage is not rage.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 261 %}Both are manifestations of the same character trait, one that flows in different directions from the same source—the grace and truth at the core of His personality.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 262 %}The meek serve when dignity says "don't bother."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 263 %}The courageous serve when safety says "don't dare."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 264 %}Both require the same inner conquest—the surrender of ego before the work of love begins.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 71 %}{% sentence 265 %}Yehoshua did not kneel because He was timid.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 266 %}He did not overturn tables because He lost control.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 267 %}He mastered His natural impulses and then acted from what remained: grace directed by truth.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 268 %}When truth called for gentleness, He was gentle without being weak.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 269 %}When truth called for confrontation, He confronted without being cruel.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 270 %}This is what mastery looks like—not the absence of strong emotion, but the governance of it by wisdom and love.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 72 %}{% sentence 271 %}I recall several moments of conflict in which I said something I thought sounded righteous but wound up being self-righteous and hurtful.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 272 %}I repent humbly before God.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 273 %}I ask those I harmed for forgiveness.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 73 %}{% sentence 274 %}I pray that I am developing a measure of Christlike character as His grace and truth work upon my spirit.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 275 %}I imitate Him imperfectly, but it is my covenant duty to try.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 276 %}There is comfort for me in holding up the character of Christ as a mirror to examine my soul.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 277 %}I remind myself often not to focus on how far I fall short of Him, but to hold to my faith that His grace sees me as I hope to be, and that His spirit works tirelessly upon me daily to close the gap.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-justice-and-mercy">{% paraspan 74 %}{% sentence 278 %}Mastery of Justice and Mercy: Grace and Truth Adjudicating{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 75 %}{% sentence 279 %}The Pharisees and their entourage of Scribes dragged her into the temple courts on a morning when Yehoshua sat teaching a gathering of the people.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 280 %}They interrupted His teaching and thrust her in the middle of His gathering.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 76 %}{% sentence 281 %}They weren't interested in redeeming her soul at all.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 282 %}They were trying to build a case against Yehoshua.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 283 %}Their challenge: "Moses in the law commanded us, that such should be stoned: but what sayest thou?"{% cite "ce-john85" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 284 %}She must have been shaking with fear and shame before the crowd.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 77 %}{% sentence 285 %}The Master stooped to write on the ground with a finger.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 286 %}We don't know what He wrote.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 287 %}We do know that Yehoshua refused to answer on their terms.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 78 %}{% sentence 288 %}When they pressed the Master, He rose and delivered one sentence that shattered their trap without breaking the law: "He that is without sin among you, let him first cast a stone at her"{% cite "ce-john87" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 289 %}They departed one by one, eldest first—stung by their own conscience.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 290 %}When only the woman remained, He delivered a powerful sermon in divine brevity: "Neither do I condemn thee: go, and sin no more"{% cite "ce-john811" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 79 %}{% sentence 291 %}Mercy without justice is indulgence that leaves a sinner unchanged.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 292 %}Justice without mercy is cruelty that crushes the soul of the sinner.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 293 %}In Yehoshua, we find that the grace and truth in Him courageously names what must change while meekly protecting the soul that must make the change.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 80 %}{% sentence 294 %}"I am the light of the world: he that followeth me shall not walk in darkness, but shall have the light of life"{% cite "ce-john812" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 295 %}The context for this verse is Yehoshua's demonstration of the dynamic between justice and mercy—and more importantly, how this affected the life of the woman whom He did not condemn.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 81 %}{% sentence 296 %}Again I hear the voice of my saintly mother: "Aaron, you never know what sorrow lies behind a smile; you never know how someone who has mistreated you was personally greeted and then treated in this life.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 297 %}Be like Jesus."{% endsentence %} {% sentence 298 %}Her voice echoes in my heart like that of Yehoshua, teaching me to neither announce my own virtues nor to assume another's vices.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 82 %}{% sentence 299 %}In all our spiritual journeys, when faced with the opportunities to judge one another, let us walk in the grace of His light.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 300 %}Anything else is walking in the dark.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="mastery-of-joy">{% paraspan 83 %}{% sentence 301 %}Mastery of Joy: Grace Rejoicing{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 84 %}{% sentence 302 %}Contrasting His own lifestyle to that of His cousin who taught in the wilderness "neither eating nor drinking," Yehoshua described Himself as "eating and drinking" with His followers{% cite "ce-matt1119" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 85 %}{% sentence 303 %}His enemies claimed He took this to excess as "a glutton and a drunkard, a friend of tax collectors and sinners".{% endsentence %} {% sentence 304 %}They meant this as an insult.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 305 %}Read it again.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 306 %}Think: what kind of person draws that accusation?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 307 %}Not a broody stoic.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 308 %}Not the pale, sorrowful figure captured in medieval stained glass.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 309 %}His enemies saw a man who showed up at feasts, who turned water into wine at a wedding—His first recorded miracle was performed to keep a celebration going at the request of His own mother{% cite "ce-john21" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 310 %}Yehoshua was the kind of man that people wanted as a friend and companion.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 311 %}His presence made rooms feel different, better.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 86 %}{% sentence 312 %}The man Yehoshua had joy.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 87 %}{% sentence 313 %}This wasn't a shallow happiness—it was the deep kind of gladness that survives grief and sustains inner purpose.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 314 %}Luke named His emotion: "In that hour Jesus rejoiced in spirit"{% cite "ce-luke1021" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 315 %}The occasion of such joy had nothing to do with miracles or outsmarting enemies.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 316 %}The seventy had just returned from their missions, and the Father had revealed truth to the hearts of the faithful.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 317 %}Yehoshua's joy was in the Father's work; in the lost sheep's safety; in the return of the prodigal.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 318 %}In one tender account, the resurrected Lord knelt among the children, blessed them one by one, and wept—not from sorrow but from joy so full it overflowed{% cite "ce-3ne1721b" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 88 %}{% sentence 319 %}This is the nature of grace rejoicing.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 320 %}It is not the absence of sorrow nor a paradise of pleasure.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 321 %}Joy that comes from Christlike grace cannot be extinguished by opposition.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 322 %}Joy and sorrow live side by side in every honest life.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 323 %}The writer of Hebrews understood: Jesus, "who for the joy that was set before him endured the cross"{% cite "ce-heb122" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 324 %}Joy was His motive, not the reward.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 89 %}{% sentence 325 %}"These things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full"{% cite "ce-john1511" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 326 %}This is His invitation for you to receive the same grace that fueled His own gladness—and to let it overflow into the lives of others.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 90 %}{% sentence 327 %}I remember a full day of shoveling manure with fellow saints for neighborhood gardens.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 328 %}We didn't dare hug or even shake hands, smiling brightly despite being thoroughly covered in muck.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 329 %}How odd it is that we can be covered in filth while filled with joy in the service of the Master and while caring for one another.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 330 %}This truly is the abundant life He promised{% cite "ce-john1010" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="beatitudes-his-self-portrait">{% paraspan 91 %}{% sentence 331 %}Beatitudes as His Own Self-Portrait: Grace and Truth Revealed{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 92 %}{% sentence 332 %}This chapter could not possibly detail all the marvelous personality traits that flow from Yehoshua's core of grace and truth.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 333 %}The Apostle John also felt the need to explain: "There are also many other things which Jesus did, the which, if they should be written every one, I suppose that even the world itself could not contain the books that should be written"{% cite "ce-john2125" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 93 %}{% sentence 334 %}My purpose here is to introduce Yehoshua—the man behind the ministry—as a relatable person that we would be delighted to have as a friend.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 335 %}Better than being introduced by servants, the Master of grace and truth shared His own character portrait in the form of the Sermon on the Mount.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 336 %}The Beatitudes are instructions—and they are also something more.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 337 %}The first words of His first recorded sermon were a character sketch of Himself.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 94 %}{% sentence 338 %}We gain marvelous confirmation of the nature of His own character by assuming that the Teacher practiced what He taught.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 339 %}He not only described what His disciples should become, He described what He already was—and what His grace makes possible in every willing heart.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 95 %}{% sentence 340 %}That same grace that is the living core of His character is a power at work in every willing disciple.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 341 %}Studying His character is not meant to measure the distance between Him and us—it is meant to show us what we can become as His grace works its refining purpose in our mortal lives.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 342 %}Beholding the truth of His grace as the mechanism of our own transformation is the whole point of this chapter{% cite "ce-2cor318" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

<h2 id="benediction">{% paraspan 96 %}{% sentence 343 %}Benediction{% endsentence %}{% endparaspan %}</h2>

{% para 97 %}{% sentence 344 %}You've sat with Yehoshua.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 345 %}You have studied the Man who is God.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 346 %}You've observed that His great heart—full of grace—beats with His life-giving truth throughout all parts of His character.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 347 %}We hear the phrase "He died for us" often, but let us not forget that He also lives for us.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 348 %}"He is the Lord of both the dead and the living"{% cite "ce-rom149" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 349 %}His heart still beats for you.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 98 %}{% sentence 350 %}Now is an important moment for your own heart.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 351 %}Will it begin to beat for Him as well?{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 99 %}{% sentence 352 %}John recorded a profound prayer of Jesus—a prayer that is called The Great Intercessor's Prayer.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 100 %}{% sentence 353 %}"My prayer is not for them alone. I pray also for those who will believe in me through their message"{% cite "ce-john1720" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 101 %}{% sentence 354 %}You are inside His prayer.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 355 %}Jesus prayed for the person reading this chapter, by name in the eternal sense.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 356 %}This is not a simple metaphor.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 357 %}He saw you.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 102 %}{% sentence 358 %}His heart still beats for you.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 103 %}{% sentence 359 %}"I have given them the glory that you gave me, that they may be one as we are one…that they may be brought to complete unity"{% cite "ce-john1723" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 360 %}That same graceful character you just studied—the humility, the compassion, the mastery of every human impulse—is not to be held at a distance for admiration.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 361 %}It is a gift that has been freely given.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 362 %}Will it be received?{% endsentence %} {% sentence 363 %}The glory of Christ's character is the gift of grace to the willing disciple.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 364 %}But it must be received through the beholding.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 104 %}{% sentence 365 %}His arms are open wide.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 105 %}{% sentence 366 %}Christ prayed that we would behold His glory{% cite "ce-john1724" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 367 %}And we are taught that beholding transforms the beholder into the same image, "from glory to glory"{% cite "ce-2cor318b" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 368 %}It has already begun in you.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 106 %}{% sentence 369 %}His hands will bless you.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 107 %}{% sentence 370 %}Christ prayed that His disciples may become "one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us…that they may be made perfect in one"{% cite "ce-john1721" %}{% cite "ce-john1723" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 371 %}The heart of Christ is not just for the individual—it is the bonding agent that makes us one with Him, one with the Father, and one with the heart of every other disciple.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 108 %}{% sentence 372 %}His voice calls you home.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 109 %}{% sentence 373 %}Christ closed His prayer with His most sincere longing for us: "that the love you have for me may be in them and that I myself may be in them"{% cite "ce-john1726" %}.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 374 %}The Great Intercessor's Prayer doesn't end with a desire for nearness—His great heart desires a true "oneness" with you in spirit and in truth.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 110 %}{% sentence 375 %}This "oneness" does not just happen.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 376 %}It is a journey—an epic story arc.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 377 %}As we learn of Him and follow, His grace transforms us, refines our own character over time.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 111 %}{% sentence 378 %}God is calling: "Come to Zion."{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 112 %}{% sentence 379 %}His grace is sufficient for you.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 113 %}{% sentence 380 %}The next chapter will show you what it cost Him.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 381 %}We will behold His mastery tested to its absolute limit as we follow Him to Gethsemane and to Golgotha.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}

{% para 114 %}{% sentence 382 %}We testify that the character of the person we have studied here is the Son of God in the Highest.{% endsentence %} {% sentence 383 %}"Salvation is found in no one else, for there is no other name under heaven given to mankind by which we must be saved"{% cite "ce-acts412" %}.{% endsentence %}{% endpara %}
