## #ch01-introduction

**Welcome to #ch01-introduction**

This is where the journey begins. Chapter 1 is a personal introduction to the writers — two brothers, Aaron and Jim Powner — and to the ministry they have spent more than twenty years building. It is not a theological argument. It is a handshake.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The human condition and the hunger for meaning that drives all honest seeking
• The origin of Words of Plainness — letters to unborn children that became a ministry
• The authors' credentials, conversion, and life as Latter-day Saints
• A Christ-centered witness that welcomes all believers
• The interfaith spirit of the project: adding light, never taking it away
• The organization and purpose of the full work

**Pastoral intent:** To establish trust. Aaron and Jim want you to know who they are, what they believe, and why — before they ask anything of you. The chapter is an open door, not a sales pitch.

**Discussion prompts:**
• What brought you to Words of Plainness? Was it a recommendation, a search, or something you stumbled into?
• The chapter says "what you get out of reading our Words of Plainness depends entirely upon what you are looking for." What are you looking for?
• Aaron and Jim describe themselves as "hunters and gatherers of knowledge." What image or metaphor would you use for your own search?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch02-our-search

**Welcome to #ch02-our-search**

Chapter 2 is unlike any other chapter in the book. It presents two voices — Aaron in his thirties and Aaron in his fifties — wrestling with the same questions across two decades. The subject is the universal human hunger for truth, meaning, and lasting happiness.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The hunger within — that restless sense that something is missing even when life is going well
• Life's fundamental questions: Who am I? Why am I? What can I become?
• How we cope — pride, despair, rebellion, submission, and self-justification — treated with compassion, not judgment
• The path forward: humility between extremes, balance as a daily practice
• Holy dissatisfaction as a divine gift, not a flaw
• God as the Refiner who sits beside the fire, patient and present
• The promise: creation is not finished — and neither are you

**Pastoral intent:** To name the hunger honestly and to reframe it as sacred. Aaron wants the reader to stop treating restlessness as a problem to solve and start seeing it as the engine of eternal progression.

**Discussion prompts:**
• Have you felt the hunger this chapter describes — a longing for something more, even when things are going well? What did you do with it?
• Aaron writes that his earlier certainties were "tempered" over two decades. Has time softened or deepened any of your own convictions?
• The chapter says "creation is not finished — neither are you." Does that idea bring comfort or restlessness? Why?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch03-academic-knowledge

**Welcome to #ch03-academic-knowledge**

Chapter 3 is a careful exploration of what human learning can and cannot do. It is written by two career educators who love scholarship — and who have also discovered its ceiling.

**What this chapter covers:**
• Faith and learning as companions in the same cause — not enemies
• The Latter-day Saint definition of truth: knowledge of things as they are, were, and are to come
• The four natural paths to knowledge: senses, authority, reason, and intuition
• Three tests for truth: correspondence, coherence, and pragmatic utility
• The contributions of philosophy, arts, and sciences — celebrated, not dismissed
• The limitations of academic knowledge — epistemic humility
• The ceiling of the natural framework: questions reason alone cannot answer

**Pastoral intent:** To honor the reader's intellect while gently bringing them to the edge of what intellect alone can reach. This chapter does not ask anyone to stop thinking — it asks them to consider whether there is a way of knowing that completes what thinking has started.

**Discussion prompts:**
• Where has academic knowledge served your faith well? Where has it created friction?
• The chapter describes a "ceiling" — questions the natural faculties cannot answer. Which of those questions matters most to you right now?
• Aaron and Jim call themselves "painfully aware" of how much they still don't know. What is one thing you have become more humble about over time?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch04-spiritual-knowledge

**Welcome to #ch04-spiritual-knowledge**

Chapter 4 picks up exactly where Chapter 3 left off — at the ceiling of human learning — and introduces the fifth faculty: revelation. This is the chapter where the ministry makes its boldest claim: God speaks, and you can learn to hear Him.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The fifth faculty — revelation through the Spirit of God communing with the human spirit
• What spirituality is not — self-help, stress management, or vague mindfulness
• The human spirit within — Latter-day Saint understanding of spirit, soul, and identity
• Three gifts of the Spirit: the Light of Christ, the Holy Ghost, and the Gift of the Holy Ghost
• Righteous and wicked spirituality — and how to tell the difference
• Experimenting on the word — Alma's sacred experiment with the seed of faith
• How revelation comes — gradually, simply, and personally

**Pastoral intent:** To move the reader from intellectual openness to personal experimentation. This chapter doesn't just explain how God communicates — it invites the reader to test it for themselves.

**Discussion prompts:**
• The chapter describes a difference between a strong emotion and a genuine spiritual witness. Have you experienced something you would call spiritual knowledge — and how did you distinguish it from ordinary feeling?
• Alma's experiment asks you to "give place for a portion of my words" and observe the results. Have you ever tested a spiritual claim by living it out? What happened?
• What does "the simpleness of the way" mean to you? Have you ever overlooked something important because it arrived too quietly?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch05-sincere-prayer

**Welcome to #ch05-sincere-prayer**

Chapter 5 is about the practice of prayer — not as ritual or performance, but as the soul's sincere desire spoken to a Father who listens and answers.

**What this chapter covers:**
• Prayer as the soul's sincere desire — no memorized language required
• The Lord's Prayer as a pattern, not a script
• Forms and postures of prayer — personal, family, congregational, constant
• Preparing for meaningful prayer — going inward, then reaching upward
• Prayer in urgent moments and the practice of constant prayer
• Praying for others — intercessory prayer as a sign of maturing discipleship
• When prayers seem unanswered — divine silence is not divine absence
• Balancing personal agency with dependence on God
• Prayer as two-way communication — learning to listen after speaking
• God's unchanging nature: the God who spoke to prophets still speaks today

**Pastoral intent:** To move prayer from obligation to relationship. Aaron wants the reader to stop performing prayer and start practicing it — as honest conversation with a God who already knows what they need but waits to be asked.

**Discussion prompts:**
• What is your honest relationship with prayer right now — alive, routine, abandoned, or untried?
• The chapter says "divine silence is not divine absence." Has there been a season when you prayed and heard nothing? What did you do with that silence?
• Aaron describes prayer as two-way communication. Have you ever felt that God spoke back — not audibly, but unmistakably? What did that feel like?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch06-embrace-the-savior

**Welcome to #ch06-embrace-the-savior**

Chapter 6 is the crossroads of Volume 1. Everything before it builds a foundation — this chapter asks you what you will do with it. It is an interactive experience that meets you where you are and helps you take your next step toward Christ.

**What this chapter covers:**
• A personal invitation to respond to what the first five chapters have laid down
• Three reader pathways — Covenant, Seeker, and Open-Hearted — each meeting a different disposition
• The relationship between faith, repentance, and the grace of Jesus Christ
• What it means to embrace the Savior — not as a theological position but as a personal encounter
• A self-assessment: where do you stand, and what is your next step?

**Pastoral intent:** To turn information into invitation. This chapter does not push you toward a conclusion — it holds up a mirror and asks what you see. The three pathways ensure that no reader is made to feel they arrived at the wrong door.

**Discussion prompts:**
• Which of the three pathways — Covenant, Seeker, or Open-Hearted — resonated most with where you are right now? Why?
• The chapter asks what it means to "embrace" the Savior rather than just believe in Him. What does that distinction mean to you?
• Is there something in the first six chapters that you found yourself resisting? What was it, and why do you think it caught your attention?

## #ch07-prophecies-birth-youth

**Welcome to #ch07-prophecies-birth-youth**

Chapter 7 opens the "Learn of Me" arc — five chapters dedicated to knowing Jesus as a person, not just a doctrine. This chapter traces the centuries of prophecy that pointed to His coming, His miraculous birth, and the hidden years of His youth.

**What this chapter covers:**
• Old Testament prophecies of the Messiah — centuries of preparation for one arrival
• The annunciation, the birth in Bethlehem, and the witnesses who recognized Him
• The flight to Egypt and the childhood years in Nazareth
• What the silence of the Gospels about His youth tells us — and what it leaves to wonder
• Biblical sources leading, Restoration scriptures deepening the witness

**Pastoral intent:** To slow the reader down. Before studying what Jesus taught or what He suffered, Aaron wants you to feel the weight of how long the world waited for Him — and how quietly He arrived.

**Discussion prompts:**
• The prophets described the Messiah centuries before His birth. What does it mean to you that God works on that kind of timeline?
• Jesus grew up in obscurity — a carpenter's son in Nazareth. What does His hidden life say about how God values the unseen years of our own lives?
• Is there a detail from the birth or youth narratives that has always struck you? What draws you to it?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch08-baptism-temptations-ministry

**Welcome to #ch08-baptism-temptations-ministry**

Chapter 8 traces Jesus from the Jordan River through the wilderness and into the public ministry that changed everything. This is where we begin to see who He is under pressure.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The baptism of Jesus by John — and what the Father's voice reveals
• The forty days of temptation in the wilderness — Satan's offers and Christ's refusals
• The calling of the disciples and the beginning of public ministry
• Key moments and miracles that reveal His identity and mission
• The growing tension between Jesus and the religious authorities

**Pastoral intent:** To show the reader a man who was tested before He taught — and who chose obedience when every alternative was easier. The temptations are not decoration; they reveal the interior structure of His character.

**Discussion prompts:**
• Jesus was tempted with power, spectacle, and provision — three things most people want. Which of those three temptations feels most familiar to you?
• The chapter shows Jesus calling ordinary people as disciples. What does it say about Him that He chose fishermen and tax collectors?
• Is there a moment from His early ministry — a miracle, a conversation, a confrontation — that has always stood out to you?

## #ch09-yehoshua-the-man

**Welcome to #ch09-yehoshua-the-man**

Chapter 9 is the centerpiece of the "Learn of Me" arc — a portrait of the personal character of Jesus Christ. Not His theology, not His miracles, but *Him*: what He was like, what it felt like to be in the room with Him, what moved Him and what He refused.

**What this chapter covers:**
• An invocation — setting aside inherited images to see the actual person
• Master of compassion — *splagchnizomai*, the gut-level response to suffering that always became action
• Master of meekness and courage — power under restraint, paired with a courage that never flinched
• His relationship with silence and speech, with crowds and individuals
• No stranger to your sorrows — He got beneath the weight, not above it
• The Restoration's deepening witness: He knows your grief from the inside

**Pastoral intent:** This is the chapter where the ministry lives or dies. If the reader does not encounter a real person here — not a stained-glass abstraction but a man who wept, who got tired, who stopped for one blind man when the crowd pressed — then the rest of the book is just information. Aaron wrote this chapter to introduce you to someone he cannot stop thinking about.

**Discussion prompts:**
• The chapter describes compassion as Christ's *first response*, not an occasional virtue. When have you witnessed compassion that looked like that — immediate, physical, without hesitation?
• Meekness is defined here as "power under restraint." Who in your life has shown you what that looks like?
• The closing section says Christ "is not a standard you are failing to meet — He is a person who has met you." How does that reframing land for you?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch10-suffering-trial-crucifixion-resurrection

**Welcome to #ch10-suffering-trial-crucifixion-resurrection**

Chapter 10 walks through the final hours — from Gethsemane through the cross to the empty tomb. Everything the previous chapters revealed about His character is now tested under conditions no other person has ever faced.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The agony in Gethsemane — what the Atonement cost Him physically and spiritually
• The arrest, the trials, and the silence before Pilate
• The crucifixion — the physical and spiritual dimensions of His death
• The resurrection — not metaphor, not symbol, but the bodily reality
• Biblical and Restoration witnesses of what happened and why it matters

**Pastoral intent:** To let the reader stand at the foot of the cross without rushing past it. Aaron does not want you to skip to Easter morning. He wants you to understand what was endured — and for whom — before the stone rolled away.

**Discussion prompts:**
• Gethsemane is described as the place where Christ took upon Himself the full weight of human suffering. What does it mean to you that He chose this voluntarily?
• His silence before Pilate is one of the most striking moments in the Gospels. What do you hear in that silence?
• The resurrection is presented here as literal and physical. How central is the bodily resurrection to your own faith? What would change if it were only metaphor?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch11-the-living-christ

**Welcome to #ch11-the-living-christ**

Chapter 11 is a brief, transitional chapter that lifts the reader's eyes from the empty tomb to the present. Christ is not only risen — He is living, active, and at work in the world today.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The risen Christ's ongoing ministry — He did not retire after the Ascension
• His work among the righteous dead, His appearances across scripture
• The testimony that He lives now and continues to lead His people
• A forward-looking invitation to continue into the Discipleship Practices or into Volume 2

**Pastoral intent:** To complete the "Learn of Me" arc with a sense of momentum, not closure. Aaron does not want the reader to leave Jesus in the past. The point of knowing Him is that He is still knowable — still present — still calling.

**Discussion prompts:**
• What does it mean to you that Christ is described as *living* rather than simply *risen*? Is there a difference?
• Have you experienced a moment — in prayer, in scripture, in life — where the presence of the living Christ felt real and immediate?
• The chapter points forward to Discipleship Practices and Volume 2. Where are you drawn to go next?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch12-the-beatitudes

**Welcome to #ch12-the-beatitudes**

Chapter 12 opens the Discipleship Practices section with the Beatitudes — Christ's description of the character He builds in those who follow Him. This is the first interactive card-chapter: each discipleship card has three tabs exploring how we practice, how it blesses, and what level of commitment you feel ready to make.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The Beatitudes as a portrait of Christlike character — not rules, but descriptions of what grace produces
• Poverty of spirit, mourning, meekness, hunger for righteousness, mercy, purity of heart, peacemaking, and endurance under persecution
• Each Beatitude explored through practice, blessing, and personal commitment
• The interactive card format: How We Practice · How It Blesses · Your Commitment

**Pastoral intent:** To move the reader from admiring Christ's character to developing it. The Beatitudes are the bridge between "Learn of Me" and "Follow Me." Each card asks: where are you in this, and what are you willing to try?

**Discussion prompts:**
• Which of the Beatitudes describes a quality you most want to develop? Which one feels hardest?
• "Blessed are the meek" is often misread as "blessed are the passive." After reading the chapter, how do you understand meekness differently?
• The card format asks you to choose a commitment level. How does it feel to be invited rather than commanded?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch13-the-sermon-on-the-mount

**Welcome to #ch13-the-sermon-on-the-mount**

Chapter 13 takes the full Sermon on the Mount — Christ's opening manifesto — and unpacks it as a standard for discipleship. If the Beatitudes describe who Christ's followers become, the Sermon describes how they live.

**What this chapter covers:**
• The Sermon on the Mount as Christ's cumulative expectation for His disciples
• Salt and light — identity and mission
• The higher law — anger, lust, oaths, retaliation, love of enemies
• Giving, praying, and fasting without performance
• Treasures in heaven, anxiety, and trust in God's provision
• The narrow gate, false prophets, and the house built on rock

**Pastoral intent:** To let the Sermon land as the demanding, beautiful, impossible-without-grace standard that it is. Aaron does not soften it. He presents it as Christ presented it — and trusts the reader to feel both the weight and the invitation.

**Discussion prompts:**
• The Sermon on the Mount sets a standard no one meets perfectly. Does that discourage you or draw you forward? Why?
• "Love your enemies" is one of the hardest commands in all of scripture. Have you ever tried it? What happened?
• Which teaching from the Sermon feels most relevant to something you are navigating right now?

— Brother Aaron

## #ch14-prayer-as-a-lifestyle

**Welcome to #ch14-prayer-as-a-lifestyle**

Chapter 14 returns to prayer — this time not as introduction but as daily practice. If Chapter 5 opened the door, this chapter asks you to live in the room.

**What this chapter covers:**
• Prayer as a lifestyle, not an event — woven into the fabric of daily living
• Deepening personal and family prayer practices
• Intercessory prayer — praying for others, including those who have hurt you
• Fasting and prayer as a combined discipline
• Recognizing and responding to answers across time
• Prayer as the sustaining practice beneath all other discipleship commitments

**Pastoral intent:** To move prayer from something you do to something you are. The earlier chapter made the case for prayer; this chapter builds the habit. Every other discipleship practice in the book rests on this one.

**Discussion prompts:**
• What is the difference between praying regularly and living prayerfully? Where are you between those two?
• Have you experienced a season when prayer carried you through something that effort alone could not?
• The chapter describes prayer as the foundation beneath all other practices. Do you agree? What happens to the other disciplines when prayer falls away?

— Brother Aaron
