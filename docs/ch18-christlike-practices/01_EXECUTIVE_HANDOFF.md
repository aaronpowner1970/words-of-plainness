# Executive Handoff

## 1. Project origin

The project began with a hand-curated list of **35 behavior-based virtues of Jesus of Nazareth** drawn from New Testament interactions and actions. The purpose was not to compile Christian theological beliefs or abstract traits, but to identify ways Jesus **practiced what He preached** that could become concrete discipleship behaviors.

A subsequent behavior-first corpus analysis expanded the list toward approximately **70 observable discipleship practices**. The study deliberately used a “fresh eyes” methodology: observe what Jesus does, refrains from doing, asks, receives, repairs, protects, permits, interrupts, or follows up on before importing theological interpretation.

This produced an important conceptual distinction:

> **Christlike virtues are durable orientations of character. Christlike discipleship practices are observable behaviors through which those virtues become visible.**

## 2. Product opportunity

The approximately 70 practices should not become a long static list or a 70-card chapter that the reader works through once.

Instead, the current recommendation is to make **Chapter 18 the introduction and user guide to a replayable discipleship web application**. The application becomes a place the reader can revisit throughout life, selecting a new area of growth each time or returning to a previously practiced behavior.

The intended experience is therefore not:

> browse → complete → check off

It is:

> **DISCERN → ENCOUNTER → CHOOSE → PRACTICE → REFLECT → RETURN**

## 3. Relationship to Keep My Commandments

The existing Keep My Commandments application provides a strong sibling product pattern but should not be cloned feature-for-feature.

Its conceptual question is:

> **What did Jesus command?**

The new application asks:

> **How did Jesus Himself behave?**

The two applications can eventually become linked corpora:

- **The Words of Christ:** commands and teachings.
- **The Way of Christ:** observable practices modeled in His life.

The existing application demonstrates the value of giving a large corpus multiple entry routes. The new application should reuse that design grammar while changing the formation model from catalog progression to repeated practice cycles.

## 4. Mature content architecture

The approximately 70 observable practices currently sort into **eight virtue domains**:

1. **Attentive Presence**
2. **Dignity & Belonging**
3. **Truth & Agency**
4. **Grace & Restoration**
5. **Humility & Inner Government**
6. **Peace & Right Use of Power**
7. **Servanthood & Stewardship**
8. **Forming Others**

These domains are not stages and should never imply a hierarchy of spiritual worth.

The conceptual model is:

> **CHARACTER → PRACTICE → CONTEXT**

Examples:

- Grace & Restoration → Patience with Slow Understanding → parenting.
- Peace & Right Use of Power → Repair Harm Caused by One's Own Side → conflict.
- Attentive Presence → Follow-through / Aftercare → ministry.
- Truth & Agency → Respect for Agency / Non-coercion → witness.

## 5. Primary discovery routes

The same practice corpus should be reachable through multiple lenses:

### Help Me Choose
A short, optional behavioral self-assessment suggests one or two domains that may be fruitful now. It recommends rather than diagnoses.

### Choose a Domain
The user intentionally selects one of the eight domain families.

### Life Situations
The user enters through a current lived circumstance such as anger, disagreement, parenting, leadership, being hurt, helping someone, or repeated failure.

### Browse All
The user searches or browses the full corpus when they already know what they want.

### For Today
One practice is surfaced for contemplation without implying that the user must adopt it.

All routes converge on the same practice records.

## 6. Practice-card experience

Each practice should have an evidence-first core:

### What Jesus Did
A concise observation of the Gospel behavior that justifies inclusion.

Then preserve the familiar Words of Plainness interaction grammar:

### How We Practice
Translate the observed behavior into contemporary discipleship.

### How It Blesses Lives
Describe likely relational or formative fruit without overclaiming causal certainty.

### How Will You Practice?
Offer concrete behavioral options plus a custom response.

The preferred sequence is therefore:

> **OBSERVE JESUS → UNDERSTAND THE PRACTICE → SEE ITS FRUIT → IMITATE IT**

## 7. Practice cycles, not virtue completion

The existing Keep My Commandments progression model should not be copied directly.

A Christlike practice is never marked “integrated” in a terminal sense. Instead, users create **practice cycles**.

A practice cycle may record:

- chosen practice;
- person or situation in view;
- concrete intended behavior;
- start date;
- later reflection;
- what was noticed;
- what was difficult;
- what to try differently;
- whether to repeat the same practice or explore another.

The cycle may close. The virtue remains available forever.

## 8. Replayability

The app should make return visits meaningfully different:

- choose a different domain;
- choose a different practice in the same domain;
- retake the behavioral discernment assessment;
- revisit a previous commitment;
- repeat the same practice under new circumstances;
- enter through a new life situation;
- accept a “For Today” suggestion.

No completion ring should imply “70/70 Christlike practices complete.”

## 9. My Practice Journey

Use language of **journey and practice**, not score and completion.

Potential descriptive metrics:

- current practice;
- practices visited;
- practice cycles;
- reflections recorded;
- domains explored;
- practices frequently revisited.

“Domains explored: 5 of 8” may be shown descriptively, but should not become a progress bar or completion target.

## 10. Chapter 18's role

Chapter 18 remains a real Volume 1 chapter but becomes primarily:

1. an introduction to what is meant by Christlike virtue-in-practice;
2. a concise explanation of the behavior-first methodology;
3. a user guide for the app;
4. the launch point into the reusable experience.

Chapters 19–29 then function naturally as **contexts where Chapter 18 practices are lived**: neighbor, marriage, parenting, stewardship, witness, service, trials, treasure, endurance, and related settings.

## 11. Methodological identity

The product's evidence claim should remain modest and transparent:

- primary focus on New Testament narratives of Jesus;
- observable/reproducible conduct over abstract trait attribution;
- distinguish enacted behavior from teaching-only evidence;
- preserve textual-critical notes when a practice depends upon a disputed passage;
- avoid using denominational traditions, creeds, commentaries, or theological systems as the extraction mechanism;
- secondary interpretations may be added later only when clearly labeled and intentionally desired.
