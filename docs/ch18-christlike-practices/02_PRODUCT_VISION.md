# Product Vision

## Working product concept

A replayable discipleship formation application built around observable behaviors of Jesus of Nazareth in the New Testament.

The app is intended to help Christ-believers ask:

> **What did Jesus actually do in the presence of real people, and what might it look like for me to practice that behavior intentionally?**

## Product thesis

Christian discipleship can become vague when expressed only as abstract aspirations such as “be compassionate,” “be humble,” or “be Christlike.” Gospel narratives provide a more concrete alternative: observable choices made by Jesus in situations involving grief, conflict, exclusion, failure, power, exhaustion, need, disagreement, acclaim, doubt, leadership, and service.

The product turns those observations into repeatable practices without pretending that a human being ever “completes” a virtue.

## Core product principles

### 1. Formation over completion
Never imply 70/70 complete, all virtues mastered, spiritual level achieved, domain cleared, or practice permanently integrated.

### 2. Agency over prescription
The user chooses where to focus. Assessment results suggest rather than assign. Suggested actions remain optional.

### 3. Behavior over self-image
Prefer concrete questions and commitments over trait claims.

Avoid: “How compassionate are you?”

Prefer: “When someone begins telling me something important at an inconvenient time, I usually give them my attention before deciding what needs to happen next.”

Avoid: “I will become more patient.”

Prefer: “When this person asks again, I will first ask what they understand and explain again without signaling irritation.”

### 4. Observation before interpretation
Every practice should answer: **What did Jesus do that warrants calling this a Christlike practice?**

### 5. Progressive disclosure
Do not confront a first-time user with a wall of approximately 70 obligations.

Initial experience should communicate:

> **Eight ways of practicing the way of Jesus. Choose one for today.**

### 6. Reflection completes the practice cycle
Returning users should be invited to consider what happened, what they noticed, what was difficult, what they learned, what they would try differently, and whether to repeat or explore another practice.

### 7. Repetition is success, not failure
Returning to the same practice is the normal form of discipleship. Show “Practiced previously” and “Practice this again,” not permanent completion.

### 8. Pastoral, non-clinical assessment
The behavioral self-assessment is a discernment aid, not a diagnostic instrument.

### 9. A sibling, not a clone, of Keep My Commandments
Reuse proven product patterns where appropriate, but preserve distinct semantics.

## Primary audience experience

The product should feel less like a course and more like a **quiet discipleship workshop** embedded in the larger Words of Plainness journey.

The emotional posture should be invitational, concrete, challenging without shaming, reflective, Christ-centered, repeatable, and respectful of agency.

## Core loop

> **DISCERN → ENCOUNTER → CHOOSE → PRACTICE → REFLECT → RETURN**

## Success definition

A successful session may be only:

1. encounter one practice of Jesus;
2. recognize a present-life situation where it matters;
3. choose one concrete behavior to attempt;
4. later return and reflect honestly.

The product succeeds when it moves discipleship from admiration to intentional imitation.
