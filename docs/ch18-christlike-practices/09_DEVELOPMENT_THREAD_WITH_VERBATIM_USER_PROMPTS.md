## Assistant

Yes. Your 35 already capture much of the moral architecture of Jesus' conduct, but the New Testament narratives contain another layer that tends to disappear when Jesus is summarized mainly by doctrines or abstract virtues. A fresh action-first reading turns up repeated practices involving **privacy, follow-through, embodied care, leadership development, restraint of one's own group, repair, receiving help, affirmation, de-stigmatization, and the handling of conflict**.

For this pass, I treated the text as a behavioral corpus. I used the New Testament with the Gospel narratives as the primary dataset. Acts and the Epistles were treated as secondary evidence when they explicitly describe Jesus' conduct, rather than using later theological interpretation to generate virtues. Revelation was not treated as historical interaction data.

My extraction rule was deliberately strict: **What does Jesus actually do, refrain from doing, permit, interrupt, repair, ask, receive, protect, or follow up on that an ordinary disciple could meaningfully imitate?**

### Strengthening several of the original 35

- **6. Unostentation:** Matthew 8:10 is weak evidence. Stronger anchors are Matthew 12:15-19; Mark 7:33-36; Mark 8:23-26.
- **9. Compassion without permissiveness:** add John 5:14 so the practice does not depend entirely on John 8.
- **11. Deliberateness under pressure:** add Mark 12:13-17 and John 18:4-11.
- **20. Non-retaliation:** add Matthew 26:51-54; Luke 22:50-51; and 1 Peter 2:23.
- **25. Gentleness with honest doubt:** add Matthew 11:2-11.
- **26. Love of enemies:** add Luke 22:50-51.
- **28. Forgiveness without limit:** add John 21:15-19.
- **30. Integrity without hypocrisy:** add Mark 1:35; Luke 5:16; Luke 6:12.
- **33. Peacemaking:** add Luke 9:54-56; Luke 22:49-51; Matthew 26:52.
- **34. Trust / freedom from anxiety:** add Mark 4:35-40 and Matthew 26:36-46.

A data-hygiene issue also emerged: John 7:53–8:11 has an unusually unstable manuscript history, so #9 and #11 should not depend solely upon it. Luke 23:34 likewise has early manuscript variation and is better used as corroborating rather than sole evidence.

Several further patterns were preserved as possible independent practices:

- **Responsiveness to another person's argument** / persuadability without ego commitment (Mark 7:24-30; Matthew 15:21-28).
- **Epistemic restraint** (Mark 13:32; Acts 1:6-8).
- **Listening as a learner** (Luke 2:46-47).
- **Dignifying marginalized people as contributors** (Luke 10:38-42; Luke 8:1-3; John 20:17-18).
- **Celebrating the growth of others** (Luke 10:17-21).

The larger discovery was that “virtue” had become too small a container. The list mixed moral orientations, interpersonal practices, conflict rules, leadership practices, disciplines, evaluative habits, and decision rules. That suggested a mature framework of virtue domains containing approximately 60–70 observable discipleship practices.

The strongest form of self-examination would therefore not be “Am I a peacemaker?” but questions such as:

> **When someone on my side mistreats an opponent, whose injury concerns me first?**

This moves imitation of Jesus from admiration of traits into observable choices.

## Assistant

Yes. This was judged to be **better than the earlier reference-catalog architecture** because it transforms Chapter 18 into a replayable discipleship experience.

The key conceptual shift:

> **Chapter 18 is not something the reader completes. It is somewhere the reader returns.**

Each visit becomes a new cycle of **discernment → exploration → commitment → practice → return**.

### Two equal entry paths

At the beginning, after a brief explanation of the eight domains, give the reader two equal choices:

| Path | Reader experience |
|---|---|
| **Choose a Domain** | “I already know where I want to grow.” |
| **Find a Growth Focus** | “Help me discern where to begin.” |

The assessment should **recommend rather than assign**.

Avoid language such as *weakest virtue*, *deficiency*, *score*, or *your lowest domain*. Prefer language such as:

> **A Possible Growth Focus**
>
> Based on your responses, **Grace & Restoration** may offer a particularly meaningful place for practice right now.
>
> You may choose this domain, explore another recommendation, or select any domain yourself.

The reader remains the decision-maker.

### The Chapter 18 experience

A single visit could look like:

```text
WELCOME
  ↓
HOW WOULD YOU LIKE TO BEGIN?
  ├─ Choose a Domain
  └─ Help Me Find a Growth Focus
  ↓
SELECTED DOMAIN
  ↓
PRACTICE CARDS
```

For example:

### Grace & Restoration

> Jesus continued loving, teaching, forgiving, and calling people who failed, doubted, misunderstood, betrayed, and disappointed Him. Grace becomes visible when we refuse to define people permanently by their worst moments.

Then the domain's six practices become cards.

For a practice such as:

### Patience with Slow Understanding
*Teaching the same hearts again and again*  
Luke 24:13–35

retain the familiar tabs:

- **How We Practice**
- **How It Blesses Lives**
- **How Will You Practice?**

The last tab should become genuinely interactive. Rather than only asking a reflective question, offer concrete behavioral possibilities plus a custom response:

> Someone in my life is learning more slowly than I would prefer. This week I will:
>
> ○ Explain again without showing irritation  
> ○ Ask what they understand before correcting them  
> ○ Separate misunderstanding from unwillingness  
> ○ Give them another opportunity after a mistake  
> ○ Write my own practice

This makes the card actionable rather than merely inspirational.

### Behavioral assessment

Do not ask abstract self-image questions such as:

> How compassionate are you?

Instead use recognizable situations.

Examples:

**Grace & Restoration**
> When someone repeatedly misunderstands something I have explained, I can usually teach it again without making them feel foolish.

**Attentive Presence**
> When someone begins telling me something important at an inconvenient time, I usually give them my full attention before deciding what needs to happen next.

**Peace & Right Use of Power**
> When someone on “my side” treats an opponent unfairly, I am willing to acknowledge the harm even when doing so weakens my side's argument.

**Forming & Empowering Others**
> When I can do something faster or better myself, I still make room for others to learn by participating.

A working target of **24 questions** was proposed: three per domain.

Suggested scale:

**Rarely · Sometimes · Often · Usually**

with **Not applicable / unsure** where necessary.

Domain labels should be hidden during the assessment to reduce demand effects.

At the end, recommend **two domains**, not one, and ask:

> **Which would you like to explore today?**

The algorithm informs discernment. It does not replace discernment.

### Choosing within a domain

Users should not be required to work through every card.

Allow them to mark items such as:

♡ **This speaks to me**

or:

**Consider for Practice**

Then invite selection of **one** practice for intentional action.

This creates replayability even within the same domain, because a later visit may surface a different practice.

### Commitments should be small and behavioral

Do not settle for:

> “I will be more forgiving.”

Instead ask:

1. **Who or what situation comes to mind?**
2. **What will you actually do differently?**
3. **When might you practice this?**

Then form a concrete intention.

Example:

> **Grace & Restoration → Patience with Slow Understanding**
>
> *When my grandson asks me again about something we have already discussed, I will first ask what he currently understands and explain again without showing frustration.*

### Replayability modes

A user can return for:

| Return experience | What changes |
|---|---|
| Different domain | Explore another family of Christlike behavior |
| Same domain, different practice | Deepen an area already under development |
| Retake assessment | Reconsider current growth focus |
| Revisit previous commitment | Reflect on what happened |
| Repeat same practice | Refine a difficult behavior |

There should be **no completion checkmark** implying that a practice has been permanently mastered.

Instead show:

> **Practiced previously: March 2027**

with:

> **Practice this again**

### Return loop

When a user returns after a commitment:

> **Welcome back.**
>
> Last time you chose:
>
> **Grace & Restoration → Patience with Slow Understanding**
>
> Before beginning another exploration, would you like to reflect on that practice?

Possible reflection questions:

- What happened?
- What did you notice about yourself or others?
- Would you like to continue this practice or explore another?

This changes Chapter 18 from an interactive article into a longitudinal formation tool.

The dynamic model became:

> **DISCERN → CHOOSE → LEARN → PRACTICE → REFLECT → RETURN**

The page should resist showing all seventy practices at once. The opening message should communicate abundance without burden:

> **Eight ways of practicing the way of Jesus. Choose one for today.**

The reader should experience possibility rather than inadequacy.
