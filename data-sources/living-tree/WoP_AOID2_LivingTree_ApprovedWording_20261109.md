## Part 1: Page introduction

> The Living Tree of Christ-Believing Traditions shows twenty-five branches of the Christian family. Choose a branch to read how that tradition describes itself, the texts its churches have adopted, and where its own churches welcome those who want to learn more. Every description is drawn from the tradition's own published words, or, where no church speaks for a branch, labelled as ours, and every link goes to the church's own copy wherever one exists.

## Part 2: Teaching additions (approved; Teaching Practices Standard)

### 2.1 Practice line (shown once, directly after the introduction)
> Before you open a branch, say in a sentence how you would describe it; then read how it describes itself.

### 2.2 How to read a tradition fairly (shown once, after the practice line)
> How to read a tradition fairly: listen to its own words; see what its churches have adopted; ask who speaks for it; and notice where no one does.

### 2.3 Key to the rows and the placement lines (shown once, before the branch list)
> The five rows group the branches by where they arose in Christian history. Each branch also carries a placement line: one of the sixteen families counted in the Articles, a movement counted within the families its churches belong to, a branch counted within another family, or outside the count because no member churches with adopted texts can be read for it.

## Part 3: Branch descriptions (one per branch, in Tree order)

### church-of-the-east | Church of the East
> The Assyrian Church of the East (in full, the Holy Apostolic Catholic Assyrian Church of the East) dates its beginning to the missionary work of the Apostolic Age and attributes its Eucharistic liturgy to "the Apostles, Addai and Mari, who discipled the East." It teaches "the faith of the universal Church as set forth in the Nicene Creed" and is governed on the apostolic model of bishops, presbyters and deacons.
Sources:
- Assyrian Church of the East, home page | https://www.assyrianchurch.org/
- Assyrian Church of the East, About Us | https://www.assyrianchurch.org/about-us/

### oriental-orthodox | Oriental Orthodox
> The Oriental Orthodox churches, among them the Coptic, Armenian, Syriac, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Malankara churches, describe themselves as a "family" of churches, "united in distinctive theological teachings" and "in communion with one another since ancient times." The Coptic Orthodox Church, for one, honours St. Mark the Apostle as "Egypt's missionary and founder of its Church."
Sources:
- The Armenian Church, Eastern Diocese | https://armenianchurch.us/?p=11309
- Coptic Orthodox Church, Coptic History | https://copticorthodox.church/en/coptic-church/coptic-history/

### eastern-orthodox | Eastern Orthodox
> The Orthodox Church confesses in the Creed "one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church," and teaches that the Church is one "because God is one" and catholic because it is "full, complete, whole, with nothing lacking." "Orthodox Christians believe that in the historical Orthodox Church there exists the full possibility of participating totally in the Church of God."
Source:
- Orthodox Church in America, The Orthodox Faith, vol. I, The Symbol of Faith: Church | https://www.oca.org/orthodoxy/the-orthodox-faith/doctrine-scripture/the-symbol-of-faith/church

### catholicism | Catholicism
> The Catholic Church professes "the sole Church of Christ, which in the Creed we profess to be one, holy, catholic and apostolic," and teaches that this Church, entrusted by the risen Lord to Peter's pastoral care, "subsists in the Catholic Church, which is governed by the successor of Peter and by the bishops in communion with him."
Source:
- Catechism of the Catholic Church, 811, 816 | https://www.vatican.va/archive/ENG0015/__P29.HTM

### lutheran | Lutheran
> Lutheran churches confess that forgiveness, won by Christ, is "a free gift to all who desire it," and they receive the Lutheran Confessions gathered in the Book of Concord as their witness to biblical doctrine. In their own words, they "gather around the word and sacraments, trusting God's grace, shaped by Jesus Christ."
Sources:
- Lutheran Church—Missouri Synod, Beliefs | https://www.lcms.org/about/beliefs
- Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, Faith Tradition | https://www.elca.org/about/what-we-believe/elca-faith-tradition

### reformed | Reformed
> Reformed churches trace their roots to the sixteenth-century Reformation, "particularly in the theology of John Calvin," and sometimes say they are "Reformed and always reforming." They confess their faith in historic standards such as the Westminster Confession of Faith with its Larger and Shorter Catechisms, the Heidelberg Catechism, the Belgic Confession and the Canons of Dort, and speak of a "Reformed accent" in which sovereignty, covenant, and kingdom "figure prominently."
Sources:
- World Communion of Reformed Churches, About | https://wcrc.eu/about/
- Reformed Church in America, Our Reformed Theology | https://www.rca.org/about/theology/
- Presbyterian Church in America, Beliefs | https://pcanet.org/beliefs/
- Christian Reformed Church in North America, Reformed Accent | https://www.crcna.org/welcome/beliefs/reformed-accent

### anglican | Anglican
> The Church of England describes itself as "part of the One, Holy, Catholic and Apostolic Church," professing "the faith uniquely revealed in the Holy Scriptures and set forth in the catholic creeds," to which its historic formularies (the Thirty-nine Articles, The Book of Common Prayer and the Ordinal) bear witness. Anglicans describe their churches around the world as "a global family of churches united in a common mission."
Sources:
- Church of England, Declaration of Assent, Canon C 15, Preface | https://www.churchofengland.org/node/9707/printable/print
- Anglican Communion, What is the Anglican Communion? | https://www.anglicancommunion.org/structures/what-is-the-anglican-communion.aspx

### anabaptist | Anabaptist
> The Mennonite and Brethren in Christ churches of Mennonite World Conference confess together, "By the grace of God, we seek to live and proclaim the good news of reconciliation in Jesus Christ," practising "baptism upon confession of faith" and calling believers to be "peacemakers who renounce violence, love our enemies, seek justice." The Church of the Brethren, which subscribes "to no formal 'creed' or set of rules," puts it plainly: "We simply try to do what Jesus did."
Sources:
- Mennonite World Conference, Shared Convictions | https://mwc-cmm.org/en/shared-convictions
- Church of the Brethren, About us | https://www.brethren.org/about/

### baptist | Baptist
> Baptists describe themselves as "a confessional people" who hold their confessions to be "only guides in interpretation, having no authority over the conscience," and affirm "Believers' Baptism by immersion" as "the biblical way to publicly declare discipleship." Through the Baptist World Alliance, by its own count "a global network of 53 million Baptists," they confess that "faith in Christ entails a passionate commitment to religious liberty, freedom, peace and justice."
Sources:
- Southern Baptist Convention, Baptist Faith and Message 2000, Preamble | https://bfm.sbc.net/preamble/
- Baptist World Alliance, Beliefs Statement, articles 10 and 16 | https://baptistworld.org/beliefs/

### methodist | Methodist
> Methodist churches trace their heritage to John Wesley and see themselves as standing "within the continuity of the one universal Church." They teach that "the grace of God is undivided": it "precedes salvation as 'prevenient grace,' continues in 'justifying grace,'" and "is brought to fruition in 'sanctifying grace.'"
Sources:
- World Methodist Council, About | https://worldmethodistcouncil.org/about/
- The United Methodist Church, Distinctive Wesleyan Emphases, Book of Discipline 2016 | https://www.umc.org/content/distinctive-wesleyan-emphases

### evangelical | Evangelical
> Evangelicals describe themselves as Christians who "take the Bible seriously and believe in Jesus Christ as Savior and Lord," and the National Association of Evangelicals names four marks: conversionism, biblicism, activism and crucicentrism, "a stress on the sacrifice of Jesus Christ on the cross." The World Evangelical Alliance calls its shared statement of faith "a foundation, not a ceiling," since "each alliance and denomination holds its own fuller confession of faith."
Sources:
- National Association of Evangelicals, What is an Evangelical? | https://www.nae.org/what-is-an-evangelical/
- World Evangelical Alliance, Statement of Faith | https://worldea.org/who-we-are/statement-of-faith/

### holiness | Holiness
> The Holiness churches stand in what the Church of the Nazarene calls "the Wesleyan-Holiness tradition," and teach that God, through the Holy Spirit, enables believers to "live holy lives, being made new." The Church of the Nazarene confesses entire sanctification as "that act of God, subsequent to regeneration, by which believers are made free from original sin," and The Salvation Army that "it is the privilege of all believers to be wholly sanctified."
Sources:
- Church of the Nazarene, Who We Are | https://nazarene.org/who-we-are
- The Wesleyan Church, About | https://www.wesleyan.org/about
- Church of the Nazarene, What We Believe, Christian Holiness and Entire Sanctification | https://nazarene.org/what-we-believe
- The Salvation Army Canada and Bermuda, We Believe, Doctrine 10 | https://salvationist.ca/articles/2009/10/we-believe-e2-80-a6/

### pentecostal | Pentecostal
> Pentecostal churches teach that all believers should "ardently expect and earnestly seek" the baptism in the Holy Spirit, "the normal experience of all in the early Christian Church," which brings "the enduement of power for life and service." Five of the six churches listed here confess that speaking in tongues accompanies that baptism, and The Foursquare Church, "a Spirit-filled movement," pledges to "consistently minister the Baptism of the Holy Spirit."
Sources:
- Assemblies of God, Statement of Fundamental Truths, articles 7 and 8 | https://ag.org/Beliefs/Statement-of-Fundamental-Truths
- Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee), Declaration of Faith | https://churchofgod.org/beliefs/declaration-of-faith/
- International Pentecostal Holiness Church, Beliefs, article 11 | https://iphc.org/beliefs/
- United Pentecostal Church International, Our Beliefs | https://www.upci.org/about/our-beliefs
- Church of God in Christ, What We Believe | https://www.cogic.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/What-We-Believe.pdf
- The Foursquare Church, Beliefs, Global Distinctive 5 | https://www.foursquare.org/about/beliefs/

### charismatic | Charismatic
> No single church or body speaks for the Charismatic branch. *In our words:* it is a renewal that seeks the baptism in the Holy Spirit and the gifts of the Spirit, carried within historic churches and through independent networks of its own. Its voices range from the Catholic Charismatic Renewal, which CHARIS, a body established by the Holy See, calls a "current of grace," to independent networks such as Vineyard USA.
Sources:
- CHARIS, Who We Are | https://formation.charis.international/pages/who-we-are
- Dicastery for Laity, Family and Life, CHARIS | https://www.laityfamilylife.va/content/laityfamilylife/en/associazioni-e-movimenti/repertorio/charis.html
- Vineyard USA, About | https://vineyardusa.org/about/

### non-denominational | Non-denominational
> No single church or body speaks for Non-denominational churches; each congregation speaks for itself. *In our words:* these are congregations that belong to no denomination and set their own statements of faith and practice, though many join loose fellowships or networks. In the United States, where they have been studied most closely, researchers at the Hartford Institute for Religion Research judge that "the vast majority of nondenominational churches would and should be categorized as evangelical."
Source:
- Hartford Institute for Religion Research, 2020 FACT report on nondenominational churches | https://hirr.hartfordinternational.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Report-on-the-2020-Faith-Communities-Today-Survey-of-Nondenominational-and-Independent-Churches.pdf

### stone-campbell | Stone-Campbell
> The Stone-Campbell churches trace their roots to Barton W. Stone, who "called for a return to simple New Testament Christianity," and to the Campbells, who championed "one Church of Christ upon earth"; today they are found mainly in three streams: the Churches of Christ, the Christian Churches/Churches of Christ, and the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ). The Disciples, who "honor our heritage as a movement for Christian unity," say, "As part of the one Body of Christ, we welcome all to the Lord's Table as God has welcomed us."
Sources:
- Disciples of Christ Historical Society, Disciples History | https://www.discipleshistory.org/node/9
- Christian Church (Disciples of Christ), Our Identity | https://disciples.org/our-identity/

### latter-day-saints | The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints
> The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints declares that it is "Christ's New Testament Church restored," "built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Jesus Christ himself being the chief corner stone" (Ephesians 2:20). It testifies that through His "infinite Atonement and literal Resurrection" Jesus Christ is "our Savior, our Exemplar, and our Redeemer," and its apostles "invite all to know ... that the heavens are open."
Sources:
- The Restoration of the Fulness of the Gospel of Jesus Christ: A Bicentennial Proclamation to the World, First Presidency and Council of the Twelve Apostles, 5 April 2020 | https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/the-restoration-of-the-fulness-of-the-gospel-of-jesus-christ/a-bicentennial-proclamation-to-the-world?lang=eng
- Ephesians 2:20 | https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/eph/2?lang=eng&id=p20#p20

### adventist | Adventist
> Seventh-day Adventists "accept the Bible as their only creed" and hold "certain fundamental beliefs to be the teaching of the Holy Scriptures." They describe themselves as "devoted to helping people understand the Bible to find freedom, healing, and hope in Jesus."
Source:
- Seventh-day Adventist Church, Beliefs, preamble to the 28 Fundamental Beliefs | https://www.adventist.org/beliefs/

### jehovahs-witnesses | Jehovah's Witnesses
> Jehovah's Witnesses describe themselves as "Christians who do our best to follow the example set by Jesus Christ and to live by his teachings," and say, "we pattern our worship after that of the first-century Christians." They "do not look to any human as their leader," holding to Jesus' words, "Your Leader is one, the Christ."
Source:
- Jehovah's Witnesses, Frequently Asked Questions | https://www.jw.org/en/jehovahs-witnesses/faq/are-jehovahs-witnesses-a-cult/

### dispensationalism | Dispensationalism
> No single church or body speaks for Dispensationalism, a movement counted within the families its churches belong to. *In our words:* it is a way of reading the Bible as unfolding through a sequence of dispensations in God's dealings with people, found in churches of several families. Dallas Theological Seminary, speaking for itself, describes the dispensations as "stewardships by which God administers His purpose on the earth."
Source:
- Dallas Theological Seminary, Doctrinal Statement, Article V | https://www.dts.edu/about/doctrinal-statement/

### messianic-jews | Messianic Jews
> No single church or body speaks for Messianic Jews. *In our words:* they are Jewish believers who honor Jesus, called Yeshua, as the Messiah of Israel while keeping Jewish identity and worship. The Union of Messianic Jewish Congregations calls itself "a network of congregations that honor Yeshua, the Messiah of Israel," while the Messianic Jewish Alliance of America declares that "Yeshua (Hebrew for Jesus) is the promised Jewish Messiah and Savior of the world."
Sources:
- Union of Messianic Jewish Congregations | https://www.umjc.org/
- Messianic Jewish Alliance of America, About | https://www.mjaa.org/about/

### african-initiated-churches | African Initiated Churches
> No single church or body speaks for all the African Initiated Churches. *In our words:* they are churches founded and led by Africans, many independent of the missionary churches since the late nineteenth century, and they vary widely in worship, organization and teaching. The Organization of African Instituted Churches gathers many of them "together in fellowship" to "preach the Good News of Jesus Christ in word and deed," keeping their theology "rooted in the AICs' founding visions."
Sources:
- World Council of Churches, African Instituted Churches | https://www.oikoumene.org/church-families/african-instituted-churches
- Organization of African Instituted Churches | https://oaic.org/

### chinese-house-churches | Chinese House Churches
> No church or network is named here, by design, for the safety of their members. *In our words:* these are Chinese Protestant congregations, from household gatherings to large regional networks, that worship outside the state-registered Protestant system; in the Pew Research Center's words, a house church "typically refers to an unregistered Protestant church or a Protestant church whose leader refuses to affiliate with the state-run Christian agency."
Sources:
- Pew Research Center, Measuring Religion in China (2023), glossary | https://legacy.pewresearch.org/religion/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2023/08/PF_2023.08.30_religion-china_A-A.pdf
- Immigration and Refugee Board of Canada, Responses to Information Requests (2025) | https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2124878.html

### independent-new-religious-movements | Independent New Religious Movements
> No single church or body speaks for this branch. *In our words:* it gathers independent movements that arose in relation to Christianity, hold distinctive revelations, scriptures or structures of authority, and themselves claim to follow Jesus Christ; how closely each stands to historic Christian teaching differs from one movement to the next. Traditions pictured elsewhere on the Tree, including The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, Jehovah's Witnesses, and the Adventist and Stone-Campbell churches, are not part of this branch.
Source: none shown (the description is in our words under the ruling for this branch; no movement or community is named).

### local-and-regional-expressions | Local & Regional Expressions
> No single church or body speaks for this branch. *In our words:* it gathers Christian communities whose church life is shaped by a particular people, culture or region and that belong to none of the other branches, such as independent Indigenous or island churches. Cultural expression within an established church, such as an Indigenous parish of a historic denomination, belongs with that church's own branch.
Source: none shown (the description is in our words under the ruling for this branch; no movement or community is named).
