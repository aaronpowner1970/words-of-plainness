## Span text (Second Edition)
s6: Trusting Him as our Guide is more important than understanding Him.
s7: Personal relationship with the Divine takes precedence over accuracy of doctrine.
s8: Correct tenets and sound understanding are desirable, but faithfully following Christ is better.
s9: Our hope is that, as we submit to Him, God will make our paths straight.

## Manuscript ground
- Ch 4 (Spiritual Knowledge): "It is NOT correct doctrine that will save one's soul. Rather, it is one's faith in God and efforts to follow His Christ."
- Ch 6 (Faith in Jesus Christ): it will not matter which of us is right about anything; what will matter is that Jesus Christ was right about everything; let no Christian condemn others over differences of belief.

## s6 floor (one candidate)
The Article confesses only what Proverbs 3:5-6 and 2 Corinthians 5:7 confess: we trust in the LORD with all our heart and lean not unto our own understanding, and He shall direct our paths; and we walk by faith, not by sight. This is the floor: trust in God's guidance does not wait on our understanding of Him, and where the two part, trust leads. It does not settle how faith and reason are related, how far God may be known by nature or by grace, or what place the intellect has in faith; each tradition fills those in its own house.
Weakness: Proverbs names the LORD and the span names Christ as Guide; the floor does not settle their relation, so a scorer reads "the LORD" in the tradition's own terms.

## s7 floor (three ranked candidates; check hardest)
Counter-texts weighed: 1 Timothy 4:16 ("take heed ... unto the doctrine"), 2 John 1:9 ("abideth not in the doctrine of Christ, hath not God"), Titus 1:9 ("by sound doctrine"). None says that accurate doctrine ranks above knowing God; each binds the doctrine of Christ to abiding in Him. A floor that honours them states that precedence is not dismissal.

### A (recommended)
The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 8:1-3 and Matthew 7:21-23 confess: knowledge puffeth up, but charity edifieth, and if any man love God, the same is known of him; and not every one that saith unto Christ, Lord, Lord, shall enter the kingdom, for to some who spoke in His name He will say, I never knew you. This is the floor: to love God and be known of Him comes first, and accurate words about Him are not the measure of the disciple. Precedence is not dismissal: doctrine is ranked, not discarded, and abiding in the doctrine of Christ (2 John 1:9) is not set against abiding in Him. It does not settle which doctrines are necessary to salvation, how much right belief God requires of each soul, or by what authority doctrine is defined; each tradition fills those in its own house.
Weakness: confessional Lutheran and Reformed texts, the Orthodox, and the Athanasian Creed's "whosoever will be saved" bind right belief closely to salvation; some cells may read "not the measure" as minimizing. This is the span's real claim, so a shortfall would be disclosed, not avoided. 2 John 1:9 is cited inside the floor but is not an s7 anchor; it would be added as one.

### B
The Article confesses only what Matthew 7:21-23 and 1 Corinthians 8:1-3 confess: not every one that saith Lord, Lord shall enter the kingdom, and if any man love God, the same is known of him. This is the floor: right confession without love of God and doing His will does not save. It does not settle ...
Weakness: nearly every tradition holds it, but it is weaker than the span. The span ranks relationship above accuracy; B says only that accuracy is not enough. Scoring B would report agreement the span's own claim may not have.

### C
The Article confesses only what John 17:3 and Philippians 3:8-10 confess: life eternal is to know God and Jesus Christ whom He has sent, and to know Him is the excellency for which all else is counted loss. This is the floor: knowing God is the end, and doctrine serves it. It does not settle ...
Weakness: many traditions read "know thee" in John 17:3 as including true doctrine, so scorers could count C on either reading; the floor would not test the ranking the span makes.

## s8 floor (one candidate)
The Article confesses only what Luke 9:23, James 1:22 and John 10:27 confess: if any man will come after Christ, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily, and follow Him; we are doers of the word, and not hearers only; and Christ's sheep hear His voice and follow Him. This is the floor: sound doctrine is good, and it serves the following of Christ, by which the disciple's life is measured. It does not settle how faith, grace and works are related in salvation, whether following Christ is the fruit or the condition of salvation, or which doctrines are necessary; each tradition fills those in its own house.
Weakness: "better" is a ranking word, and the floor carries it as "serves" and "measured by"; a scorer reading through a grace-and-works lens might mistake the ranking for a claim about merit. The exclusion clause is written to stop that (no grace-versus-works axis).

## s9 floor (one candidate)
The Article confesses only what Proverbs 3:5-6, James 4:7-8 and Psalm 32:8 confess: in all our ways we acknowledge Him, and He shall direct our paths; we submit ourselves to God and draw nigh to Him, and He will draw nigh to us; and He will instruct us and teach us in the way which we shall go. This is the floor: God guides those who submit to Him, and our hope rests on His promise. It does not settle the manner of God's guidance (by Scripture, conscience, the Church or inward prompting), how His guidance bears on human freedom, or whether the straight path is assured in this life; each tradition fills those in its own house.
Weakness: the span's "make our paths straight" echoes 2 Nephi 4:33 and the Proverbs 3:6 margin more than the KJV text ("direct thy paths"); the floor quotes the KJV text.
