## Floors

### A5-F1. The promised Messiah, sent in the fulness of time (s1, s2, s3)

> The Article confesses only what Galatians 4:4, Isaiah 9:6-7 with Acts 10:43, and 1 John 2:22 confess: when the fulness of the time was come, God sent forth His Son; unto us a child is born, whose government and peace shall have no end, and to Him give all the prophets witness; and he is a liar that denieth that Jesus is the Christ. This is the floor: in the time God chose, He sent Jesus, the Messiah the prophets foretold, and Jesus is the Christ. It does not settle how each prophecy is to be read, or which marks distinguish the true Christ from false ones beyond the confession that Jesus is the Christ; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

### A5-F2. The Son of God, Divinity made flesh (s4, s8)

> The Article confesses only what John 1:1, 14, Colossians 2:9 and John 3:16-17 confess: the Word was with God, and the Word was God, and the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us; in Him dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily; and God sent His only begotten Son into the world, that the world through Him might be saved. This is the floor: Jesus of Nazareth is the divine Son of God, sent from the Father, the Word made flesh. It does not settle the Son's relation to the Father (one substance, one Godhead of distinct persons, or otherwise), whether or how the Son is eternally begotten, how His divine and human natures are joined, or the manner of His existence before His birth; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the Son family's scope note (codex A4, Example, RNR-H05) holds that shared filial identity controls and Nicene eternal generation is not required. The exclusion follows it.

Scoring risk: Jehovah's Witnesses read John 1:1 as "a god" and Colossians 2:9 as "divine quality," and teach that the Son was God's first creation. Whether their adopted texts make "the divine Son ... the Word made flesh" is a C4(a)(i) judgment the scorer quotes. The floor is not narrowed to get past the cell.

### A5-F3. The image of the Father (s5)

> The Article confesses only what Colossians 1:15, Hebrews 1:3 and John 14:9 confess: Christ is the image of the invisible God, the express image of His person; he that hath seen me hath seen the Father. This is the floor: in Christ the Father is seen and known. It does not settle what "image" says about the Father's own nature or form, or how the Son's likeness to the Father is to be described; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct; "the Father's heart" is theological inference from Christ's compassion (Matthew 9:36; Matthew 23:37), which the anchors carry.

### A5-F4a and A5-F4b. The prophets in part and in earthen vessels; the Son in whole (s6, two floors under A4(f))

**RULED by Aaron 27 Oct 2026, 04:37 EDT: "s6 stands; two floors as revised" (AOID2-A05-S6, topic-only).** The text is unchanged: "What the prophets spoke imperfectly and only in part, Christ embodied in whole and in perfection." Aaron meant "imperfectly" (27 Oct, 04:29 and 04:35 EDT): the prophets spoke truly but from imperfect human perspectives in limited human language; Scripture is without error in what it affirms, "but the affirmation itself is a broken vessel ... yet [it manages] to bring souls to the God who can save and perfect them." Chat's v1 objection that the word breached A4(g) is withdrawn: A4(g) governs floors and commentary, not what an Article confesses; a dividing teaching is kept plainly and its division disclosed (section 0). The floor ledger names A5-F4a as paragraph 1 and A5-F4b as paragraph 2 (A4(f)).

> **A5-F4a.** The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 13:9-12, 1 Peter 1:10-11, Hebrews 1:1-2 and John 5:39-40 confess: we know in part and prophesy in part, and see through a glass, darkly; the prophets enquired and searched diligently what the Spirit of Christ in them did signify; God, who spake by the prophets at sundry times and in divers manners, hath in these last days spoken unto us by His Son; and the scriptures testify of Christ, yet life is found in coming to Him. This is the floor: the prophets saw and spoke in part; in Christ the fulness is embodied, and life is in Him, to whom the Scriptures testify. It does not settle whether God continues to reveal, or how Scripture and the Church's teaching are related; each tradition fills those in its own house.

> **A5-F4b.** The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 13:9-10 and 2 Corinthians 4:7 confess: we prophesy in part, and that which is in part stands against that which is perfect; and we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us. This is the floor: the prophets spoke truly, yet as imperfect men in limited human words; "imperfectly" names the vessel, not the truth it carries, and the power by which their witness brings souls to God is His, not theirs. Only Christ is the perfect Word. It does not settle how the prophets' words were kept, gathered into a canon, translated or rightly interpreted; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for "in part" and "in whole"; A5-F4b's definition of "imperfectly" follows Aaron's stated meaning and 2 Corinthians 4:7.

Apparatus additions for s6: biblical column, 1 Corinthians 13:9-12, 1 Peter 1:10-12, Daniel 12:8-9, Job 26:14, 1 Corinthians 8:2, John 5:39-40, 2 Corinthians 4:7; Restoration column, Articles of Faith 1:8 as witness. Aaron's fuller case (transmission, loss, canon, translation; Articles of Faith 1:8; Christ, not a book) is logged as Volume 2 item V2-023.

No result is predicted. v1 expected A5-F4b to be witness; with Aaron's clarification much of it may be common ground (the Chicago Statement, Articles XIII and X, grants the human authors' styles and the limits of their language). The scoring decides. The panel comment's opening paragraphs carry the meaning of "imperfectly" to a reader who might first hear it as "in error."

### A5-F5. Savior and Redeemer of the world (s7)

### A5-F6. Born of the virgin Mary, without sin (s9)

**Article text change APPROVED by Aaron 27 Oct 2026, 04:40 EDT (AOID2-A05-S9-VIRGIN, option 1, topic-only).** Live: "He was born of Mary, lived a sinless life." Approved (Second Edition): "He was born of the virgin Mary, lived a sinless life." Applied with the other approved text changes, behind the Second Edition switch; Article 5's narration and film to be checked first.

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 1:18, 23, Luke 1:34-35, Hebrews 4:15 and 1 Peter 2:22 confess: before Joseph and Mary came together, she was found with child of the Holy Ghost; behold, a virgin shall be with child, and shall bring forth a son; Mary said, How shall this be, seeing I know not a man? and the angel answered, The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, therefore that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God; He was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin; who did no sin, neither was guile found in His mouth. This is the floor: Jesus was born of the virgin Mary, and He lived without sin. It does not settle Mary's place and honors (Mother of God, her own sinlessness, perpetual virginity), the manner of the conception beyond what Matthew and Luke state, or how His sinlessness is related to His divine nature; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Superseded draft (v1, never ratified): "This is the floor: Jesus was born of Mary, and He lived without sin."

Kind of witness: direct.

Text question (RESOLVED 27 Oct 2026, option 1; kept on record): the Article says "born of Mary," and no Article says "virgin." Matthew 1:18-25 and Luke 1:34-35 state the virgin conception plainly; the span's own anchors carry it (Isaiah 7:14, Luke 1:26-35, Matthew 1:18-25, and Alma 7:10 and 1 Nephi 11:18 in the Restoration column); the Apostles' and Nicene Creeds confess it. Under the plainness principle (section 0: "what the Bible states plainly is set down plainly, and is not softened or left out because traditions divide on it"), its absence is a question for the author. The floor cannot add what the Article does not say. Options, ranked:
1. "He was born of the virgin Mary, lived a sinless life." Weakness: an Article-text change, and the floor and its count change with it.
2. Keep the text; the floor stays at "born of Mary," and the apparatus carries the virgin conception as anchor. Weakness: a plain biblical teaching held by nearly every family goes unstated in the Article.
3. Keep the text, and add the virgin conception to the floor's exclusions. Weakness: an exclusion would present as open a teaching the Bible states plainly.

Apparatus error (not a floor matter): s9's panel comment says Alma 7:10 "names Mary by name (a precision the Bible itself does not reach in this register)." Luke 1:27 names her ("the virgin's name was Mary"). Goes into a commentary correction.

### A5-F7. His ministry (s10 second floor, s11)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 4:23, Acts 10:38, Matthew 23:13 and John 13:1 confess: Jesus went about teaching, preaching the gospel of the kingdom, and healing all manner of sickness; He went about doing good, and healing all that were oppressed; He rebuked the scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites; and having loved His own, He loved them unto the end. This is the floor: Jesus taught the Kingdom, healed the sick, rebuked the proud, loved His own to the end, and went about doing good. It does not settle how His miracles are to be understood today, or whether the gifts of healing continue in the Church; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct; "loved without measure" is theological inference from "unto the end."

Why a second floor on s10 (A4(f)): G14 is ratified on s10 and tests only "forgave." The span also says Jesus taught, healed, rebuked and loved; without a second floor those four go unmeasured. Under A4(f) each floor stands verbatim as its own paragraph; the ledger names G14 as paragraph 1 and A5-F7 as paragraph 2. G14 is unchanged.

### A5-F8. Suffered, died and rose (s12, s13, s14)

**Article text change APPROVED by Aaron 27 Oct 2026, 04:42 EDT (AOID2-A05-S14-BODILY, option 1, topic-only).** Live: "and rose again from death in glory on the third day." Approved (Second Edition): "and rose again bodily from death in glory on the third day." The s14 span's own text changes; applied in step 102 behind the Second Edition switch. Luke 24:39 and John 20:27 join s14's biblical column.

> The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 15:3-4, Isaiah 53:5, Philippians 2:8 and Luke 24:39 confess: Christ died for our sins according to the scriptures, and He was buried, and He rose again the third day according to the scriptures; He was wounded for our transgressions, He was bruised for our iniquities; He became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross; and the risen Christ said, handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have. This is the floor: Christ suffered for our sins, died by crucifixion, and rose bodily from the dead on the third day. It does not settle how His suffering and death atone (substitution, ransom, victory, example, or otherwise), where His atoning suffering began (Gethsemane as well as the cross), the shape of the instrument on which He died, or what the glory of His risen body is beyond what Scripture states (1 Corinthians 15:42-44); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Superseded draft (v1, never ratified): "This is the floor: Christ suffered for our sins, died by crucifixion, and rose from the dead on the third day. It does not settle ... or the nature of His risen body."

Kind of witness: direct; "beyond our understanding" and "in glory" are theological inference.

Scoring risks: Jehovah's Witnesses teach that Christ died on a stake, not a cross. Testing each house on its own terms (A4(c)), the floor's claim is death by crucifixion, so the shape is excluded rather than tested. They also teach that Christ was raised as a spirit, not in the body; see the text question below.

Text question (RESOLVED 27 Oct 2026, option 1; kept on record): the Article says "rose again from death in glory on the third day," and no Article says the resurrection was bodily. Luke 24:39 ("handle me, and see; for a spirit hath not flesh and bones, as ye see me have") and John 20:27 state it plainly, and the Restoration canon is emphatic (3 Nephi 11:14-15; D&C 130:22). The draft floor keeps "the nature of His risen body" in its exclusions only because the Article does not claim it. Options, ranked:
1. "and rose again bodily from death in glory on the third day." Weakness: an Article-text change; one family (Jehovah's Witnesses) would likely then differ, which C4 can bear (thirteen of sixteen).
2. Keep the text; the floor stays at "rose from the dead on the third day," and Luke 24:39 is added to s14's anchors. Weakness: as with the virgin birth, a plain teaching goes unstated in the Article.
3. Keep the text and the exclusion as drafted. Weakness: presents as open a teaching the Bible states plainly.

### A5-F9. The will of God made flesh (s15)

> The Article confesses only what John 6:38, Hebrews 10:7 and Philippians 2:8 confess: I came down from heaven, not to do mine own will, but the will of Him that sent me; Lo, I come to do thy will, O God; and He humbled Himself, and became obedient unto death. This is the floor: Christ came to do the will of the Father, and did it perfectly. It does not settle whether Christ has one will or two (divine and human), or how His obedience is related to His divine nature; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct; "the one perfect vessel" is theological inference joined to A5-F6's sinlessness.

Scoring risk: Catholic and Orthodox families confess two wills in Christ (Third Council of Constantinople). "The will of God has been made flesh" could be heard as one will; the floor tests obedience to the Father's will and excludes the number of wills.

### A5-F10. The measure of every disciple (s16)

> The Article confesses only what Ephesians 4:13, 1 Corinthians 11:1 and Hebrews 12:2 confess: we grow unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ; be ye followers of me, even as I also am of Christ; and we look unto Jesus, the author and finisher of our faith. This is the floor: Christ is the measure by whom every disciple and every witness is weighed, and the one all are to follow. It does not settle how Scripture, tradition and living teachers are weighed against one another in practice; each tradition fills that in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for disciples; "every other witness" (prophets and apostles) is theological inference from 1 Corinthians 11:1.
