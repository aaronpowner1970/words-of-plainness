## Floors

### A7-F1. The kingdom promised, and at hand (s1, s2)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 25:34 with 2 Peter 3:13, and Mark 1:14-15 with Luke 17:20-21, confess: the King shall say, Come, ye blessed of my Father, inherit the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world, and we, according to His promise, look for new heavens and a new earth; and Jesus came preaching the gospel of the kingdom of God, saying, The time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God is at hand, for behold, the kingdom of God is within you. This is the floor: the kingdom of God is promised in its fulness at the end, and Christ proclaimed it already at hand. It does not settle how the Kingdom present is related to the Kingdom to come, the order of events at the end, a millennium, or whether "within you" means within each soul or in the midst of the people; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the exclusion "how the Kingdom present is related to the Kingdom to come" is copied word for word from A11-F8, which floors the same Mark 1:15 claim, so the two floors cannot be scored against each other. The future pole stays at the promise, as G10 and G11 hold it.

Scoring risk: some dispensational bodies distinguish "the kingdom of heaven" offered and postponed from the kingdom present now. Dispensationalism is a movement counted within its churches' families (C4(d)); whether any adopted text denies that Christ proclaimed the kingdom at hand is a C4(a)(i) judgment, not a guess.

### A7-F2. Within reach of every soul who will hear (s3)

> The Article confesses only what Mark 4:9, Isaiah 6:10 with Matthew 13:16, and Romans 10:17 confess: he that hath ears to hear, let him hear; make the heart of this people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes, lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed; but blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear; and faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. This is the floor: Christ's call to the kingdom goes out to every soul, and those who open ears, heart and eyes to Him receive it. It does not settle how that opening comes about, whether by the soul's own willing, by God's gift, or by both together, nor whether every soul will receive it; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the call and the faculties (ears, heart, eyes are all in Isaiah 6:10 and Matthew 13:15-16); theological inference for "every soul," drawn from the open form of Christ's invitation.

Candidates considered for this floor (load-bearing: the floor governs every cell of the span), ranked:
1. **As drafted above.** Keeps the Article's "who would open" as the condition and leaves to each house how the opening comes about. Weakness: a reader who hears "who would open" as a claim about free will may feel the exclusion says less than the Article. The Article's words stand unchanged; only the floor declines to settle what they leave open.
2. **The soul's own choice stated as the floor.** Weakness: it makes one answer to how the opening comes about into the floor itself, which A4(g) and AOID2-NO-HOUSE-RULES forbid for an open question. It would likely divide Reformed and Lutheran cells on a point the Article did not set out to decide.
3. **The invitation only** ("Christ calls every soul to hear"), dropping "receive." Weakness: it says less than the span says. That is narrowing to get past cells, which C4(a)(vi) forbids.

Recommended: 1.

Scoring risk: Reformed and Lutheran adopted texts confess the call of the gospel to all (UNVERIFIED; the Canons of Dort II.5 are the likely deciding text) while teaching that the will is made willing by grace. Under the floor as drafted, those texts make the claim; under candidate 2 they might not.

### A7-F3. Entering the kingdom in this life (s4)

> The Article confesses only what John 3:3, 5 and Colossians 1:13 confess: except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God, and except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God; and God hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of His dear Son. This is the floor: Christ calls us to enter the kingdom in this mortal life by being born again, and those who enter are brought into it now. It does not settle what "water" signifies, when in this life the new birth takes place, or how covenants with Christ are entered; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the exclusion follows A11-F7 (John 3:5) clause for clause, so that the two floors on the same verse leave the same questions open.

Note: Matthew 11:12 stays in the biblical column, but the floor does not rest on it. Its sense ("suffereth violence ... the violent take it by force") is disputed across traditions, and Colossians 1:13 makes the present-entry claim plainly.

### A7-F4. Eternal life: knowing God, begun now (s5, s6)

> The Article confesses only what John 17:3, John 5:24 with 1 John 5:11-12, and 1 John 1:3 confess: this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent; he that heareth my word, and believeth on him that sent me, hath everlasting life, and is passed from death unto life, and God hath given to us eternal life, and this life is in His Son; and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with His Son Jesus Christ. This is the floor: eternal life is knowing God and Jesus Christ, in fellowship with the Father and the Son; it is given in this life to those who come to Him, and it can continue without end. It does not settle what coming to Him consists of, what eternal life will be in its fulness in the world to come, or whether one who has received it can finally fall away; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: "whether ... can finally fall away" follows A11-F8's exclusion. The Article's own word, "can continue," neither promises nor denies that it will; the floor keeps that as written.

### A7-F5. Rest, joy and peace before death, perfection and glory (s7, s8, s9)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 11:28-29, John 15:11 and John 14:27 confess: come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest, and ye shall find rest unto your souls; these things have I spoken unto you, that my joy might remain in you, and that your joy might be full; peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you. This is the floor: Christ gives His rest, His joy and His peace to His disciples in this life, while they are still being made perfect and before they are glorified. It does not settle how the rest given now is related to the rest that remains for the people of God, whether or when a disciple is made perfect in this life, or how fully these gifts are known before glory; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct that the gifts are given in this life (the words were spoken to living disciples, on the night one of them would deny Him); theological inference for "before perfection" and "before glory," drawn from who received the promises.

Scoring risk: Methodist and Holiness families teach entire sanctification in this life (UNVERIFIED as to wording). The floor leaves "whether or when a disciple is made perfect" open, so their texts are not read against it.

### A7-F6. The Spirit's present work: witness, healing, renewal (s10)

> The Article confesses only what Luke 4:18, Romans 8:16 and 2 Corinthians 4:16 confess: the Spirit of the Lord is upon Christ, who was sent to heal the brokenhearted; the Spirit itself beareth witness with our spirit, that we are the children of God; and though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day. This is the floor: the Spirit works in disciples now, bearing witness within them, healing and renewing them, and they may know His working in their lives. It does not settle how His working is known (by feeling, through the Word, through the sacraments, or otherwise), whether that witness gives assurance, or the manner of the Spirit's indwelling; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct (Romans 8:16, 2 Corinthians 4:16, Luke 4:18). Acts 10:38 stays in the column as illustrative application: it describes Christ's own anointing and His healing of the oppressed, which the span applies to the Spirit's work in disciples.

Anchor addition proposed: **Luke 4:18** to s10's biblical column. Without it, "healing ... of our souls" rests only on Acts 10:38, which is about the bodies of the oppressed in Christ's ministry. This follows the A3-F5 precedent of adding anchors at Pass 0.

Relation to Article 9: R5 (Sanctifier) floors sanctification; R4 the Spirit's witness of Christ. s10 claims something neither states: that the Spirit's work is present and perceived now. It gets its own floor, with R2's exclusion ("the manner of the Spirit's indwelling") copied.

### A7-F7. The kingdom come to those who receive it, and its fruit as witness (s11)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 14:17, Mark 10:15 and Galatians 5:22-23 confess: the kingdom of God is not meat and drink, but righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost; whosoever shall not receive the kingdom of God as a little child, he shall not enter therein; and the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness, temperance. This is the floor: the kingdom has already come to those who receive it, and the Spirit's fruit in them is its witness. It does not settle how the Kingdom present is related to the Kingdom to come, how the kingdom is received, or what weight fruit carries as assurance; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct (Romans 14:17 describes the kingdom as the Spirit's righteousness, peace and joy); theological inference for "witness," drawn from that description. Matthew 7:16, 20 stays in the column as illustrative application; it is spoken of false prophets, and the span applies its rule to the kingdom.

Anchor addition proposed: **Romans 14:17** to s11's biblical column. It is now anchored only at s13; it is the plainest biblical statement of s11's claim. Repetition across spans is legitimate when the verse applies meaningfully to each use (editorial principle).

### A7-F8. Seeking to build the kingdom (s12)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 6:10, Colossians 4:11 and 1 Corinthians 3:9 confess: thy kingdom come, thy will be done in earth, as it is in heaven; these only are my fellowworkers unto the kingdom of God; for we are labourers together with God. This is the floor: those who receive the kingdom pray for its coming and labour together with God for it. It does not settle whether the kingdom grows by human labour or by God alone working through His servants, what form that labour takes, or how it is related to the Church; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Anchor addition proposed: **Colossians 4:11** to s12's biblical column. It is the only verse that joins the disciple's labour to "the kingdom of God" in so many words; 1 Corinthians 3:9 speaks of God's building, which is the church.

Scoring risk: Lutheran and Reformed texts guard against any claim that human beings build the kingdom (UNVERIFIED). The Article says "seek to build," and the floor reads it at Colossians 4:11's level, fellow workers unto the kingdom, and leaves who builds to each house. This is the span's own claim read with its anchors, not a narrowing: the panel comment already reads "seek to build" as participation, not construction.

### A7-F9. The Father's will: to live, not merely endure (s13, s14)

> The Article confesses only what John 10:10, John 6:40 and Deuteronomy 30:19-20 confess: I am come that they might have life, and that they might have it more abundantly; this is the will of Him that sent me, that every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on Him, may have everlasting life; and I have set before you life and death, therefore choose life, for He is thy life. This is the floor: the Father's will for His children in mortal life is life in Christ, abundant and begun now, not mere endurance while they wait for Him. It does not settle what abundant life brings in outward circumstance, or how the waiting for His coming is to be kept; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the Father's will that we have life (John 10:10, John 6:40). The negation in s14 ("never ... merely endure") is theological inference, supported as illustrative application by John 9:4 and the parable of the talents (Matthew 25:14-30).

Note: "what abundant life brings in outward circumstance" keeps the floor from being read as a promise of prosperity, which nothing in the span or its anchors claims.

### Panel comment (recommended)

The Article confesses only what Mark 4:9, Isaiah 6:10 with Matthew 13:16, and Romans 10:17 confess: he that hath ears to hear, let him hear; make the heart of this people fat, and make their ears heavy, and shut their eyes, lest they see with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their heart, and convert, and be healed; but blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear; and faith cometh by hearing, and hearing by the word of God. This is the floor: Christ's call to the kingdom goes out to every soul, and those who open ears, heart and eyes to Him receive it. It does not settle how that opening comes about, whether by the soul's own willing, by God's gift, or by both together, nor whether every soul will receive it; each tradition fills those in its own house.

The three faculties are Scripture's own. Christ's invitation "he that hath ears to hear, let him hear" (Matthew 11:15; 13:9; Mark 4:9) names the ears; His blessing on the disciples names their eyes and ears (Matthew 13:16); and the Isaiah passage He quotes to explain His parables names all three, eyes, ears and heart, closed against understanding (Isaiah 6:9-10; Matthew 13:14-15). "To feel" is the Article's own word for the heart's part. Romans 10:17 adds that faith itself comes by hearing the word of God.

What the Article leaves open is named here and not answered: how a soul's ears, heart and eyes come to be opened, and by what means the kingdom is received, which Article 11 takes up. What it does not leave open is Christ's call, which stands as He spoke it: he that hath ears to hear, let him hear. Every church and every believer, our own included, is asked to examine their own doctrine, pray sincerely for wisdom, and follow the Spirit of God.

### Biblical anchors (recommended comments)

- **Matthew 13:16.** Direct witness. Christ blesses the disciples whose eyes see and ears hear, the open faculties set against the closed ones of Isaiah 6, which He has just quoted (Matthew 13:14-15).
- **Isaiah 6:9-10.** Direct witness. The prophet names all three faculties, eyes, ears and heart, in the passage Christ quotes when He explains why He teaches in parables. The heart in the Article's triad is taken from here.
- **Mark 4:9.** Direct witness. Christ's repeated invitation to hear, given to the whole crowd, not to a chosen few; it is the plainest form of the call the span says goes out to every soul.
- **Romans 10:17.** Direct witness that faith comes by hearing the word of God; theological inference when read as hearing being the way the kingdom is entered.

### Restoration anchors (recommended comments)

- **D&C 84:46-47.** Direct witness, in the Restoration's own words, that the Spirit gives light to every person who comes into the world, and that whoever hearkens to it comes to the Father. It is that tradition's ground for "every soul."
- **Alma 32:27-28.** Theological inference, in the Restoration's own words: Alma's call to awake and arouse the faculties and give place for the word reads as the soul's opening the span describes. (Also used at A3 s11, verse 27 alone, for the soul's giving place for faith.)
