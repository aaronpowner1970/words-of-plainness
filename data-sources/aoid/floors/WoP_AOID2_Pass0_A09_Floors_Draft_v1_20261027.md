## Floors

### A9-F5. Christ the fixed way to God (s1, s2, s5)

> The Article confesses only what John 14:6, Acts 4:12, Hebrews 12:2 and Revelation 22:16 confess: I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me; neither is there salvation in any other; looking unto Jesus the author and finisher of our faith; I am the bright and morning star. This is the floor: Jesus Christ is the way to the Father, the one to whom every traveler looks, and by Him every soul may find the way to God. It does not settle for whom Christ died in the sense the traditions dispute, the state of those who never heard the gospel in this life, whether the gospel is preached to the dead, or the place of the Church in salvation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the way and the one name (John 14:6; Acts 4:12); illustrative application for the star (Revelation 22:16), which the Article draws out as the North Star.

Precedent: the exclusions are copied from A6-F1 (John 14:6 and Acts 4:12 floored there) and G13 ("for whom Christ died in the sense the traditions dispute"), because "every soul may find the way" touches the extent question.

### A9-F6. The same yesterday, to day and for ever (s3, s4)

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 13:8, Matthew 24:35 and Galatians 1:8 confess: Jesus Christ the same yesterday, and to day, and for ever; heaven and earth shall pass away, but my words shall not pass away; though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed. This is the floor: Christ does not change, His words stand, and no age or claim of men alters what He taught or who He is. It does not settle how His unchanging nature is related to His taking flesh, whether and how the Church's understanding of His teaching develops, or whether God's speaking today includes new prophecy or revelation carrying doctrinal authority; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the last exclusion follows A4-F3 word for word ("new prophecy or revelation carrying doctrinal authority"), so "what He taught is what He taught" cannot be scored against continuing revelation, and the development clause keeps Catholic teaching on the development of doctrine from being read as a claim that Christ changed.

### A9-F7. The Spirit's witness in every honest conscience (s10)

> The Article confesses only what John 1:9, Romans 2:14-15 and John 16:13 confess: that was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world; the Gentiles which have not the law show the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness; and the Spirit of truth will guide you into all truth. This is the floor: God gives a light within to every soul, by which the honest conscience is drawn toward what is true and warned away from what would destroy it. It does not settle what that light is, whether the Holy Spirit Himself, the Light of Christ, grace that goes before conversion, or the conscience God gives at creation; how it is related to the Spirit given to disciples; or whether it can bring a soul to salvation apart from the gospel; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the light given to every man (John 1:9) and the witness of conscience (Romans 2:15); theological inference joining these to the Spirit of truth (John 16:13), which the Article names as "the Spirit of God."

Anchor addition proposed: Romans 2:14-15 at s10. It is the Bible's plainest statement of conscience as an inward witness given to all; the span now rests on John 1:9 and John 16:13 alone.

Candidates (judgment call 1; load-bearing), ranked:
1. **As drafted.** Keeps "given to each honest conscience" and leaves what the light is to each house. The live panel comment already says the span "confesses the influence and leaves its manner open." Weakness: some readers will hear "the Spirit of God" in the Article as the Holy Spirit Himself and find the floor more reserved than the text; the text is unchanged.
2. **The floor names the Holy Spirit as the light in every conscience.** Weakness: makes one house's answer the floor (A4(g)); Reformed (common grace), Catholic (natural law) and Latter-day Saint (Light of Christ, distinct from the gift of the Holy Ghost) texts may each read differently.
3. **The floor limits the witness to disciples** (John 16:13 only). Weakness: drops "each honest conscience." Narrowing, C4(a)(vi).

Recommended: 1.

### A9-F8. The inward witness tried by Christ (s11, s12, s13)

> The Article confesses only what Jeremiah 17:9, 1 John 4:1-2 and Galatians 1:8 confess: the heart is deceitful above all things: who can know it?; believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God, for every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God; and though an angel from heaven preach any other gospel, let him be accursed. This is the floor: our own hearts can mislead us, so every inward prompting is tried by Christ, by what He taught and who He is, and a prompting that leads away from Him is not of God. It does not settle by whom the trial is made, whether the believer, the Church, Scripture or a teaching office; whether Scripture is the only infallible rule; or how Christ's teaching is known; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the deceitful heart and the command to try the spirits; theological inference for "wounds, fears, desires" as the forms the heart's pull takes.

Precedent: "whether Scripture is the only infallible rule" is copied from A4-F5, which floors the weighing of every word against the law and the testimony.

Judgment call 2 (s11's class), ranked:
1. **Teaching, joined to s12 and s13 (recommended).** s11 is the premise of a commanded test: the heart can mislead (Jeremiah 17:9), therefore try the spirits (1 John 4:1). Weakness: its list (wounds, fears, desires, the pull of those we love and resent) is plain observation, which AOID2-SPAN-CLASS-OBSERVATION would call descriptive on its own.
2. **Descriptive** (observation of the human condition, as A3 s14 was ruled). Weakness: leaves unmeasured the claim that the inward witness can be misread, which is Scripture's teaching, not only observation.
3. **Teaching, its own floor at Jeremiah 17:9 alone.** Weakness: a floor on total depravity would invite one house's anthropology (Reformed) into a span that claims only that the heart can mislead.

### A9-F9. The Scriptures, a sure word through which the Spirit spoke (s14, s16)

> The Article confesses only what 2 Peter 1:19, 21, Psalm 119:105, 2 Timothy 3:16 and John 5:39 confess: we have also a more sure word of prophecy, whereunto ye do well that ye take heed, as unto a light that shineth in a dark place; holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost; thy word is a lamp unto my feet, and a light unto my path; all scripture is given by inspiration of God; and they are they which testify of me. This is the floor: the Scriptures are a sure word and a light for the way, the testimony of those through whom the Spirit spoke, and they testify of Christ. It does not settle inerrancy, the history of the text, which books make up the canon, the relation of the written word to Christ the Word, or the place of tradition; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the exclusions are A4-F6's ("inerrancy, the history of the text ... which books make up the canon") and A4-F7's ("the relation of the written word to Christ the Word, or the place of tradition"), which floor the same verses' claims.

Anchor addition proposed: John 5:39 at s14, for "pointing always to the same North Star." It is the plainest verse that the Scriptures testify of Christ, and it is not now anchored in Article 9.

### A9-F10. Word and Spirit together, under Christ (s17, s20)

> The Article confesses only what Acts 17:11, John 5:39-40, John 16:13-14 and Romans 8:14 confess: they received the word with all readiness of mind, and searched the scriptures daily, whether those things were so; search the scriptures, for they testify of me, and ye will not come to me, that ye might have life; the Spirit of truth will guide you into all truth, and he shall glorify me; and as many as are led by the Spirit of God, they are the sons of God. This is the floor: the disciple is led neither by inward prompting alone nor by the written word alone, but by Christ, through His Spirit and the Scriptures together, and the Spirit leads to Christ and never away from Him. It does not settle whether Scripture is the only infallible rule, the authority of tradition or a teaching office, or how what the Spirit speaks is related to Scripture; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for each part (the Bereans' searching; the Scriptures pointing to Christ and not replacing Him, John 5:40; the Spirit glorifying Christ); theological inference for joining them as the disciple's way.

Precedent: exclusions from A4-F5 and A4-F3 ("how what the Spirit speaks is related to Scripture"). A6-F9 floors a neighbouring claim (the Scriptures testify of Christ and the Spirit guides into truth) and is consistent with this floor.

Scoring risk: "not by written word alone" may be heard as a denial of sola scriptura. It is not: the floor's contrast is between the written word without Christ and His Spirit, and the word read with them (Westminster 1.6 confesses the inward illumination of the Spirit as necessary to saving understanding of the word; UNVERIFIED as to that cell's deciding text). The exclusion keeps the rule-of-faith question out.

Anchor addition proposed: John 16:14 at s20 ("He shall glorify me"), for "leads us toward Him and never away."

### A9-F11. The way back for the lost (s18)

> The Article confesses only what Psalm 23:3 and Luke 15:4-6 confess: he restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake; and what man of you, having an hundred sheep, if he lose one of them, doth not go after that which is lost, until he find it? This is the floor: the Lord seeks those who are lost and restores those who turn to Him, and the disciple who strays is never without the way back. It does not settle whether a true believer can finally fall away, or how one who has strayed is restored, whether by repentance alone, by a sacrament or ordinance of reconciliation, or otherwise; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: "whether a true believer can finally fall away" from A11-F8; the restoration clause follows the exclusion of the Stage 1 floor on sin after conversion ("by confession to God, a sacrament of penance or reconciliation, or otherwise").

### A9-F12. No approval required beyond Christ's (s19)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 14:4, James 4:12 and 1 Corinthians 4:3-4 confess: who art thou that judgest another man's servant? to his own master he standeth or falleth; there is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy; with me it is a very small thing that I should be judged of you, or of man's judgment, but he that judgeth me is the Lord. This is the floor: the disciple's walk answers to Christ, and requires no approval beyond His, for He alone is the righteous Judge of souls. It does not settle whether and how Christ gives His approval through the ministry and ordinances of His Church, the discipline a church exercises over its own members, or assurance of one's own salvation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the last two exclusions are copied from A6-F8, which floors Romans 14:4 and James 4:12 as "the final judgment of every soul is Christ's."

Anchor addition proposed: 1 Corinthians 4:3-4 at s19. It is the one verse that speaks of human judgment of the disciple, not only of final judgment.

Candidates (judgment call 4; load-bearing, and the Article line most likely to divide), ranked:
1. **As drafted (recommended).** The Article says no approval is required "beyond the approval of Christ Himself." A church that holds Christ approves through its ministry and ordinances does not, by that alone, require approval beyond His; so the floor leaves that open, as the Article's word "beyond" does. Weakness: the scorer must hold a line. A church whose adopted text makes its own approval a requirement in its own right, apart from Christ's, DIFFERS; one that teaches Christ acts through it does not. That line will be tested hardest where a church's authority to bind and loose, admit to ordinances or judge worthiness is confessed (UNVERIFIED: Catholicism, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, Latter-day Saints, Jehovah's Witnesses). Our own house is among them; check it hardest.
2. **The floor rules mediation out** ("no church's approval stands between the disciple and Christ"). Weakness: makes one house's ecclesiology the floor (A4(g)), and says more than "beyond" says.
3. **Joined to A6-F8** (final judgment only). Weakness: says less than the span, which speaks of the disciple's walk now, not only the last judgment. Narrowing, C4(a)(vi).

If the count falls short through DIFFERS, the span stands as declared witness with its division disclosed (section 0, integrity).

### A9-F13. The Father guides the sincere; the Son leads no one astray (s21, s22)

> The Article confesses only what Jeremiah 29:13, 1 Samuel 16:7 and John 10:27-28 confess: ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart; the LORD seeth not as man seeth, but the LORD looketh on the heart; and my sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me, and I give unto them eternal life. This is the floor: the Father, who knows the heart, guides every soul who seeks Him in sincerity, and Christ leads truly all who follow Him. It does not settle the manner of God's guidance, the state of those who seek Him but never hear the gospel in this life, or whether a true believer can finally fall away; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for seeking and finding, and for the Shepherd's sheep following Him; theological inference for "never led any honest disciple astray," drawn from the Good Shepherd's leading.

Precedent: "the manner of God's guidance" from A9-F4; "the state of those who never heard the gospel in this life" from A6-F1; "whether a true believer can finally fall away" from A11-F8, needed because John 10:28 continues "they shall never perish."

## Judgment call 3: s15, "They experienced their own wrestles with God"

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Descriptive (recommended).** The span reports what the Scriptures themselves record of their witnesses: Jacob wrestled (Genesis 32:24-28), Jeremiah complained (Jeremiah 20:7-9), the psalmist cried "why hast thou forsaken me?" (Psalm 22:1). No church confesses or denies that record as a doctrine. Weakness: the span stands between s14 and s16, both teaching, and a reader may feel the Article's thought about scripture's human witnesses goes unmeasured; its nearest teaching (God's servants are frail and the power is His) is measured at A4-F4, and the vessel's imperfection at A5-F4b.
2. **Teaching, joined to A4-F4.** Weakness: A4-F4 floors frailty and God's power; s15 claims neither in so many words, so the join would score claims the span does not make.
3. **Teaching, new floor** ("those through whom God spoke wrestled with Him"). Weakness: few adopted texts speak of the prophets' wrestling as a class; A4-F4's first form fell short through silence on just such a claim (taught 6, no position 6). It would be a floor built to go unconfessed.
