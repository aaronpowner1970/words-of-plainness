## Floors

### A8-F1. Received as brothers and sisters, children of the one Father (s1, s2, s3)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 15:7, Galatians 3:26, Matthew 12:50 and Ephesians 4:6 confess: receive ye one another, as Christ also received us to the glory of God; for ye are all the children of God by faith in Christ Jesus; whosoever shall do the will of my Father which is in heaven, the same is my brother, and sister; one God and Father of all. This is the floor: disciples receive every soul who comes to Christ as a brother or a sister, children of the one Father. It does not settle what coming to Christ consists of, how disciples become children of God, whether by creation, adoption, new birth or spirit birth, the place of baptism in it, or who may share a church's sacraments or membership; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct (Romans 15:7 commands the receiving; Galatians 3:26 and Matthew 12:50 name the kinship).

Precedent: "how disciples become children of God, whether by creation, adoption, new birth or spirit birth" is copied word for word from G15 (s19), so the two floors on the Father's children cannot be scored against each other.

Anchor addition proposed: Romans 15:7 at s1. It is the one verse that commands what s1 does ("receive ye one another, as Christ also received us"); the present anchors name the kinship but not the receiving.

Scoring risk: the exclusion on sacraments and membership is load-bearing. Without it, closed-communion families (Catholicism, Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, confessional Lutheran bodies) would be read against s1 on a point the span does not make.

### A8-F2. Love and honor that do not wait on return (s4, s5)

> The Article confesses only what Luke 6:32, 1 John 4:19, Romans 12:10 and 1 Peter 2:17 confess: for if ye love them which love you, what thank have ye? for sinners also love those that love them; we love him, because he first loved us; in honour preferring one another; honour all men. This is the floor: the disciple's love and honor toward others do not wait on being loved or honored in return, for God loved us first.

Kind of witness: direct for love (Luke 6:32); theological inference for honor "whether they honor us or not," drawn from "honour all men" and "in honour preferring one another."

Not joined to L3 (love of enemies): L3 claims more than these spans (enemies, persecutors, overcoming evil), and joining would score s4 and s5 on claims they do not make. No exclusion is needed; nothing here bears on a question the traditions divide on.

### A8-F3. One body, larger than any denomination and older than every division (s7)

> The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 12:12-13, 27, Ephesians 4:4-6 and Hebrews 12:22-23 confess: as the body is one, and hath many members, so also is Christ, for by one Spirit are we all baptized into one body; now ye are the body of Christ; there is one body, and one Spirit, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all; and ye are come unto the general assembly and church of the firstborn, which are written in heaven. This is the floor: the body of Christ is one, made one by His Spirit; it is not bounded by any one denomination, and it was before every division among Christians. It does not settle where the Church subsists or is found in its fulness, whether one visible communion is the Church, how those outside it are related to it, the bounds of the Church, or what visible unity among Christians requires; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the one body made one by the Spirit; theological inference for "not bounded by any one denomination" (drawn from entry by one Spirit and from "written in heaven") and for "before every division" (the body confessed in 1 Corinthians and Ephesians predates every later division).

Precedent: "the bounds of the Church, or what visible unity among Christians requires" is copied from L11.

Candidates (judgment call 2; load-bearing, the floor governs every cell of the span), ranked:
1. **As drafted.** Keeps "larger than any single denomination" as "not bounded by any one denomination," and leaves to each house where the Church subsists. Weakness: this is the claim most likely to DIFFER. Families whose adopted texts identify the Church with one visible communion may contradict it outright (UNVERIFIED: Eastern Orthodox, Jehovah's Witnesses; the Latter-day Saint cell must weigh D&C 1:30 against D&C 10:67-68, which is already an anchor at this span; the Catholic cell must read Lumen Gentium 8 with Unitatis Redintegratio 3). A shortfall through DIFFERS makes the span declared witness, with its division disclosed.
2. **Drop "not bounded by any one denomination"**, keeping only the one body and its priority to division. Weakness: says less than the span. Narrowing to get past cells, which C4(a)(vi) forbids.
3. **Posture, under the A8 conviction.** Weakness: the span stands under "We believe" and makes a claim about Christ's body, not about the ministry's method; calling it posture would leave the Article's central claim unmeasured while appearing settled.

Recommended: 1. If it falls short, integrity (section 0) requires the dagger, not a rewording.

### A8-F4. The walls are not His work (s9)

> The Article confesses only what John 17:11, 1 Corinthians 1:10, 13 and Ephesians 2:14 confess: Holy Father, keep through thine own name those whom thou hast given me, that they may be one, as we are; I beseech you, brethren, that there be no divisions among you; is Christ divided?; for he is our peace, who hath made both one, and hath broken down the middle wall of partition between us. This is the floor: Christ prays that His people be one and breaks down the walls between them, and the divisions that estrange His people from one another are not His work. It does not settle whether any separation was right or required, which body has kept the truth, the bounds of the Church, or what visible unity among Christians requires; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for John 17:11 and 1 Corinthians 1:10, 13; illustrative application for Ephesians 2:14, whose wall is between Jew and Gentile.

Candidates (judgment call 3), ranked:
1. **Teaching, as drafted.** Weakness: some adopted texts hold that separation from error was commanded (UNVERIFIED: Reformed and Lutheran confessions on separation from Rome; the Latter-day Saint teaching of apostasy and restoration). The exclusion "whether any separation was right or required" is what keeps those texts from making the cell DIFFER; the scorer must hold the line between "the estrangement is not His work" and "every separation was wrong."
2. **Posture, joined to the A8 conviction.** Weakness: AOID2-A8-CONVICTION-FORM names the refusal to defend the walls (s10), not the claim about who built them; s9 says something about Christ.
3. **Floor at Christ's prayer only** ("Christ prays that His people be one"). Weakness: drops "not built by Him." Narrowing, C4(a)(vi).

Recommended: 1.

### A8-F5. Not returning evil for evil (s13)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 12:17, 21, 1 Peter 3:9 and 1 Corinthians 13:4-5 confess: recompense to no man evil for evil; be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good; not rendering evil for evil, or railing for railing: but contrariwise blessing; charity suffereth long, and is kind, and thinketh no evil. This is the floor: disciples do not return insult for insult, exclusion for exclusion or suspicion for suspicion, but answer evil with blessing and think no evil of others. It does not settle whether or how a church disciplines its own members, whether a disciple may bear arms, the use of force by civil authority, or questions of self-defense; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for insult (railing for railing); theological inference for exclusion and suspicion, drawn from "evil for evil" and "thinketh no evil."

Precedent: "whether a disciple may bear arms, the use of force by civil authority, or questions of self-defense" is copied from L3, which floors Romans 12:17-21; Matthew 5:38-42 is anchored here too.

The new exclusion, church discipline, is load-bearing: every family practices some discipline (Matthew 18:17; 1 Corinthians 5), and "exclusion for exclusion" must not be read as forbidding it.

Anchor addition proposed: 1 Corinthians 13:4-7 at s13. "Thinketh no evil" is the plainest biblical warrant for "suspicion for suspicion"; nothing at the span carries it now.

### A8-F6. The fellowship He purchased with His blood (s16)

> The Article confesses only what Acts 20:28, 1 Peter 1:18-19 and Revelation 5:9 confess: the church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood; ye were not redeemed with corruptible things, as silver and gold, but with the precious blood of Christ; thou wast slain, and hast redeemed us to God by thy blood out of every kindred, and tongue, and people, and nation. This is the floor: the fellowship of Christ's people is His Church, purchased with His own blood and gathered out of every nation. It does not settle any one account of how the Atonement saves, the extent of the atonement, or where the Church is found; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: "any one account of how the Atonement saves ... the extent of the atonement" is copied from A6-F3. The extent clause matters here: Revelation 5:9 ("redeemed us ... out of every kindred") is read by Reformed confessions for particular redemption, and the floor must not take a side.

### A8-F7. Free, plain and without price (s17)

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 55:1, Romans 3:24, Ephesians 2:8 and 2 Corinthians 3:12 confess: come, buy wine and milk without money and without price; being justified freely by his grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus; by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves: it is the gift of God; we use great plainness of speech. This is the floor: what Christ offers is His free gift, given without price and set forth plainly. It does not settle any one account of how the Atonement saves, whether righteousness is imputed or imparted, the extent of the atonement, or the place of works; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for "free" and "without price" (Romans 3:24; Ephesians 2:8); illustrative application for Isaiah 55:1, an invitation to Israel applied to the gospel; direct for "plain" in Paul's manner of preaching (2 Corinthians 3:12).

Precedent: the exclusion is A6-F3's, with "the place of works" from A6-F7, since "without price" touches the grace-and-works question that A6-F7 already leaves to each house.

Anchor addition proposed: 2 Corinthians 3:12 at s17. "Plain" now rests only on 2 Nephi 31:3 in the Restoration column, and the floor needs a biblical witness for it.

## Posture spans: the judgment calls

### Judgment call 1: s6

"We hold them as family whether or not their tradition recognizes ours, and whether or not ours has treated them rightly."

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Posture (recommended).** "Hold them as family" restates s1, which A8-F1 floors; what s6 adds is the ministry's resolve that recognition between communions, and the wrongs of the past, will not set the measure of its fellowship. No command sets that measure; it is how this ministry chooses to hold others. The one commanded element, confessing our own wrongs, is floored already at G14 (1 John 1:9). Weakness: a reader may feel the span's second clause is plain teaching left unmeasured here; it is measured, at G14.
2. **Teaching, new floor** ("brotherhood in Christ rests on Christ, not on recognition between communions; disciples confess their own wrongs toward others," Daniel 9:5, 1 John 1:9). Weakness: the first clause has no plain command behind it, and it would likely be scored as a claim about mutual recognition of baptism or membership, which the Article does not make.
3. **Teaching, joined to A8-F1.** Weakness: s6 says more than A8-F1 (its two conditions), so joining would show the conditions as measured when they are not.

### s8, s10, s11, s12 (no judgment call needed)

- **s8** "We are not indifferent to Christian institutions or traditions": the ministry's own stance, like the Prologue's s2 ("we neither reject nor disrespect religious institutions"), which was ruled posture.
- **s10** "We refuse to defend them": ruled the ministry's own conviction by AOID2-A8-CONVICTION-FORM and declared as such in "How we hold this." Not measured, by that ruling.
- **s11** "We will neither allow nor defend the use of any doctrine or teaching as a weapon ...": the Second Edition places it "within the sphere of this interfaith ministry"; it extends s10's conviction to the ministry's gatherings. Teaching close to it is measured elsewhere: A8-F5 (s13) and L3. If Aaron reads s11 as obedience to 2 Timothy 2:14 rather than as the ministry's rule, it would take a floor of its own; I do not recommend that, because "allow" governs the ministry's own space.
- **s12** "who names His name": the one boundary, posture as the Prologue's boundary sentence was ruled; R4 carries 1 Corinthians 12:3.

### Judgment call 4: s14 and s15

"We will sit at the table with every soul who has found Christ, and we will leave the door open to every soul still searching for Him."

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Both posture (recommended).** In the Second Edition the next words are "But hospitality does not require us to permit hostility. We welcome respectful disagreement while protecting the dignity of every participant," with a footnote to the Fellowship Agreement. The table and the door are the ministry's own gatherings. Weakness: s15's ground (God is not willing that any should perish, 2 Peter 3:9) is plain teaching; it is a reason the span gives, not what the span confesses, and it is anchored, not floored, here.
2. **Teaching, joined to L6 (hospitality).** Weakness: L6 says less than the spans; and "table" would invite the scorer to read s14 as sacramental intercommunion, where most families DIFFER, measuring a claim the Article does not make.
3. **Teaching, new floor** (Romans 15:7 with 2 Peter 3:9). Weakness: duplicates A8-F1 for s14, and floors as the span's claim what the span gives as its reason.
