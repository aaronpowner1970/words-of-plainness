## Article 2, Of God

### A2-F1. The Father of our spirits (s11, s1): carried over, already in floor form

A2 s1's panel comment is already written as a floor ("The Article confesses only what Hebrews 12:9 confesses ..."), and it was the model for the 22 Sep floors. It is proposed for ratification as it stands, with s11 ("believe in God") joined to it. No new wording.

### A2-F2. God is good, His purposes are good, and His love for His children has no limit (s12, s2 *(empty)*): AMENDED AND RATIFIED by Aaron 3 Oct 2026

> The Article confesses only what Psalm 145:9, Romans 8:28 and Romans 8:38-39 confess: the LORD is good to all, and His tender mercies are over all His works; all things work together for good to them that love God; and nothing shall be able to separate His children from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus. This is the floor: God is good, His purposes toward His children are good, and nothing can separate them from His love. It does not settle the extent of the atonement, whether all will finally be saved, how God's love is related to His judgment, how His purposes bear on human freedom and suffering, or who is meant by "His children" (see A11-F1); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Why it was amended (3 Oct 2026): s12 reads "whose purposes are good". The 2 Oct floor tested Psalm 145:9, James 1:17 and Romans 8:38-39, and never stated the Article's claim that God's purposes are good. Romans 8:28 replaces James 1:17 in the floor, and James 1:17 returns to the anchors. The floor asks no more than the Article says.

Scoring risk (carried): Reformed bodies confess God's goodness to all and a particular, saving love for the elect. "Without limit" is read as nothing can separate His children from it, not as everyone is saved.

Superseded wording (ratified 2 Oct 2026, kept on record):
> The Article confesses only what Psalm 145:9, James 1:17 and Romans 8:38-39 confess: the LORD is good to all, every good and perfect gift comes down from the Father of lights, and nothing in all creation shall be able to separate His children from the love of God which is in Christ Jesus. This is the floor. It does not settle the extent of the atonement, whether all will finally be saved, how God's love is related to His judgment, or who is meant by "His children" (see A11-F1); each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A2-F3. Providence: He sustains, He knows, He cares (s3, s4, s5, all *(empty)*)

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 1:3, Hebrews 4:13 and Matthew 10:29-30 with 1 Peter 5:7 confess: God upholds all things by the word of His power, all things are naked and opened unto His eyes, not a sparrow falls without the Father, and He careth for us. This is the floor. It does not settle the manner of God's foreknowledge, whether He stands outside time, predestination, or how His providence bears on human freedom; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A2-F4. God in the Highest (s6 *(empty)*)

> The Article confesses only what Luke 2:14 confesses: glory to God in the highest. God is the Most High, above all. This is the floor. It does not settle the relation of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, or any account of God's being beyond His supremacy; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A2-F5. What no mortal knows, and what He reveals (s8 *(empty)*, s9, s10 *(empty)*): AMENDED AND RATIFIED by Aaron 3 Oct 2026

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 55:8-9, Romans 11:33, Deuteronomy 29:29 and 1 Corinthians 13:9, 12 confess: God's ways and thoughts are higher than ours, His judgments unsearchable; the secret things belong unto the LORD, and those things which are revealed belong unto us; and now we know in part and see through a glass, darkly. This is the floor: no one in mortality knows the full nature of God, and we know Him as far as He reveals Himself. It does not settle whether God will remain beyond the full knowledge of every creature forever, or whether His children will come to know Him fully in the life to come; nor how far God may be known by nature or by grace; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Why it was amended (Aaron, 3 Oct 2026): s8 reads "no mortal human can claim to know the full nature of God." The 2 Oct floor tested only God's ways and judgments, not the Article's claim about His nature. Aaron distinguished the two readings: "God's being is incomprehensible" (classical) is a permanent division between Creator and created, while "we don't know His full nature" is temporary, holding out hope that knowing Him is a process longer than a mortal life, continuing into the eternities. The floor holds the shared part, which is that no one in mortality knows His full nature. The permanent-or-temporary question goes in the exclusions. Aaron's fuller hope is amplifying witness (1 Corinthians 13:12; 1 John 3:2; John 17:3) and is stated plainly in Volume 2. It is not scored here.

Superseded wording (ratified 2 Oct 2026, kept on record): "The Article confesses only what Isaiah 55:8-9, Romans 11:33 and Deuteronomy 29:29 confess: God's ways and thoughts are higher than ours, His judgments unsearchable and His ways past finding out; the secret things belong unto the LORD, and those things which are revealed belong unto us. This is the floor. It does not settle how far God may be known by nature or by grace, whether His self-revelation is complete, or the place of negative and positive speech about God; each tradition fills those in its own house."

### A2 s7: a posture, not a teaching. Proposed: not scored

"Of the eternal functions and hierarchy of heavenly populations we remain carefully neutral ..." states how the ministry speaks, not what any church teaches. C4 asks whether a family teaches a line, and a restraint has no teaching to test. The parallel is Article 8: this line is declared as the ministry's own method, and the Article names it as such. The honest cost is that the Trinitarian families confess the Trinity as necessary teaching, and this line does not ask them to set that aside. It only keeps the Article from deciding it. The June study rated this an author's question with low felt urgency. **CONFIRMED by Aaron 2 Oct 2026: the ministry's posture, not scored.**

## Article 4, Of God's Word

### A4-F1. Prophets, men and women, whose word was of God (s1, s2, s3)

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 1:1, 2 Peter 1:20-21 and Joel 2:28 confess: God spoke in time past by the prophets; holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost, so that prophecy came not by the will of man; and God promised that sons and daughters would prophesy, as Deborah, Huldah and the daughters of Philip did. This is the floor. It does not settle whether women may hold prophetic or ordained office today, the manner of inspiration, or inerrancy; each tradition fills those in its own house.

The apostolic witness that pairs with this floor is G1 (ratified 22 Sep). The two floors sit side by side, and neither repeats the other.

### A4-F2. Worship belongs to God alone (s4, s5)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 4:10 and Revelation 22:8-9 confess: thou shalt worship the Lord thy God, and Him only shalt thou serve; no prophet and no messenger of God, however great, receives worship. This is the floor. It does not settle the honor or veneration a tradition gives to saints, icons or the book of the Gospels, so long as it is distinguished from worship, nor whether the Son and the Holy Spirit are worshipped as God; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Why the exclusions are there: the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches distinguish veneration (*dulia*) from worship (*latria*). A floor that left veneration open would score them against a line they teach themselves. The second exclusion keeps the floor clear of the Trinitarian and Jehovah's Witness division over worship of the Son.

### A4-F3. God spoke, speaks, and will yet speak (s6): FORM A RULED (AOID2-A4S6-FLOOR, option 1, topic-only, 2 Oct 2026)

Ruled: Form A is the floor. Form B is kept below only as the record of what was weighed. Aaron's note: Form A suits Volume 1's interfaith purpose, and the fuller Restoration claim (JS-H 1:19; God as His own interpreter, above scripture and councils) is stated plainly in Volume 2.

Step 34 requires this floor to say whether it claims that God speaks today by His Spirit or that prophecy continues. Westminster Confession 1.1 tests the difference: the "former ways of God's revealing his will unto his people" are "now ceased", while 1.6 acknowledges "the inward illumination of the Spirit of God" as "necessary for the saving understanding" of the Word.

**Form A: God speaks today by His Spirit.**
> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 1:1-2, John 16:13 and Acts 2:17-18 confess: God, who spoke in time past by the prophets, hath in these last days spoken by His Son; the Spirit of truth guides disciples into all truth; and God has poured out His Spirit upon all flesh. This is the floor: God speaks to His people today by His Spirit. It does not settle whether that speaking includes new prophecy or revelation carrying doctrinal authority, whether the canon is open, or how what the Spirit speaks is related to Scripture; each tradition fills those in its own house.

**Form B: prophecy continues.**
> The Article confesses only what Acts 2:17-18, Amos 3:7 and Revelation 11:3 confess: in the last days God pours out His Spirit and sons and daughters prophesy, the Lord GOD will do nothing but He revealeth His secret unto His servants the prophets, and His witnesses shall prophesy. This is the floor: the gift of prophecy has not ceased. It does not settle who holds it, what authority it carries, or whether the canon is open; each tradition fills those in its own house.

What each form costs (UNVERIFIED):
- Form A is the floor on which the Reformed family can count. Westminster 1.1 rules out new revelation, but 1.6 and 1.10 confess the Spirit speaking in and through the Word. Pentecostal and Latter-day Saint readers fill it more fully. This is the container strategy, and it is the June firewall exactly: continuing revelation shared, open canon kept for Volume 2.
- Form B is almost certain to fail C4. Westminster 1.1 makes the Reformed family differ. The 1689 Baptist Confession keeps the same clause in substance, which makes the Baptist family differ too. That is two families at least, and the threshold allows three to fall short.
- Either way, A5 governs the anchors. Revelation 11:3-6, Revelation 14:6-7 and Malachi 4:5-6 stay in the biblical column whichever form is ratified. Under Form A they support "will yet speak" (the future clause) rather than "speaks today".

Decided 2 Oct 2026: Form A.

### A4-F4. Broken vessels (s7): AMENDED AND RATIFIED by Aaron 4 Oct 2026

> The Article confesses only what 2 Corinthians 4:7, 1 Corinthians 1:27-29 and Romans 3:23 confess: we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us; God hath chosen the weak things of the world, that no flesh should glory in His presence; and all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God. This is the floor: the servants through whom God works are frail and fallible, and the power of His work is His, not theirs. It does not settle which servants God has chosen, what authority they bear, whether any servant besides Christ was kept from sin, whether God preserves any servant from error in what He gives them to teach, or how a servant's unworthiness bears on the ordinances; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Why it was amended (AOID2-A4F4-FLOOR, Aaron, 4 Oct 2026): the 2 Oct floor scored taught 6, contradicted 0, no position 6, queue 4 (CODE-AOID2-09); no tradition differed, but few confessions speak of God's servants as a class. The exclusions keep Catholic teaching on Mary and on infallible definitions, and the question of an unworthy minister and the ordinances, out of the scoring. The 1 Corinthians clause joins verses 27 and 29 as the 2 Oct floor did, and claims no more than the verses. KJV wording verified on churchofjesuschrist.org, 4 Oct 2026. Rescored as Task 0 of the Article 6 run.

This is the floor. It does not settle which servants God has chosen, or what authority they bear; each tradition fills those in its own house."

### A4-F5. Study, the Spirit, and the Scriptures as the measure (s8, s14, s16, s17)

> The Article confesses only what Proverbs 3:5, Acts 17:11, 1 Corinthians 2:12-14, 1 Corinthians 14:33 and Isaiah 8:20 confess: disciples search the scriptures daily without leaning unto their own understanding; the things of God are known by the Spirit of God; God is not the author of confusion; and every word is weighed against the law and the testimony He has given. This is the floor. It does not settle whether Scripture is the only infallible rule, the authority of tradition or a teaching office, or which books make up the canon; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Scoring note: the floor says weighed against the Scriptures, not against the Scriptures alone. Catholic and Orthodox churches confess Scripture as a norm (with Tradition), and sola scriptura churches confess it as the only norm. Both meet the floor.

### A4-F6. Preserved, plainly (s10): AMENDED AND RATIFIED by Aaron 4 Oct 2026

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 40:8, Matthew 24:35 and Romans 15:4 confess: the word of our God shall stand for ever, and Christ's words shall not pass away; and whatsoever things were written aforetime were written for our learning, that we through patience and comfort of the scriptures might have hope. This is the floor: God's word stands, and the Scriptures were given for our learning and hope. It does not settle inerrancy, the history of the text, whether anything once written has been lost, how God restores what is lost, or which books make up the canon; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Why it was amended (AOID2-A4F6-FLOOR, Aaron, 4 Oct 2026): the 2 Oct floor scored taught 11, contradicted 0, no position 4, queue 1 (CODE-AOID2-09); no tradition differed, but few adopted texts confess the two verses as a doctrine of Scripture. Under C4(a)(ii) it returned to the author and gained the Article's own half, "preserved for us", in Paul's words (Romans 15:4, KJV verified on churchofjesuschrist.org, 4 Oct 2026). The Anglican Book of Common Prayer collect for the Second Sunday in Advent ("written for our learning") is to be confirmed under B2(f) in the rescore. The Latter-day Saint cell (D&C 1:38; Joseph Smith-Matthew 1:35, with 1 Nephi 13 and Articles of Faith 1:8 inside the exclusions) is unchanged. Rescored with A4-F4 as Task 0 of the Article 6 run.

Superseded wording (ratified 2 Oct 2026, kept on record): "The Article confesses only what Isaiah 40:8 and Matthew 24:35 confess: the word of our God shall stand for ever, and Christ's words shall not pass away. This is the floor. It does not settle inerrancy, the history of the text, whether anything once written has been lost, or how God restores what is lost; each tradition fills those in its own house."

**Scoring risk, home tradition:** the Article's own words ("what God has spoken plainly ... has been preserved for us") sit uneasily beside 1 Nephi 13:26-29 (plain and precious things taken away) and Articles of Faith 1:8 ("as far as it is translated correctly"). The floor is held at Isaiah 40:8 so that The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints counts on its own terms (A4(c)), since it confesses that God's word endures and is restored. **Aaron, 2 Oct 2026: the sentence stays unchanged.** His reason: "preserved, but not perfectly" is the view of more than the Latter-day Saints alone. The floor's exclusions (inerrancy, the history of the text, whether anything was lost) leave room for that view in every family.

## Article 6, Of Salvation

### A6-F1. Entrance to the Kingdom through Christ alone (s1)

> The Article confesses only what Mark 1:15, John 10:9, John 14:6 and Acts 4:12 confess: Christ preached the gospel of the kingdom of God; He is the door, and by Him if any man enter in he shall be saved; no man cometh unto the Father but by Him; and there is salvation in no other name. This is the floor. It does not settle the state of those who never heard the gospel in this life, whether the gospel is preached to the dead, or the place of the Church in salvation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A6-F2. What we are saved from and for, known in part (s2, s3)

> The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 13:9-12 and 1 Corinthians 2:9 confess: we know in part, and eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, the things which God hath prepared for them that love Him. This is the floor. It does not settle the nature of heaven, of hell, or of the state after death; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A6-F3. Grace and the Atonement of the Son of God as the ground (s4)

> The Article confesses only what Ephesians 2:8-9 and Acts 4:12 confess: by grace are ye saved through faith, and that not of yourselves, it is the gift of God; salvation from sin and death is in Christ alone. This is the floor. It does not settle any one account of how the Atonement saves, whether righteousness is imputed or imparted, or the extent of the atonement; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A6-F4. The arc: created in His image, conformed to His, glorified with Him (s5, s6, s7)

> The Article confesses only what Genesis 1:27, 2 Corinthians 3:18, 1 John 3:2 and Romans 8:17 confess: God created man in His own image; beholding the glory of the Lord we are changed into the same image from glory to glory; when He shall appear we shall be like Him; and the children of God are joint-heirs with Christ, that we may be also glorified together. This is the floor. It does not settle the nature of the glorified state, deification or exaltation, degrees of glory, or where the redeemed dwell; each tradition fills those in its own house.

**Scoring risk:** Jehovah's Witnesses' adopted teaching reserves joint heirship with Christ to the 144,000 anointed, while the "great crowd" lives forever on a paradise earth. On its own terms (A4(c)), the family may differ from "we ... co-heirs with Christ" as a statement about all disciples. The family is on interim footing (AOID2-JW-FOOTING). Flag for the scorer. Do not narrow the floor to get past this cell.

### A6-F5. Known in part, and never reduced (s8, s9)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 11:33 and Matthew 23:23 confess: how unsearchable are God's judgments and His ways past finding out; and of all that the Lord commanded, these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. This is the floor: the gospel Christ taught is received whole, and no part of it is set aside. It does not settle the order of justification and sanctification, which of them is the ground of salvation, or whether faith alone justifies; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Scoring note: the Lutheran confessions hold justification by faith alone as the chief article. The floor tests whether the whole of Christ's teaching is received, not whether any part is ranked above another. Lutheran texts confess sanctification and good works as the fruit of faith (Augsburg VI and XX), so the family should meet it.

### A6-F6. The gospel's parts: faith, repentance, new birth, the Spirit's fruit and sealing (s10, s11, s13)

> The Article confesses only what Mark 1:15, John 3:3-5, Galatians 5:22-23 and Ephesians 1:13-14 with John 11:25 confess: repent and believe the gospel; except a man be born again he cannot see the kingdom of God; the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, longsuffering, gentleness, goodness, faith, meekness and temperance; believers are sealed with that Holy Spirit of promise; and He that believeth in Christ shall live. This is the floor. It does not settle the order of these parts, when the new birth takes place, or how the sealing is given; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A6-F7. Works as fruit, not price (s12)

> The Article confesses only what Ephesians 2:8-10 and John 15:8 confess: we are saved by grace, not of works, yet we are created in Christ Jesus unto good works; herein is the Father glorified, that we bear much fruit. This is the floor: good works are the fruit God's grace brings forth, and no one is saved by works done apart from His grace. It does not settle merit, reward, the forensic order, or the necessity of works for final salvation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

This floor is shared with A11-F3. Catholic teaching on merit (merit is itself God's gift; the Catechism, 2008-2011) and Latter-day Saint teaching ("relying wholly upon the merits of him who is mighty to save", 2 Nephi 31:19) should both meet it. UNVERIFIED.

### A6-F8. Judgment belongs to Christ (s14)

> The Article confesses only what John 5:22, Romans 14:4 and James 4:12 confess: the Father hath committed all judgment unto the Son; who art thou that judgest another man's servant; there is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy. This is the floor: the final judgment of every soul is Christ's. It does not settle whether a church may declare that a departed soul is with God, the discipline a church exercises over its own members, or assurance of one's own salvation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

**Scoring risk, and why the floor is narrower than the sentence:** the Article says "judging no soul to be saved or condemned in His place". The Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches canonize and glorify saints, which declares particular souls to be with God, though neither ever declares a soul condemned. The floor is held at the reservation of judgment to Christ, which every family confesses. The Article's fuller refusal stands as the ministry's own conviction and is not scored. **CONFIRMED by Aaron 2 Oct 2026**: this is the one place where the floor is deliberately narrower than the Article's words.

### A6-F9. The charge, the Spirit and the Word, and the disciple's labor (s15, s16, s17, s18)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 22:37-40, John 5:39 with John 16:13, Proverbs 3:5-6 and Philippians 2:12-13 confess: thou shalt love the Lord thy God and thy neighbour as thyself; the scriptures testify of Christ and the Spirit guides into truth; trust in the LORD and lean not unto thine own understanding; and work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God which worketh in you both to will and to do of His good pleasure. This is the floor. It does not settle how the disciple's working and God's working are related; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Philippians 2:13 is set beside 2:12 as Aaron proposed on 1 Oct. The apparatus at s18 already anchors 2:12-13, but the Article's sentence names only verse 12 ("Paul's counsel to work out our own salvation with fear and trembling"). **Aaron, 2 Oct 2026: the sentence is to carry verse 13, "for it is God which worketh in you", read as a willing partnership and not as predestination.** The proposed wording is in the Article text edits section below, for his approval.

## Article 11, Of Covenants and Commitments

### A11-F1. Children of God by birth (s1)

> The Article confesses only what Acts 17:28-29 confesses: in Him we live, and move, and have our being; we are also His offspring. Every human being is God's offspring, owing life and breath to Him, and is never to be spoken of as outside His care. This is the floor. It does not settle whether the soul or spirit existed before mortal birth, or how being God's offspring by creation is related to being His child by adoption and new birth (John 1:12; Romans 8:15); each tradition fills those in its own house.

From Aaron's thesis "Children of God by birth. Disciples of Christ by covenant", and his 1 Oct note that it is "a very dangerous course to look at another of God's creations and pronounce 'You are not a child of God.'" Under A4(c), each body is tested on whether it confesses Acts 17:28-29 as Scripture's teaching that all are God's offspring. It is not tested on whether it uses "child of God" for the unregenerate, which Reformed and evangelical texts reserve for adoption (Westminster 12). The container holds both.

### A11-F2. Disciples by covenant (s2)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 28:19-20 and Hebrews 8:10 confess: Christ commanded His disciples to make disciples of all nations, baptizing them and teaching them to observe all things He commanded; and God promised to put His laws into their mind and write them in their hearts. This is the floor: discipleship is a bond with Christ that one enters, not a birthright. It does not settle whether baptism is a sacrament or an ordinance, its mode, who may receive or administer it, or whether "covenant" is a church's own word for that bond; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Scoring risk: the Salvation Army, in the Holiness family, does not practise water baptism. Following G6's pattern, the floor is written as what Christ commanded, not as what disciples practise.

### A11-F3. Love proven by obedience, and not salvation's price (s3, s4)

> The Article confesses only what John 14:15, John 15:14 and Ephesians 2:8-9 with Galatians 5:6 confess: if ye love me, keep my commandments; ye are my friends, if ye do whatsoever I command you; by grace are ye saved, not of works; and faith worketh by love. This is the floor. It does not settle merit or the necessity of works for final salvation (see A6-F7); each tradition fills those in its own house.

### A11-F4. The cost, the cross, the return (s5, s6, s7, s8)

> The Article confesses only what Luke 9:23, Luke 14:27-28, Romans 12:1 and Romans 3:23 with 1 John 1:9 confess: if any man will come after Christ, let him deny himself, take up his cross daily and follow Him; whosoever doth not bear his cross cannot be His disciple, and the builder counts the cost; disciples present their bodies a living sacrifice; all have sinned, and if we confess our sins He is faithful to forgive. This is the floor. It does not settle how sin after conversion is forgiven (by confession to God, a sacrament of penance or reconciliation, or otherwise), or whether grace once received can be lost; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Note for Aaron, outside the floor: the June study found that distanced readers cool at "Live it ... as reality" (s5). Its proposed fix was to bring A10's patience ("no soul finishes in a day") into this paragraph. s8's "we repent ... and strive to sin no more" already does part of that. ### A6 s18: carry Philippians 2:13, as partnership

Current: "We heed Paul's counsel to work out our own salvation with fear and trembling before the Lord."

- **Proposed (recommended):** "We heed Paul's counsel to work out our own salvation with fear and trembling before the Lord, as He works in us both to will and to do."
- Alternative: "..., for it is God who works in us both to will and to do of His good pleasure."

Why "as" rather than "for": "for" is the KJV's own word, but it reads as cause, and a monergist reader can take it as God working instead of us. "As" holds the two workings together in time, the disciple working out and God working in. That is the willing partnership Aaron named, and it does not deny the verse's own claim that God is at work. The span type stays "paraphrase", and the s18 panel comment already anchors 2:12-13 and Psalm 2:11.
