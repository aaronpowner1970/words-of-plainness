## Floors

### A10-F1. God speaks to every open conscience, whether we feel worthy or not (s1)

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 30:21, Romans 2:14-15 and Luke 18:13-14 confess: thine ears shall hear a word behind thee, saying, This is the way, walk ye in it; the Gentiles show the work of the law written in their hearts, their conscience also bearing witness; and the publican, who would not lift up so much as his eyes unto heaven, but said, God be merciful to me a sinner, went down to his house justified. This is the floor: God speaks to every conscience open to hearing Him, and He does not wait for us to feel worthy before He speaks. It does not settle what that inward speaking is, whether the Holy Spirit Himself, the Light of Christ, grace that goes before conversion, or the conscience God gives at creation; how it is related to the Spirit given to disciples; or whether it can bring a soul to salvation apart from the gospel; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for God's word to the hearer and the witness of conscience; illustrative application for the publican, who is heard while feeling unworthy.

Precedent: the exclusion is A9-F7's, word for word, because the two spans make the same claim about the inward witness.

Anchor addition proposed: Luke 18:13-14 at s1. "Whether we feel worthy or not" now has no anchor; the publican is the Gospels' plainest case.

### A10-F2. Guided daily, toward a character He is shaping (s2)

> The Article confesses only what Philippians 1:6, Romans 8:28-29 and Jeremiah 29:11 confess: he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ; all things work together for good to them that love God, to be conformed to the image of his Son; and I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace. This is the floor: God guides each life in its daily paths and circumstances, and is shaping His children toward the character of His Son. It does not settle the manner of God's foreknowledge, predestination, or how His providence and guidance bear on human freedom and suffering; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the good work God performs and the conforming to His Son; illustrative application for Jeremiah 29:11, spoken to the exiles in Babylon.

Precedent: the exclusion joins A2-F3's ("the manner of God's foreknowledge ... predestination, or how His providence bears on human freedom") and A2-F2's ("how His purposes bear on human freedom and suffering"), which floor Romans 8:28 and providence.

Anchor addition proposed: Romans 8:28-29 at s2, for "toward a character He is shaping" (conformed to the image of His Son).

### A10-F3. Our daily choices matter (s3)

> The Article confesses only what Galatians 6:7-8 and Matthew 7:24-25 confess: whatsoever a man soweth, that shall he also reap; and whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock. This is the floor: what we choose and do each day matters before God and bears its fruit. It does not settle how the will's choosing is related to God's grace, whether the will is free or bound apart from grace, or how our doings bear on merit or final salvation (see A6-F7); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Scoring risk: confessions that teach the bondage of the will (UNVERIFIED: Lutheran, Reformed) still confess that sowing and reaping are real; the exclusion keeps the freedom question out, so they are not read against a claim the span does not make.

### A10-F4. Grace is no license to sin; it is God's own and His power (s4)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 6:1-2, Titus 2:11-12, Exodus 34:6 and 2 Corinthians 12:9 confess: shall we continue in sin, that grace may abound? God forbid; the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men, teaching us that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we should live soberly, righteously, and godly; the LORD God, merciful and gracious; and my grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. This is the floor: grace is not leave to sin; it belongs to God's own nature, and it is His power at work in those He saves. It does not settle any one account of how the Atonement saves, whether righteousness is imputed or imparted, or the place of works; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for each clause.

Precedent: the exclusion is A6-F3's with A6-F7's "the place of works."

Anchor addition proposed: 2 Corinthians 12:9 at s4. "Power" now rests on Titus 2:11-12, which shows grace teaching; 2 Corinthians 12:9 names grace as strength.

Scoring risk: a confession that defines grace chiefly as God's favor (UNVERIFIED: some Lutheran and Reformed texts) may still confess that grace works in the believer (the Holy Spirit's renewal). The floor claims "His power at work in those He saves," not a doctrine of infused grace; the scorer must not read it as the latter.

### A10-F5. A walk, a maturing, a becoming; no soul finishes in a day (s5, s8)

> The Article confesses only what Philippians 3:12-14, 2 Peter 1:5-8 and Ephesians 4:13-15 confess: not as though I had already attained, either were already perfect: but I follow after; add to your faith virtue, and to virtue knowledge; till we all come unto a perfect man, unto the measure of the stature of the fulness of Christ, that we may grow up into him in all things. This is the floor: the Christian life is a walk and a growing up into Christ, begun and not completed in a day. It does not settle whether or when a disciple is made perfect in this life, how sanctification is related to justification, or what the disciple is finally made (see A6-F4); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: "whether or when a disciple is made perfect in this life" from A7-F5; "how sanctification relates to justification" from R5; the glorified state is left to A6-F4's exclusion ("deification or exaltation").

### A10-F6. Doing good after His example, as unto the Lord (s6, s7)

> The Article confesses only what Acts 10:38, 1 Peter 2:21, Colossians 3:23-24 and Matthew 6:1-4 confess: Jesus of Nazareth went about doing good; Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that ye should follow his steps; whatsoever ye do, do it heartily, as to the Lord, and not unto men; and take heed that ye do not your alms before men, to be seen of them, and thy Father which seeth in secret himself shall reward thee openly. This is the floor: disciples follow Christ's example in doing good, and they do it as unto the Lord, whether or not men authorize, recognize or reward it. It does not settle who may administer the ordinances or sacraments or hold office in a church, or what authority a church's ministry requires; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the example (Acts 10:38; 1 Peter 2:21) and for working as to the Lord and not men (Colossians 3:23); theological inference for "authorized," drawn from the same verses.

Anchor addition proposed: Matthew 6:1-4 at s7. It is Christ's own teaching that good done for men's recognition loses its reward; s7 now rests on Colossians 3:23-24 alone in the biblical column.

Judgment call 1 (s7), ranked:
1. **Teaching, joined to s6 (recommended).** s7 completes s6: the good we do does not wait on men's warrant. The exclusion keeps the word "authorized" from being read as a claim about authority to administer ordinances or hold office, which the span does not touch (it speaks of "going about doing good"). Weakness: "authorized" is the word most likely to be misread; the scorer must hold the line between doing good, which needs no warrant, and ministering ordinances, which the floor leaves open.
2. **Posture**, as the ministry's own conviction (like A8 s10). Weakness: the span stands under "We believe" and Christ taught it (Matthew 6:1-4); calling it posture leaves His teaching unmeasured.
3. **Joined to A9-F12** (no approval beyond Christ's). Weakness: A9-F12 speaks of the disciple's standing; s7 speaks of good works, and joining would score claims the span does not make.

### A10-F7. No soul beyond His reach (s9)

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 59:1, Luke 19:10 and Romans 8:38-39 confess: the LORD's hand is not shortened, that it cannot save; the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost; and nothing shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord. This is the floor: no soul is beyond the reach of God's saving hand. It does not settle the extent of the atonement, whether all will finally be saved, how God's love is related to His judgment, or the state of those who die without hearing the gospel; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for Isaiah 59:1 and Luke 19:10; theological inference for "no soul," drawn from the hand that is not shortened.

Precedent: the exclusions are A2-F2's, which floors Romans 8:38-39, with A6-F1's clause on those who never heard.

Anchor addition proposed: Luke 19:10 at s9 (G13's floor verse, "to seek and to save that which was lost").

Scoring risk: "No soul" may be heard as universalism. The floor claims the reach of God's hand, not that every soul will be saved; the exclusion says so.

### A10-F8. No one may shut the kingdom or stand between the Shepherd and His lambs (s10, s17)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 23:13, Luke 11:52, Matthew 19:14 and John 10:11 confess: woe unto you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for ye shut up the kingdom of heaven against men: for ye neither go in yourselves, neither suffer ye them that are entering to go in; them that were entering in ye hindered; suffer little children, and forbid them not, to come unto me; and I am the good shepherd. This is the floor: God's grace is His to give, and no one may shut the kingdom against those whom He would bring in, or stand between Christ and those who come to Him; any who do so are called to repent. It does not settle the Church's ministry of word, sacrament or ordinance and how grace is received through it, a church's discipline of its own members, or who will finally be saved; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the woe and the command "forbid them not"; illustrative application for John 10:11, from which the Article draws "the Shepherd and His lambs."

Anchor additions proposed: Luke 11:52 and Matthew 19:14 at s17. The span's command ("do not stand between") now rests on John 10:11-15 and Ezekiel 34:10, which describe the shepherd; the two verses state the command.

Judgment call 2 (s10), ranked:
1. **Teaching, joined to s17 (recommended).** "We call to repentance anyone who would think to manage or restrict the grace of God" takes Christ's own woe (Matthew 23:13) as its warrant, and pastoral integrity (section 0) does not let it be softened. The exclusion keeps the Church's sacramental ministry, through which many families confess that grace is given, from being read as "managing" grace. Weakness: a reader in a sacramental church may still hear "manage" as aimed at his church; the floor's exclusion answers the scorer, not every reader.
2. **Posture, with s11** (the ministry's own call within its community). Weakness: s10 is not confined to "this interfaith community," as s11 is; it calls "anyone," and Christ's woe stands behind it.
3. **Its own floor at Matthew 23:13 alone.** Weakness: s10 and s17 make one claim (do not bar the way to Christ); two floors on one claim could be scored against each other.

### A10-F9. Not ours to condemn over differences in doctrine (s12)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 14:10, 13, Matthew 7:1 and James 4:12 confess: why dost thou judge thy brother? for we shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ; let us not therefore judge one another any more; judge not, that ye be not judged; and there is one lawgiver, who is able to save and to destroy: who art thou that judgest another? This is the floor: it is not ours to condemn any soul over differences in doctrine or theology; judgment belongs to God. It does not settle which doctrines are necessary to salvation, how much right belief God requires of each soul, by what authority doctrine is defined, a church's discipline of its own members, or its teaching on right and wrong; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the command not to judge (Romans 14:13; Matthew 7:1); theological inference for "over differences in doctrine," drawn from Romans 14, where the disputes are over doctrine and practice.

Precedent: "which doctrines are necessary to salvation, how much right belief God requires of each soul, or by what authority doctrine is defined" is copied from A9-F2, where the same exclusions keep a creedal text on those questions from making a cell DIFFER (C4(a)(v)). "A church's discipline ... or its teaching on right and wrong" is from P-F8.

Anchor addition proposed: Romans 14:10, 13 and Matthew 7:1 at s12. The span now rests on Romans 2:1, 1 Corinthians 4:3-4 and D&C 64:9-10; the two verses are the plainest commands.

Judgment call 3 (s12), ranked:
1. **New floor with A9-F2's exclusions (recommended).** Keeps "over differences in doctrine," which is the span's own point. Weakness: the cells that will strain are those whose adopted creeds attach final loss to doctrinal error (UNVERIFIED: the Athanasian Creed received in Lutheran, Anglican and Catholic texts). A9-F2 faced the same cells; the scorer applies C4(a)(v) the same way here, and does not narrow the floor.
2. **Joined to P-F8** ("Judgment we leave to God"). Weakness: P-F8 does not name doctrine; joining would score a general claim and leave the span's point unmeasured.
3. **Posture**, as part of the community's standard. Weakness: the span is an exhortation "in the name of Jesus Christ" resting on His plain command; it would leave the command unmeasured.

### A10-F10. Those who seek Him are precious to Him, and He guards them (s13, s19, s14)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 18:6, 10, 14, Isaiah 43:4 and John 2:17 confess: whoso shall offend one of these little ones which believe in me, it were better for him that a millstone were hanged about his neck, and that he were drowned in the depth of the sea; take heed that ye despise not one of these little ones; it is not the will of your Father which is in heaven, that one of these little ones should perish; thou wast precious in my sight; and the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up. This is the floor: every soul who seeks and believes in Christ is precious to Him; He guards them with jealous care, and He warned with terrible seriousness against causing one of them to stumble. It does not settle who "these little ones" are, whether children, new believers or all disciples; what the millstone signifies; or whether a true believer can finally fall away; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the warning and the Father's will (Matthew 18:6, 14); illustrative application for Isaiah 43:4 (spoken to Israel) and John 2:17 (zeal for the temple), which the Article draws on for Christ's protective care.

Precedent: "whether a true believer can finally fall away" from A11-F8.

Note: s14 quotes Matthew 18:6 in the KJV word for word; it is a direct quotation and is floored by the verse it quotes.

### A10-F11. Gentleness toward the misguided; receiving the weak in faith (s15, s16)

> The Article confesses only what 2 Timothy 2:24-25, Hebrews 5:2 and Romans 14:1 confess: the servant of the Lord must not strive; but be gentle unto all men, apt to teach, patient, in meekness instructing those that oppose themselves; who can have compassion on the ignorant, and on them that are out of the way; and him that is weak in the faith receive ye, but not to doubtful disputations. This is the floor: disciples deal gently with the ignorant and those who have gone astray, and receive the weak in faith without quarrelling over disputable things. It does not settle which matters are disputable and which are not, or a church's discipline of its own members; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for 2 Timothy 2:24-25 and Romans 14:1; illustrative application for Hebrews 5:2, which describes the high priest's compassion and is taken as the disciple's pattern.

Anchor addition proposed: none needed for the floor, but 2 Timothy 2:24-25 is already at s15.

Note (not a ruling): s15 and s16 are marked `data-type="direct"`, but their wording is not the KJV. "Accept the one whose faith is weak, without quarreling over disputable matters" closely follows a modern translation of Romans 14:1; "Deal gently with the ignorant and misguided" paraphrases Hebrews 5:2. The Articles otherwise quote the KJV. No change is proposed here; it is named so that Aaron can decide whether the Second Edition keeps the modern wording, marks it as a paraphrase, or quotes the KJV. Whatever is decided, the floor quotes the KJV.
