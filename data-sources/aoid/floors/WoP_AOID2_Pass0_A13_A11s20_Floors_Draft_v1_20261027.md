## Floors, Article 13

### A13-F1. Christ is enough (s1)

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 7:25, Colossians 2:10 and 2 Corinthians 12:9 confess: he is able also to save them to the uttermost that come unto God by him, seeing he ever liveth to make intercession for them; ye are complete in him; and my grace is sufficient for thee. This is the floor: Christ is sufficient; in Him those who come to God through Him are made complete, and nothing outside Him is needed to save them. It does not settle what faith, confession, repentance and ordinances Christ requires of those able to receive them, which stand where the Articles set them (Article 11), or the place of the Church in salvation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the first exclusion is P-F5's, word for word; "the place of the Church in salvation" is A6-F1's. Together they keep "Christ is enough" from being read as a denial of the ordinances or the Church, which the Articles set elsewhere and which pastoral integrity (section 0) will not let any reader set aside.

Anchor addition proposed: Colossians 2:10 at s1 ("ye are complete in him"), the plainest verse for sufficiency.

### A13-F2. A gospel for the flawed, the weary, the wounded and the searching (s2)

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 61:1, Matthew 11:28, Matthew 9:12-13 and Isaiah 57:15 confess: he hath sent me to bind up the brokenhearted; come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden; they that be whole need not a physician, but they that are sick, for I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance; and I dwell with him that is of a contrite and humble spirit. This is the floor: Christ's gospel is for the sinful, the weary, the brokenhearted and the seeking, and it calls them to Him and to repentance.

Kind of witness: direct.

Anchor addition proposed: Matthew 9:12-13 at s2. "The flawed" now rests on Isaiah 61 and 57; Christ's own word "I am not come to call the righteous, but sinners" states it plainly, and its "to repentance" keeps the floor from reading as welcome without call (AOID2-PASTORAL-INTEGRITY).

No exclusion is needed; nothing here bears on a question the traditions divide on.

### A13-F3. The invitation to every nation, tongue and tradition (s3)

> The Article confesses only what Revelation 7:9, Isaiah 49:6 and Revelation 22:17 confess: a great multitude, which no man could number, of all nations, and kindreds, and people, and tongues, stood before the throne, and before the Lamb; I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles, that thou mayest be my salvation unto the end of the earth; and whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely. This is the floor: Christ's invitation goes to every nation, kindred and tongue, and reaches whosoever will come, in whatever tradition they stand. It does not settle how mission is carried out, whether it is directed toward members of other Christian traditions, or who will finally be saved; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for every nation, kindred and tongue; theological inference for "across every tradition," drawn from "whosoever will."

Precedent: the first two exclusions are G12's, word for word (G12 floors Matthew 28:18-20 at s12 of this Article).

Anchor addition proposed: Revelation 22:17 at s3 ("whosoever will"), which carries "across every tradition" better than the present anchors.

### A13-F4. He receives all who sincerely confess Him and seek the Father's will (s4)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 7:21, John 6:37, 40 and Acts 10:34-35 confess: not every one that saith unto me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doeth the will of my Father which is in heaven; him that cometh to me I will in no wise cast out; and this is the will of him that sent me, that every one which seeth the Son, and believeth on him, may have everlasting life; God is no respecter of persons: but in every nation he that feareth him, and worketh righteousness, is accepted with him. This is the floor: Christ receives all who sincerely confess His name and seek to do the will of His Father, and He casts out none who come to Him. It does not settle what faith, confession, repentance and ordinances Christ requires of those able to receive them, which stand where the Articles set them (Article 11); how faith and works are related in salvation (see A6-F7); or the state of those who never hear the gospel in this life; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: exclusions from P-F5, A6-F7 and A6-F1. Step 42's prepended comment ("Confession is the door, and the Father's will is the path; the Lord's own definition of that will (John 6:40) is belief on the Son, so the Article teaches no salvation by works") stays after the floor (A4(e)); under A4(g) step 103 should check that its last clause names an open question without answering it.

Scoring risk: Acts 10:34-35 ("in every nation he that feareth him ... is accepted") is read in some traditions as bearing on those outside the visible Church (UNVERIFIED). The exclusion on those who never hear the gospel keeps that question out.

### A13-F5. Christ in us; we abide and stand with Him (s5, s8)

> The Article confesses only what Galatians 2:20, Colossians 1:27, John 15:4-5 and Matthew 10:32 confess: Christ liveth in me; Christ in you, the hope of glory; abide in me, and I in you; and whosoever therefore shall confess me before men, him will I confess also before my Father which is in heaven. This is the floor: Christ dwells in the hearts of His disciples, they abide in Him, and they stand with Him and confess Him before men. It does not settle the manner of His indwelling, whether by His Spirit, by faith, in the sacrament, or otherwise; each tradition fills that in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: the exclusion follows G3's "the manner of His presence, whether by His Spirit, in the gathered body, or in the sacrament."

## Judgment call 1: three joins (s6, s7, s11)

Each span makes, from the disciple's side, a claim already floored elsewhere in the Articles. Joining keeps one floor per claim, so the same claim is not scored twice against itself.

- **s6 → P-F5 verbatim** ("Not barred for want of perfect understanding"). s6 confesses from our side what P-F5 confesses from Christ's: He receives those who call on Him without a perfect understanding. "Divine Savior" is measured in Article 5's floors on who Christ is, so it is not left unmeasured.
- **s7 → A9-F12 verbatim** ("No approval required beyond Christ's"). Its anchors are Acts 5:29 and Galatians 1:10, the same claim. A9-F12's exclusion (whether Christ gives His approval through the ministry and ordinances of His Church) applies here too.
- **s11 → A11-F1 verbatim** ("Children of God by birth"; Acts 17:28-29 is anchored at both spans). A11-F1 is already scored: taught 15, contradicted 0, no position 1 of 16. Joining carries that count, and its container (offspring by creation; adoption and new birth left to each house) is exactly what "We are all God's children" needs.

Candidates, ranked:
1. **All three joins (recommended).** Weakness: a reader may feel Article 13, the closing confession, deserves its own measurements; the joins measure the same claims, and the apparatus names the join so the repetition reads as intended (as the Prologue's s19 joined L1).
2. **New floors for each.** Weakness: three floors would restate three ratified floors in other words, and could be scored against them.
3. **Join s6 and s11, new floor for s7.** Weakness: s7 says "permission or approval of others" where A9-F12 says "approval ... beyond the approval of Christ"; the difference is wording, not claim, so a new floor would duplicate A9-F12.

## Judgment call 2: s9 and s10 as posture

- **s9** "we welcome all who seek to understand, whether or not they share the same belief in God, or don't yet know what they believe, or even if they hold to creeds that differ from the basic foundations professed within these articles": the ministry's own welcome, as A8 s14 and s15 were ruled. Its ground (Romans 15:7; Luke 15:1-2) is measured at A8-F1.
- **s10** "In the spirit of peace, we build on those beliefs we hold in common, and exercise careful restraint and Christ-like patience regarding things that might divide us": the method of the Articles themselves, described in their closing. Its commanded core (keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace, Ephesians 4:3; the servant of the Lord must not strive, 2 Timothy 2:24) is measured at A8-F5 and A10-F11.

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Both posture (recommended).** Weakness: Ephesians 4:3 is a plain command anchored at s10; it is measured in substance at A10-F11 and A8-F5, not here.
2. **s10 teaching, new floor at Ephesians 4:3.** Weakness: "build on those beliefs we hold in common" is the Articles' own method, which no church is asked to teach; the floor would score the ministry's method as doctrine.
3. **s9 teaching, joined to A8-F1.** Weakness: s9 welcomes those who do not yet believe in God, which A8-F1 (those who come to Christ) does not cover; the join would misstate both.

## Judgment call 3: A11 s20, "Christ's disciples today are not all of one mind on sexual holiness, yet the word of God on it is as plain now as it was then"

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Posture: a declared conviction resting on a measured fact (recommended).** The first clause describes the churches: it is the measured division itself (29a taught in 11 of 16, 29b in 10 of 16), disclosed in "Of Sexual Holiness: A Disclosure of Division." The second clause is this ministry's own conviction that Scripture is plain on it, which the disclosure already says is a confession made by "this interfaith ministry," and which Aaron set as the Article's stance ("I will not dither on sexual holiness"). The teaching itself is measured at s21 (29a and 29b), under the dagger. Weakness: the span is not purely the ministry's method; its second clause is a claim about Scripture. It is held as conviction, as A8 s10 was (AOID2-A8-CONVICTION-FORM), and is not dressed as common ground.
2. **Descriptive.** Weakness: true of the first clause only; the second clause is not a description of the churches.
3. **Teaching, new floor ("Scripture's teaching on sexual holiness is plain").** Weakness: it would measure the 29a and 29b division a second time under another name, and nearly every church would confess Scripture plain on its own reading, so the count would hide the division the disclosure exists to show. That breaches integrity (section 0).
