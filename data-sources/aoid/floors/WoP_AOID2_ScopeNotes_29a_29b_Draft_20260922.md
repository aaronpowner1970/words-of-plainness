## 29a: Sexual relations belong within marriage

**Panel comment (the floor), RATIFIED by Aaron 22 Sep 2026:**

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Thessalonians 4:3-7 confess: sexual union is a holy gift whose rightful place is marriage, and outside marriage the disciple keeps chastity. This is the floor, and it is received across historic Christian teaching. It does not settle who may marry (see item 29b); whether marriage is a sacrament; its permanence, divorce or remarriage; the purposes of marital intimacy beyond its holiness; or questions of contraception. Each tradition fills those in its own house. The Restoration's fuller teaching (the law of chastity as covenant; D&C 42:22; Jacob 2:28) is amplifying witness, not the Article's claim.

**ATTRIBUTE or ACTION:** neither in the codex's sense, since this is a moral teaching and not a predicate about God. Scored as a TEACHING: does the body officially teach it?

**Test at the floor (C4):** for each family, does any member church's adopted official position (B2) hold that sexual relations outside marriage are rightly practised? If so, that family does not count.

**Not imported:**
- a definition of marriage (that is 29b);
- procreative purpose as a requirement;
- the language "husband and wife."

Importing any of these would score 29b under 29a's name. That is the Son-family failure (A4(b)).

**Floor question, RULED 22 Sep 2026 (AOID2-29A-FLOOR, option 1, filed as a refinement of A4):** each body is tested by its own word. The test asks whether the body teaches that sexual relations belong within marriage, as that body defines marriage. A blessed union the body does not call marriage counts against 29a only if the body officially affirms sexual relations within it. A rite that is silent on the point goes to the review queue (C3); it is not scored against the body. General form for the codex: a body is tested against its own definition of a floor's terms, never another tradition's.

**Evidence genres (F.2):** this teaching is rarely in the creeds. It sits in catechisms (for example on the Sixth or Seventh Commandment), confessions (Westminster ch. 24), marriage rites, canons, and assembly social statements. See the F.2 note below.

## 29b: Marriage is between a man and a woman

**Panel comment (the floor), RATIFIED by Aaron 22 Sep 2026:**

> The Article confesses only what Genesis 2:24 and Matthew 19:4-6 confess: from the beginning God made them male and female, and marriage is the union of a man and a woman who become one flesh. This is the floor. It does not settle whether marriage is a sacrament; its permanence; its duration beyond death; or civil law and public policy. Each tradition fills those in its own house. The Restoration's fuller teaching (eternal marriage, D&C 132:19; "The Family: A Proclamation to the World") is Volume 2 witness and is not read onto the Article.

**Test at the floor (C4):** for each family, does any member church's adopted official position recognise or solemnise marriage between two people of the same sex? If so, that family does not count. A church's position on civil law is not its teaching on what marriage is, and it is not tested.

**Not imported:**
- eternal marriage;
- sacramental status;
- a position on civil marriage law;
- a teaching on the morality of same-sex attraction as inclination. Inclination is not action: the distinction the manuscript keeps.

**"A man and a woman" and number:** the floor reads the phrase as one man and one woman in the present teaching of each body. Historical practice is outside the floor, the Latter-day Saint history included, so the pilot scores current adopted positions only (Official Declaration 1 is the present Latter-day Saint text).

**Expected result (UNVERIFIED, and more than the disclosure draft now says):** the working draft counts three divided families: Anglican, Lutheran and Reformed. Methodist should be checked as a possible fourth. The United Methodist Church's 2024 General Conference changed its official language on marriage, while other Methodist bodies did not. If that holds, 29b is nearer 9 of 13 than 10. The "ten of thirteen" in paragraph 1 of the disclosure must not be carried forward until the pilot reports. Baptist bodies that confess no binding creed fall under F.8: no position is recorded as no position, never as divided (TPS G2).

## F.2 for AoID: RULED 22 Sep 2026 (AOID2-F2-GENRES, option 1, topic-only)

For AoID scoring, any text a competent body has adopted under B2 counts as that body's official position: creed, confession, catechism, authorised rite, canon or assembly statement. Unadopted writing by theologians or church presses is context only. Where two adopted texts of one body disagree, the body's own rules of precedence decide. If those rules are unclear, the cell goes to the review queue (C3). F.2 stays open in the codex for other topics, and the rule may be promoted to a principle after the 29a/29b pilot.
