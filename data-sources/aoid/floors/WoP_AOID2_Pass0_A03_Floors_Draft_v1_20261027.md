## Floors

### A3-F1. The Creator (s1)

> The Article confesses only what Isaiah 45:18 and Acts 17:24 confess: God Himself formed the earth and made it; He created it not in vain, He formed it to be inhabited; and God made the world and all things therein, being Lord of heaven and earth. This is the floor: God made this world, and made it with purpose, as a home for His creatures. It does not settle whether God created from nothing or organized what already existed, the manner or length of creation, or how Scripture's account of it is to be read; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Scoring risk: many families confess creation from nothing (*ex nihilo*); the Latter-day Saint canon teaches organization of existing matter. Neither differs from the floor, which is why the exclusion names the question.

### A3-F2. Agency given by God (s3)

> The Article confesses only what Deuteronomy 30:19 and Joshua 24:15 confess: I have set before you life and death, blessing and cursing: therefore choose life; choose you this day whom ye will serve. This is the floor: God has made human beings able to choose, and calls them to choose. It does not settle what the fall did to the will, whether fallen human beings can choose spiritual good without grace going before them, or how human choice and God's sovereignty are related; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the call to choose; theological inference for "divinely-endowed."

Scoring risk: Lutheran and Reformed confessions teach that the fallen will cannot choose spiritual good unaided (bondage of the will), while also teaching that God endued the will with natural liberty (for example Westminster 9.1). The scorer tests "able to choose and called to choose," not "free in spiritual things without grace."

### A3-F3. Good and evil met in this life (s4)

> The Article confesses only what Genesis 2:16-17, Romans 7:18-19 and James 1:14 confess: of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it; the good that I would I do not, but the evil which I would not, that I do; and every man is tempted when he is drawn away of his own lust, and enticed. This is the floor: in this life human beings meet good and evil, before them and within them. It does not settle the origin of evil, or whether opposition between good and evil is necessary to moral existence (2 Nephi 2:11, which stands as Restoration witness, not as the Article's claim); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

### A3-F4. Life is God's purpose, not an accident (s7, s12)

> The Article confesses only what Genesis 1:31, Ephesians 2:10 and Romans 8:28 confess: God saw every thing that He had made, and behold, it was very good; we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them; and all things work together for good to them that love God. This is the floor: human life in this world is God's purposeful work, not an accident, and He works in it for the good of those who love Him. It does not settle whether death and the hardships of mortality came as a penalty for sin (Genesis 3:17-19; Romans 5:12; Romans 6:23), which stand as Scripture states them, or how God's providence bears on human freedom and suffering (see A2-F3); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for "very good" and "workmanship"; theological inference for "not an accident."

What this floor cannot test: s7 also says life "is not a punishment." Read as "life itself is God's gift, not a sentence," most families would agree. Read as "the hardships of mortality are not a penalty for sin," it parts from confessions that teach death and the miseries of this life came as the wages of sin (for example Westminster 6.6; the Catholic Catechism on death as a consequence of sin), and from Genesis 3:17-19 and Romans 6:23 themselves. The floor tests the first reading and names the second in its exclusion. Disclosure: the count for s7 measures "not an accident"; "not a punishment" is read as life itself, not its pains. ART3-HOLDS keeps the wording; any change to it would reopen that ruling.

Romans 8:28 is already in s7's and s12's anchors and in A2-F2; it is reused here for s12's "for our benefit."

### A3-F5. The fall within God's purpose (s8)

> The Article confesses only what 1 Peter 1:19-20 and Romans 8:20 confess: Christ, as of a lamb without blemish and without spot, was foreordained before the foundation of the world; and the creation was made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of Him who hath subjected the same in hope. This is the floor: the fall did not take God by surprise or defeat His purpose; redemption in Christ was foreordained before it, and creation's subjection was made in hope. It does not settle whether God willed the fall or permitted it, whether it was necessary or fortunate (felix culpa; 2 Nephi 2:25, which stands as Restoration witness, not as the Article's claim), or how sin and death passed to Adam's descendants (Romans 5:12); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: theological inference (foreordained redemption implies a foreseen fall).

New anchors: 1 Peter 1:19-20 and Romans 8:20 are not in s8's anchors, which (Genesis 3:17-19, Romans 5:12, 1 Corinthians 15:22) show the fall's consequence, not its place in God's purpose. Adding them to the biblical column is additive and within ART3-HOLDS.

### A3-F6. Choose, fall, rise, be made new (s9)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 3:23, Galatians 6:1 and 2 Corinthians 5:17 confess: all have sinned and come short of the glory of God; if a man be overtaken in a fault, ye which are spiritual, restore such an one; and if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away, behold, all things are become new. This is the floor: all fall short; the fallen may be restored; and those who are in Christ are made new by His grace. It does not settle whether God's saving grace is extended to every soul or made effectual in the elect, how grace and the human will work together (see A3-F2), or how a believer who falls is restored (repentance, confession, the sacraments or ordinances); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for each clause; "every soul has room" is theological inference.

Scoring risk: "every soul has room ... to be made new" can be read against particular redemption (Canons of Dort, Second Head). The floor tests that the fallen may be restored and that those in Christ are made new; the exclusion names the extent question.

### A3-F7. Not abandoned (s11)

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 13:5 and Romans 8:35, 37 confess: I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee; who shall separate us from the love of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or sword? Nay, in all these things we are more than conquerors through Him that loved us. This is the floor: hardship in this life is not a sign that God has forsaken His children. It does not settle why God permits suffering, or who is meant by "His children" (see A11-F1); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Alternative: join s11 to A2-F2, which confesses Romans 8:38-39. Weakness: A2-F2 also tests God's goodness and the goodness of His purposes, which s11 does not claim (C4(a)(iii)).

### A3-F8. Shaped by the trials of this life (s5, s10)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 5:3-4, James 1:3-4 and Hebrews 12:11 confess: tribulation worketh patience, and patience experience, and experience hope; the trying of your faith worketh patience, that ye may be perfect and entire, wanting nothing; and no chastening for the present seemeth to be joyous, but grievous, nevertheless afterward it yieldeth the peaceable fruit of righteousness unto them which are exercised thereby. This is the floor: God uses the trials of this life to shape the character of His children. It does not settle whether mortal life is chiefly formation or chiefly probation, what perfection finally is, or how God's shaping and the soul's cooperation are related; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for s5 and s10.

s14 is not under this floor. Aaron's ruling of 27 Oct 2026 (AOID2-SPAN-CLASS-OBSERVATION): its intent is "to point out the simple truth that life shapes every individual, whether they believe in a Creator or not. This is pure observation." v1 of this draft read "the shaping" through s10 and s14's own anchors and panel comment as God's formative work, and so found a gap that was only in that reading; that analysis is withdrawn.

### A3-F9. Creation's witness, acknowledged or denied (s13)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 1:20 and Acts 14:17 confess: the invisible things of Him from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood by the things that are made, even His eternal power and Godhead, so that they are without excuse; and He left not Himself without witness, in that He did good, and gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons. This is the floor: creation bears witness to its Creator and His goodness, and each person may acknowledge that witness or suppress it. It does not settle how much of God can be known from creation without revelation; each tradition fills that in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Pastoral integrity: "We are free to either deny the Creator" names a freedom, not a permission. Romans 1:20's "without excuse" is quoted in the floor so the apostle's word stands beside the Article's.
