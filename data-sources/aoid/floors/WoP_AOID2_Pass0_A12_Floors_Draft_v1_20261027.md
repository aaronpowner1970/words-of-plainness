## Floors

### A12-F1. The grave is not the end; Christ's resurrection the promise and the proof (s1, s2)

> The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 15:19-20 and Job 19:25-26 confess: if in this life only we have hope in Christ, we are of all men most miserable; but now is Christ risen from the dead, and become the firstfruits of them that slept; I know that my redeemer liveth, and in my flesh shall I see God. This is the floor: this life is not all there is, death is not the end, and Christ's resurrection is both God's promise of ours and the proof of it. It does not settle the state of the dead between death and the resurrection, whether the soul lives consciously in that time, or the order and timing of the resurrection; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for 1 Corinthians 15:19-20 (firstfruits is both promise and proof); illustrative application for Job 19:25-26.

Scoring risk: the intermediate-state exclusion is load-bearing. Families that teach the sleep of the dead until the resurrection (UNVERIFIED: Adventist; Jehovah's Witnesses) still confess that the grave is not the end, because the dead will rise; without the exclusion they would be read against "this life is not all there is" on a point the span does not make.

Precedent: A5's floor on Christ's own bodily resurrection leaves "the glory of His risen body ... beyond what Scripture states" open; this floor takes His resurrection as the firstfruits and says nothing about its manner.

### A12-F2. Sown in sorrow, raised in glory (s3)

> The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 15:42-44 and Psalm 126:5 confess: so also is the resurrection of the dead; it is sown in dishonour; it is raised in glory: it is sown in weakness; it is raised in power; and they that sow in tears shall reap in joy. This is the floor: what is sown in weakness and sorrow in this life is raised in glory. It does not settle who is raised to glory and who to judgment, the nature of the glorified state, deification or exaltation, or degrees of glory; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the resurrection body (1 Corinthians 15:43); illustrative application for Psalm 126:5, from which the Article draws "sorrow."

Precedent: "the nature of the glorified state, deification or exaltation, degrees of glory" is copied from A6-F4.

### A12-F3. What is given to Him is not lost (s4)

> The Article confesses only what John 6:39 and 2 Timothy 1:12 confess: of all which the Father hath given me I should lose nothing, but should raise it up again at the last day; and I know whom I have believed, and am persuaded that he is able to keep that which I have committed unto him against that day. This is the floor: what is given to Christ, He keeps, and none of it is lost. It does not settle whether a true believer can finally fall away, or who are those the Father gives the Son; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Precedent: "whether a true believer can finally fall away" from A11-F8. The floor keeps the Article's own words, "given to Him," and the exclusion keeps both Reformed perseverance and Arminian teaching that a believer may fall away from being read against it: each confesses that what Christ keeps is not lost, and they differ on who may withdraw from His keeping.

Scoring risk: "who are those the Father gives the Son" is the election question (UNVERIFIED: Reformed and Lutheran texts on predestination). The exclusion keeps it out.

### A12-F4. Every soul raised to give account; nothing forgotten (s5, s6)

> The Article confesses only what John 5:28-29, 2 Corinthians 5:10, Romans 14:12, 1 Corinthians 13:12 and Psalm 56:8 with Malachi 3:16 confess: all that are in the graves shall hear his voice, and shall come forth; we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ; every one of us shall give account of himself to God; then shall I know even as also I am known; put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?; and a book of remembrance was written before him for them that feared the LORD. This is the floor: every soul will be raised and will stand before God, each will give an honest account of his own life and will know as he is known, and nothing of our tears or our joys is forgotten by Him. It does not settle the order and timing of the resurrections, the form the judgment takes, what follows it for each soul, whether any punishment is everlasting, or degrees of glory; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for the resurrection of all, the judgment and the account (John 5:28-29; 2 Corinthians 5:10; Romans 14:12); direct for knowing as we are known (1 Corinthians 13:12); illustrative application for the tears in God's bottle and the book of remembrance, from which the Article draws "every tear and smile will be remembered."

Precedent: "the form the judgment takes" matches s7's own restraint; P-F1 floors 1 Corinthians 13:12 for what we now know in part; A6-F8 floors that the final judgment of every soul is Christ's.

Anchor addition proposed: John 5:28-29 at s5. "Every soul will be raised" now rests on Revelation 20:12-13; John 5:28-29 is Christ's own word that all in the graves come forth.

Scoring risk: "every soul will be raised." Jehovah's Witnesses' adopted teaching holds that some will not be resurrected (UNVERIFIED as to the deciding text); the cell may DIFFER. The floor keeps the span's "every soul" and is not narrowed. Families teaching that the wicked are raised and then destroyed (UNVERIFIED: Adventist) still confess that every soul is raised; the exclusion "whether any punishment is everlasting" keeps that question out.

### A12-F5. God is faithful; Christ has spoken, and it is enough (s8, s10)

> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 10:23, Lamentations 3:22-23, Numbers 23:19 and 2 Corinthians 1:20 confess: he is faithful that promised; his compassions fail not, they are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness; God is not a man, that he should lie; and all the promises of God in him are yea, and in him Amen. This is the floor: God is faithful and keeps every promise; what Christ has spoken can be trusted, and His word is enough to ground our hope. It does not settle how His word is known to us, whether by Scripture alone, with Tradition, or with continuing revelation; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for God's faithfulness and His promises in Christ; theological inference for "this is enough for us," drawn from "yea, and Amen."

Precedent: the exclusion follows A4-F5 and A9-F10 on the means by which His word is known, so "enough" is not read as a claim about the sufficiency of Scripture.

### A12-F6. Made to fellowship with Him in His glory (s9)

> The Article confesses only what John 17:22, 24 and 1 John 1:3 confess: the glory which thou gavest me I have given them; Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am, that they may behold my glory; and truly our fellowship is with the Father, and with his Son Jesus Christ. This is the floor: God made us for fellowship with Him, and Christ wills that His own be with Him and behold His glory. It does not settle the nature of the glorified state, deification or exaltation, degrees of glory, or where the redeemed dwell; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for Christ's will that His own share and behold His glory, and for fellowship with the Father and the Son; theological inference for "made to," drawn from the Son's will for those the Father gave Him.

Precedent: the exclusion is A6-F4's, word for word. A7-F4 floors fellowship begun now; this floor is its fulness.

Anchor addition proposed: 1 John 1:3 at s9, for "fellowship," which the present anchors (John 17:3, 17:22-24) do not name.

## Judgment call 1: s7, "What form that accounting will take, and what glories await, we leave to God"

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Posture (recommended).** The classification test's own worked example of posture is "neutrality on heavenly hierarchy": a restraint the ministry chooses, with no command behind it. What glories await, and the form of the accounting, are exactly that: the Articles leave degrees of glory and the manner of judgment to each house (Volume 2 will speak the Restoration's fuller witness). The commanded restraint, leaving the judgment of souls to God, is already measured at P-F8 and A6-F8. Weakness: Deuteronomy 29:29 and 1 Corinthians 2:9 are anchored here, and a reader may feel that "we leave to God" is itself Scripture's teaching; that teaching is measured at P-F1.
2. **Teaching, joined to P-F1** ("what God has not revealed is His"). Weakness: s7 is narrower than P-F1 and is the ministry's chosen silence on particular questions, not a claim about what God has or has not revealed; joining would score the Article's neutrality as though it were a doctrine every house must confess.
3. **Teaching, new floor** (1 Corinthians 2:9: "eye hath not seen ... the things which God hath prepared"). Weakness: it would imply a claim that nothing is revealed about those glories, which the Restoration witness, among others, does not hold; a floor that rules one way on an open question breaches A4(g).

## Judgment call 2: s6 joined to A12-F4

Candidates, ranked:
1. **Joined to A12-F4 (recommended).** "Every tear and smile will be remembered" belongs to the accounting of s5; the floor states it as "nothing of our tears or our joys is forgotten by Him," with Psalm 56:8 and Malachi 3:16. Weakness: the span's poetry ("smile") has no verse of its own; the floor carries it by inference.
2. **Its own floor.** Weakness: few adopted texts speak of God remembering our tears and joys as a class; under C4(a)(ii) it would likely fall short through silence, a floor built to go unconfessed.
3. **Descriptive.** Weakness: it is not an observation about the human condition or a description of the churches; it is a claim about God's remembrance, and calling it descriptive would misclassify it.
