## The classification test used

Drawn from the three confirmed precedents, stated here so it can be checked:

- **Teaching**: the span makes a claim about God, Christ, Scripture or humanity, or obeys a plain command of Christ or the apostles. A family can be asked whether it teaches it (C4).
- **Posture**: the span states the ministry's own method or restraint, a choice it makes about how it speaks, which no church is asked to teach (A2 s7 "we remain carefully neutral"; A4 s15 "we contend for none of these boundaries").
- **Descriptive**: the span describes the churches or the document itself (A4 s12 "each faith tradition recognizes its own canon"). Amended 27 Oct 2026 (AOID2-SPAN-CLASS-OBSERVATION): a plain observation of the human condition, which needs no revelation and which no church confesses or denies, is also descriptive (A3 s14).

The line between teaching and posture: a restraint is teaching when it is itself commanded (leaving final judgment to God, 1 Corinthians 4:5), and posture when it is the ministry's chosen method with no command behind it (neutrality on heavenly hierarchy). If Aaron accepts this test it is a refinement of A4 and should be filed against the codex; it is not filed here.

## Floors

### P-F1. What we know now, and what God keeps (s1, s3, s5, s9, s10)

> The Article confesses only what Deuteronomy 29:29 and 1 Corinthians 13:9-12 confess: the secret things belong unto the LORD our God, but those things which are revealed belong unto us and to our children for ever, that we may do all the words of His law; and now we know in part and prophesy in part, and see through a glass, darkly, but then face to face, and then shall we know even as also we are known. This is the floor: in this life our knowledge is partial; what God has not revealed is His, and what He has revealed is given to us to do; fuller knowledge waits for the day when we shall know as we are known. It does not settle what "that which is perfect" is or when it comes, which things God has revealed and which He keeps, how far He is known by nature, by Scripture, by the Church's teaching or by continuing revelation, or whether any creature will come to know God fully (see A2-F5); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness (A5(a)): direct for s5, s9, s10; theological inference for s1 ("knowledge of truth" is wider than Paul's "knowledge" and "prophecy") and s3 (if all know in part, none has charted heaven whole).

Alternative: join s1, s5, s9, s10 to A2-F5, which already confesses Deuteronomy 29:29 and 1 Corinthians 13:9, 12 and is scored. Weakness: A2-F5's floor sentence is about God's nature; the Prologue's claim is about truth and heaven generally, so A2-F5's count would not measure these spans' claim (C4(a)(iii)). Weakness of P-F1: near-duplicate verses scored twice.

Scoring risk: families whose adopted texts or rites describe the orders of heaven (angelic ranks in Eastern and Catholic liturgy and tradition; the degrees of glory in the Latter-day Saint canon) do not claim to have charted heaven whole, so s3 should not divide them; the scorer should test "full ranges," not "any knowledge of heaven."

### P-F2. The bounds God has set (s4)

**Article text change APPROVED by Aaron, 27 Oct 2026, 03:27 EDT (CHAT-AOID2-64a).** Aaron's reply: "Approve s4 as I wrote it". The wording is Aaron's own.

Live (First Edition, unchanged by step 75):
> We firmly maintain that any souls who profess such knowledge, even basing themselves in scripture, are speculating beyond the bounds of wisdom God has set.

Approved (Second Edition):
> We firmly maintain that any souls who profess such knowledge, inferring beyond what the Lord has plainly revealed, are speculating beyond the bounds of wisdom God has set.

Why: read plainly, the live sentence pronounced on every house holding adopted teaching on heaven's order (Eastern and Catholic angelic ranks; D&C 76 and 131; Volume 2), which A4(g) forbids, and a floor exclusion cannot undo the Article's own words. The approved wording aims at inference past revelation, so each house stands on what it holds as revealed. The repeated "beyond" is deliberate: inferring beyond revelation is speculating beyond wisdom. This reopens the step 75 approval (Prologue body unchanged) for this one span; it is an Article-text edit for the Gate 1 freeze. The span text in `a01.njk` and `apparatusData.json` (A01 s4 is the span "speculating beyond the bounds of wisdom God has set", whose wording does not change) and any Prologue narration or film are to be checked before a Code session applies it.

Draft floor, revised to follow the approved text (not yet ratified):

> The Article confesses only what Deuteronomy 29:29 and Proverbs 3:5-7 confess: the secret things belong unto the LORD our God; trust in the LORD with all thine heart, and lean not unto thine own understanding; be not wise in thine own eyes. This is the floor: what God has not revealed is His, and to infer beyond what He has plainly revealed is not wisdom. It does not settle which teachings about heaven, its inhabitants or its order God has revealed; each tradition holds its own revelation, liturgy and teaching on these in its own house, and this floor rules on none of them.

Superseded draft wording (v1, 27 Oct 2026, never ratified): "This is the floor: what God has not revealed is His, and to lean on our own understanding beyond what He has given is not wisdom."

Kind of witness: direct (Proverbs 3:7) with theological inference for "bounds."

The exclusion is now a safeguard rather than a repair: the approved text no longer pronounces on any house, and the floor still rules on none of them (A4(g)).

Alternative: classify s4 as posture (it is introduced by "We firmly maintain"). Weakness: the span's own words make a claim about God ("bounds of wisdom God has set"), which the test above calls teaching.

### P-F3. The weightier matters (s6)

> The Article confesses only what Matthew 23:23 and Luke 11:42 confess: Christ rebuked those who tithed mint and anise and cummin and omitted the weightier matters of the law, judgment, mercy, and faith, and said, these ought ye to have done, and not to leave the other undone. This is the floor: Christ named justice, mercy and faithfulness the weightier matters of the law, and He did not release the lesser. It does not settle which commands of the law of Moses bind Christians, or how justice, mercy and faith are ordered in the Christian life; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct. The Greek *pistis* is rendered faith or faithfulness; the apparatus carries the range.

Pastoral integrity: the Prologue says "We constrain this ministry to the weightier matters." That is the ministry's scope, not a release from the rest of Christ's commands; the floor keeps His "not to leave the other undone" standing beside it. A6's ratified floor already confesses that clause of Matthew 23:23 with Romans 11:33; P-F3 tests the weightier-matters claim on its own.

### P-F4. Plain for our salvation (s17)

> The Article confesses only what Deuteronomy 30:11-14 and 2 Corinthians 3:12 confess: the commandment God gives is not hidden, neither is it far off, but the word is very nigh, in thy mouth and in thy heart, that thou mayest do it; and the apostles, having such hope, used great plainness of speech. This is the floor: what God has made known for our salvation He has made plain and near, not hidden or far off. It does not settle whether Scripture is clear in itself apart from the Church's reading of it, by what means that plainness is received (Scripture, the Church, the Spirit, or living prophets), or which teachings are the weighty things; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for "not hidden ... very nigh"; theological inference for "for our salvation."

Scoring risk: "plain" can be read as the Protestant perspicuity of Scripture, which Catholic and Orthodox teaching on the Church's authentic interpretation does not share in that form. The floor does not ask for perspicuity, and the exclusion says so; the scorer should test "God has made the saving word near and knowable," not "Scripture interprets itself."

### P-F5. Not barred for want of perfect understanding (s12)

> The Article confesses only what Romans 10:9-13 and Luke 23:42-43 confess: whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved; and to a dying man who asked only, Lord, remember me when thou comest into thy kingdom, Christ said, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise. This is the floor: Christ receives those who call on Him in faith, and salvation in Him is not withheld for want of a perfect understanding. It does not settle what faith, confession, repentance and ordinances Christ requires of those able to receive them, which stand where the Articles set them (Article 11); the state of those who never hear of Him; or whether the thief's case is a norm or an exception; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct for Romans 10:13; theological inference for the floor's second clause (the thief's understanding was small and his reception sure).

Pastoral integrity: nothing here loosens Christ's command to be baptized; the exclusion points to Article 11, where it is set plainly.

Scoring risk (highest in the Prologue): the Athanasian Creed, adopted in the Lutheran Book of Concord, the Thirty-nine Articles (VIII) and Catholic tradition, opens "Whosoever will be saved: before all things it is necessary that he hold the Catholic Faith." Whether a text requiring that the faith be held differs from a floor about perfect understanding is a C4(a)(i) judgment the scorer must make and quote, not assume either way.

### P-F6. Believe also in me (s13)

> The Article confesses only what John 14:1 confesses: Christ said to His disciples, Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me. This is the floor: Christ calls those who believe in God to believe also in Him. It does not settle the relation of the Son to the Father, or what belief in Christ includes beyond trust in Him; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct. Restoration anchor 3 Nephi 11:32 stays amplifying witness.

### P-F7. Love that keeps His commandments (s14)

> The Article confesses only what John 14:15 and John 14:21 confess: If ye love me, keep my commandments; he that hath my commandments, and keepeth them, he it is that loveth me. This is the floor: love for Christ is shown in keeping His commandments. It does not settle which commandments bind and how, or whether keeping them bears on merit or final salvation (see A6-F7); each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct.

Alternative: join s14 to the ratified A11 floor that confesses John 14:15 with John 15:14, Ephesians 2:8-9 and Galatians 5:6. Weakness: that floor tests four claims, three of which s14 does not make (C4(a)(iii)).

### P-F8. Judgment we leave to God (s16)

> The Article confesses only what 1 Corinthians 4:5 and Romans 14:10-12 confess: judge nothing before the time, until the Lord come, who will bring to light the hidden things of darkness and make manifest the counsels of the hearts; for we shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ, and every one of us shall give account of himself to God. This is the floor: final judgment of a soul belongs to God, and it is not ours to pronounce it on another. It does not settle a church's discipline of its own members or its teaching on right and wrong (Matthew 18:15-17; 1 Corinthians 5:12-13), which stand; or the manner and timing of the judgment; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Kind of witness: direct. The "mysteries" half of the span is carried by P-F1 (s5).

Why teaching, not posture: "we leave to God" is a restraint, like A2 s7, but this restraint is commanded (1 Corinthians 4:5; Matthew 7:1), so it is obedience a family can be asked to teach.

## Posture and descriptive items (not scored)

- **s2** "we neither reject nor disrespect religious institutions, traditions, or their formalized creeds and theologies": the ministry's stance toward other houses, introduced "In this interfaith ministry." Romans 14 is a command about receiving fellow believers within the church, not about honoring other traditions' creeds, so no command stands behind it. Same status as A4 s15.
- **s7** "merognosis": the ministry's name for its own stance, used in the first person only; it is never applied as a label to other believers. Its content is measured under P-F1.
- **s8** "sophistries of gnosticism, agnosticism, skepticism, atheism, and any '-ism' that divides faith in Christ": what the ministry avoids. Scoring it would ask families to condemn named positions and an open class ("any '-ism'") no adopted text can be tested against. Alternative: teaching under Colossians 2:8 ("beware lest any man spoil you through philosophy and vain deceit ... and not after Christ"). Weakness: the floor would claim far less than the span names.
- **s11** "we will not bind the conscience of any fellow believer with doctrines built from our own speculation or from the theologies of others": the ministry's method, the same restraint as A2 s7. It keeps the Articles from deciding; it does not ask any family to stop teaching its confession as binding.
- **s18** "He never said 'be sure to sort out every theological question before you love and accept one another'": an argument from Christ's silence, which no adopted text can confirm or deny; its existing panel comment names that exposure itself. Christ's positive command is measured under L11 (John 13:34-35) and through s19. Alternative: teaching, joined to L11. Weakness: L11 tests the command to love, not the claim that doctrinal agreement is no precondition of it, and a family with closed communion could be miscounted either way.
- **Boundary sentence (Second Edition)**: "The one line these Articles draw for fellowship among fellow believers is the confession of Christ's name (1 Corinthians 12:3; 1 John 4:2), and it closes no door ...": the Articles' own fellowship line, a method. Its 1 Corinthians 12:3 teaching is measured under R4, whose floor already "connects to the one boundary the Prologue names." Alternatives: (2) a new floor on 1 Corinthians 12:3 and 1 John 4:2, weakness: duplicates R4 and tests a fellowship rule; (3) join R4, weakness: R4 does not carry 1 John 4:2. Note: 1 John 4:2 is then floored nowhere.
- **Edition note with the three-principles sentence (Second Edition)**: describes the edition and states the ministry's principles. Descriptive and posture; its measurement claims are answered by the disclosures under C4 and B4.
