## 1. Full Article text

New text is marked with ▶ in the left margin. Everything else is the live text of `src/_includes/aoid/a11.njk` as read on 12 Oct 2026, unchanged.

### Of Covenants and Commitments

**We are** children of God by birth. **We become** disciples of Christ by covenant. Jesus said plainly: "If you love me, keep my commandments," and again "You are my friends if you do what I command." This does not mean that we save ourselves through perfect obedience and good works. Rather, obedience is an expression of love.

Recognizing that the discipline required to be a "disciple" is not automatic, and it is not easy, **we labor to surrender** our will into His and then live it. Not merely profess it. Not merely believe it. Live it — in the body, in the home, in the world, every day of the week, as reality. No soul finishes in a day, and He is patient with our becoming. Jesus called together the multitude and said, "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me." We hope to live up to the Lord's expectations. **We embrace** His name as our central identity, staking our lives on Him. Whenever we sin and fall short of the glory of God, we repent sincerely and strive to sin no more.

▶ Christ's disciples today are not all of one mind on sexual holiness, yet the word of God on it is as plain now as it was then. In the body, **we receive** sexual union as a holy gift whose rightful place is marriage, the union of a man and a woman who become one flesh, and outside marriage we live chastely.†

▶ In the home, **we hold** marriage in honor among all, for what God hath joined together, let not man put asunder.

▶ In the world, though not of the world, **we render** to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's. We honor those in authority and pray for them; where their commands contradict God's, we obey God rather than men. **We work** with our own hands, as we are able, that we may eat our own bread and have something to give to those in need.

**We accept** that Christ chose to be baptized to fulfill all righteousness, and instituted ordinances to remember Him always. ▶ On the night He was betrayed, He took bread and the cup and said, "This do in remembrance of me," and as often as His disciples eat and drink it, they show the Lord's death till He come. ◀ Each religious institution formalizes these ordinances and sacraments for their own membership. Still, the Lord receives all who sincerely come to Him in His name and denies none — black and white, bond and free, male and female; He remembereth the heathen, and to Him all are alike, both Jew and Gentile.

**We recognize** that His apostles were sent into the world and taught, "Be ye doers of the word, and not hearers only." Those who feel the Spirit of Truth in their teachings and begin to believe in Christ always ask, "What shall we do?" The answer was as plain then as it is now: "Repent and be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost." Each disciple must feel after God's Spirit for what it means to be "born of the water and the Spirit" and how to enter into covenants with Christ.

**We walk** the path Christ set before us — Himself being the way, the truth, and the life. **We pray** always and "faint not." **We endure** in faith through joy and trials as God refines our new identity into mature discipleship over a lifetime of spiritual sanctification by the power of His Spirit. **We seek** to enter into God's Kingdom, which is at hand — now, in this moment — for those who have eyes to see, ears to hear, and willing hearts.

> *Reflection:* What covenant or commitment do you most readily profess — and find hardest to actually live?

▶ † This teaching is set down as biblically anchored witness rather than as common ground. Of sixteen Christian traditions, measured by their own adopted texts, eleven teach that sexual relations belong within marriage, and ten that marriage is between a man and a woman; four are divided within themselves on the first and five on the second, and one holds no common position. That falls short of the more-than-four-in-five standard these Articles keep. See [*Of Sexual Holiness: A Disclosure of Division*](/aoid/disclosures/sexual-holiness).

## 4. Floors to transcribe (verbatim from the ratified floor documents)

### 29a — `floors/WoP_AOID2_ScopeNotes_29a_29b_Draft_20260922.md`
> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 13:4 and 1 Thessalonians 4:3-7 confess: sexual union is a holy gift whose rightful place is marriage, and outside marriage the disciple keeps chastity. This is the floor, and it is received across historic Christian teaching. It does not settle who may marry (see item 29b); whether marriage is a sacrament; its permanence, divorce or remarriage; the purposes of marital intimacy beyond its holiness; or questions of contraception. Each tradition fills those in its own house. The Restoration's fuller teaching (the law of chastity as covenant; D&C 42:22; Jacob 2:28) is amplifying witness, not the Article's claim.

### 29b — same document
> The Article confesses only what Genesis 2:24 and Matthew 19:4-6 confess: from the beginning God made them male and female, and marriage is the union of a man and a woman who become one flesh. This is the floor. It does not settle whether marriage is a sacrament; its permanence; its duration beyond death; or civil law and public policy. Each tradition fills those in its own house. The Restoration's fuller teaching (eternal marriage, D&C 132:19; "The Family: A Proclamation to the World") is Volume 2 witness and is not read onto the Article.

### G7 — `floors/WoP_AOID2_ScopeNotes_A04_A05_Draft_20260922.md`
> The Article confesses only what Hebrews 13:4 and Genesis 2:24 with Matthew 19:6 confess: marriage is to be held in honor among all, and what God has joined together, let no man put asunder. This is the floor. It does not settle who may marry (item 29b), the boundary of sexual relations (item 29a), whether marriage is a sacrament, divorce and remarriage, or marriage beyond death; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### L9 — `floors/WoP_AOID2_ScopeNotes_A14_Love_Draft_20260922.md`
> The Article confesses only what Mark 12:17, Romans 13:1, 7 and 1 Peter 2:13-17 confess: disciples render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's and to God the things that are God's, give honor and what is due to those in authority, and pray for them; and where men's commands contradict God's, we ought to obey God rather than men (Acts 5:29). This is the floor. It does not settle when disobedience or resistance is right, oaths, military service, or the relation of church and state; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### L10 — same document
> The Article confesses only what 2 Thessalonians 3:10-12, 1 Thessalonians 4:11-12 and Ephesians 4:28 confess: disciples work with their own hands, eat their own bread, and labor that they may have something to give to him that needeth. This is the floor. It does not settle economic systems, public or church welfare, or the obligations of those who cannot work; each tradition fills those in its own house.

### G6 — `floors/WoP_AOID2_ScopeNotes_A04_A05_Draft_20260922.md`
> The Article confesses only what Luke 22:19-20 and 1 Corinthians 11:23-26 confess: on the night He was betrayed, Christ took bread and the cup and said, "this do in remembrance of me," and as often as His disciples eat and drink it they show the Lord's death till He come. This is the floor. It does not settle the manner of Christ's presence, whether it is a sacrament or an ordinance, who may preside or partake, how often it is kept, or which elements are used; each tradition fills those in its own house.
