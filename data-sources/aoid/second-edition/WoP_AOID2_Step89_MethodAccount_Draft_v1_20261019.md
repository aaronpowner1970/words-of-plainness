## How These Articles Were Measured

**In short.** Every teaching these Articles set down as shared was checked against sixteen Christian traditions, by reading each tradition's own adopted texts. A teaching is set down as held in common only when at least thirteen of the sixteen teach it. Where a teaching falls short, the Article says so, and says why.

### What we read

We did not poll believers or ask what most members think. We read what each church has formally adopted as its own teaching: its creeds, confessions, catechisms, rites of worship and official statements. In those texts a church speaks for itself, and we have tried to let it.

### The sixteen traditions

Each tradition is counted as a family: a church, or a group of churches, whose adopted texts can be read. The families are named as they appear on *The Living Tree of Christ-Believing Traditions*: Catholic; Eastern Orthodox; Lutheran; Anglican; Reformed; Methodist; Baptist; Pentecostal (with the charismatic churches); Stone-Campbell; Anabaptist; Adventist; The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints; Jehovah's Witnesses; Oriental Orthodox; Church of the East; and Holiness.

The list reaches beyond the historic creeds on purpose, because these Articles are offered to everyone who confesses Jesus' name. It includes two families outside the Nicene confession, The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (this ministry's own faith) and the Jehovah's Witnesses, and one family that holds no creed by conviction, the Stone-Campbell churches.

The Living Tree shows twenty-five branches. Sixteen are measured as families. The charismatic branch is counted with the Pentecostal family. Evangelicalism and Dispensationalism are movements, and their churches are counted in the families they belong to. Six branches stay outside the count because they have no adopted texts this method can read: the non-denominational churches, Messianic Jews, African Initiated Churches, the Chinese house churches, independent new religious movements, and local and regional expressions. Leaving them out says nothing about their faith. It says only what this method can measure.

### The standard

A teaching is set down as held in common when more than four in five of the sixteen teach it, which means at least thirteen.

### The three numbers

Every measured teaching carries three numbers: taught in so many, contradicted in so many, no position in so many, of sixteen.

- **Taught:** at least one church of the family has adopted a text that makes the same claim, in its own words, and no church of the family teaches otherwise.
- **Contradicted:** a church of the family has adopted a text that says otherwise. That church is named.
- **No position:** no church of the family has adopted a text on the point, either way.

Only the first number counts toward thirteen.

### Silence is not disagreement

A church that has never adopted a text on a question has not rejected it. "No position" is never counted, reported or described as disagreement. When a teaching falls short only because too few traditions have spoken to it, we say it is not confessed in enough traditions' adopted texts. We never call it disputed, divided or rejected.

### What "taught" means, read plainly

A family is made up of many churches, and they do not all adopt texts on every question. The churches that have spoken decide for the family. So "taught" means that at least one church of the family teaches it in an adopted text, and no church of the family was found to teach otherwise. The reverse also holds: one church's adopted text can divide a whole family. Every disclosure names the churches whose texts decided a family, and says that the others were silent or not read.

### When a teaching falls short

A few teachings fall short of thirteen and are kept anyway, because the Article's own witness rests on them. They are marked with a dagger (†) and set down as biblically anchored witness rather than as common ground. Each has its own disclosure that shows the count and explains it.

### Readings still being weighed

Where a church's text could fairly be read more than one way, the reading goes to a review queue and is not counted until it is decided. Families still under review are named beside the count, and every count carries the date it was measured.

### How quotations were checked

Every quotation from a church's text, and every passage of scripture quoted in these Articles, was read from its publisher by two separate means and compared word for word. Texts were checked against two publishers wherever two could be found. For the scriptures of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the Church's own website is the authority.

### If we have it wrong

If your church has adopted a text we have misread or missed, please [tell us](/aoid/corrections/). Every correction is read, and any change to a count is decided openly and recorded with its date and reason.
