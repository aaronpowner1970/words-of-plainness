## R1. The 1662 sources line (wherever the Anglican cells cite the Book of Common Prayer 1662)

Book of Common Prayer (1662): the Annexed Book edition (Eyre and Spottiswoode, 1892), checked where read against John Field's Cambridge printing of 1662, and the Church of England's online text. Eyre and Spottiswoode were the Queen's Printers, who also held the Crown patent behind the Church of England's later printed text.

## R2. The Bill and Barker variant (Anglican note for A6-F6 and A11-F7)

One 1662 printing (Bill and Barker, London) reads "of water and the Holy Ghost," without the second "of." The Annexed Book and the other editions read as quoted. The count is unaffected.

## R3. Support marks (Anglican support lines)

Confirmation prayer: Supporting line; one edition (Howell's transcription); not found in the printed edition read.

Sanctus: Supporting line; printed edition, read from its text layer only.

## R4. G8, Oriental Orthodox queue note

Still being confirmed. The Syriac Orthodox order of baptism (Samuel, 1974) carries the line in one English edition. It would be settled by a reading of the Syriac original, by the Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church's adoption of its English order, or by a second edition of the Armenian font prayer.

## R5. A11-F2, Oriental Orthodox entry (CONDITIONAL)

Teaches, decided by the Coptic Orthodox Church. Its prayer over the baptismal water recalls Christ's command, "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them." Sources: *Rites of the Holy Baptism* (Fr. Jacob Nadian, St. Bishoy Coptic Orthodox Church, Canada), a parish edition naming Pope Tawadros II; Denzinger, *Ritus Orientalium* (Würzburg, 1863), p. 204, a scholarly Latin edition. The Armenian font prayer gives the same command (Conybeare, *Rituale Armenorum*, Oxford, 1905, p. 95; one edition). Other churches of the family were silent or not read.

Condition: the build run first re-reads the prayer over the baptismal water in the Nadian PDF and Denzinger p. 204. If the quoted command is not there word for word in Nadian (and in sense in Denzinger's Latin), R5 is not built and the run reports.
