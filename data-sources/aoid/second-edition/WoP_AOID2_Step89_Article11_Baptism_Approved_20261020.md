## 3. "What the measurement found", Article 11 (replaces the two paragraphs named)

**Born of water and of the Spirit.** Christ was baptized "to fulfil all righteousness" (Matthew 3:15). He commanded His apostles to teach all nations, baptizing them (Matthew 28:19), and those who gladly received the word were baptized (Acts 2:41). Christ's baptism and His command to baptize are both held in common by the standard these Articles keep. [Families named from the record] confess in their own adopted texts that Christ ordained baptism with water, and [those named from the record] join it to the new birth He spoke of: "Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God" (John 3:5). Others confess the new birth as the Spirit's work and baptism as its sign; the texts that decide the Reformed, Baptist and Holiness counts on John 3:5 speak of the Spirit's work, and the Reformed and Baptist readings are marked borderline. The measurement counts what the churches confess. It does not settle what "water" signifies, or how baptism is related to the new birth. That is not leave to set either aside. Christ's example and His command stand, and every church and every believer is asked to search their own doctrine, pray for wisdom, and follow the Spirit of God in them. The Article's companion verse, that we should seek the Lord and feel after Him (Acts 17:27), was found in the adopted texts of the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches; not every family's texts were searched for it.

**The Salvation Army.** The Salvation Army, counted with the Holiness churches, does not practise water baptism or the Lord's Supper. In its own words, "No particular outward observance is necessary to inward grace," and "Christ is the one true Sacrament." Its official Handbook of Doctrine confesses Christ's command to make disciples, His baptism by John, and the gift of the Spirit to those who repent and believe. The Article does not settle whether baptism and the Supper are sacraments or ordinances, or who receives them, so the Army is not counted as disagreeing. Its practice is reported here as it is, beside Christ's example and His command.
