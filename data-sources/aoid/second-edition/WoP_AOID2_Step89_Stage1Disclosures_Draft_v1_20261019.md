## Article 6, Of Salvation

Status: APPROVED by Aaron 19 Oct 2026, 20:20 ET ("Approve Article 6"). BUILD TEXT, subject to the two [VERIFY] items being confirmed by the build run before going live.

### What the measurement found

All nine teachings measured in this Article are held in common by the standard these Articles keep, and no tradition's adopted texts were found to teach otherwise on any of them. Each teaching's count stands beside it, and the full record is on the Record tab. Some of that agreement is plainer than the rest, and these notes say where.

**Created, conformed, glorified.** The teaching that we were created in God's image, are being conformed to the image of His Son, and will be glorified with Him is held in common chiefly through its first part, our creation in God's image (Genesis 1:27). The rest, being conformed to Christ and glorified with Him, is confessed in their own words by about nine or ten of the sixteen traditions. What glorification finally is, the Article leaves to each tradition. The Eastern Orthodox teaching of theosis was not overlooked; it was not measured, because it answers a question the Article leaves open.

**Known in part.** Several traditions confess that God is incomprehensible, or a mystery beyond our understanding. We read that as confessing that, in this life, we know in part, and each such reading is marked borderline in the record.

**Promises some churches confine to part of their people.** Some churches confess a teaching but hold that it is fulfilled in one part of their people in one way, and in others in another. The Jehovah's Witnesses hold parts of these teachings to belong in full to the anointed 144,000, with the great crowd living forever on the earth. Such readings count, because the Article does not settle who receives a promise, or where, or how. Each is marked borderline, with its restriction named. [VERIFY: which A6 floors carry the Latter-day Saint reading; the review note names A6-F4 only.]

**The Jehovah's Witnesses' footing.** Their teaching is set in publications approved by their Governing Body, counted as the family's adopted teaching for now, while the rule for traditions without creeds is still being settled.

**The Stone-Campbell churches.** These churches hold no binding creed, by conviction. Their General Assembly's resolutions are offered for the consideration of their members and congregations and do not bind them (General Assembly Special Rules 2.5.1 and 2.5.6). Where they have no adopted text on a point, that is their conviction against binding creeds, not disagreement, and it marks a limit of measuring by adopted texts. Their members live by *The Design for the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)* and its Preamble, and by believer's baptism after the Good Confession of Jesus as the Christ, which is how the family lives out "repent and believe." On entrance to the Kingdom through Christ alone, the 1987 General Assembly referred a resolution that would have confessed Christ as the only Savior (Resolution 8728) to its Commission on Theology and Christian Unity, on the recommendation of its General Board, which gave as the first of its reasons the church's rejection of creedal statements as tests of faith. Their silence there is that conviction, not doubt that Christ is the only way. On grace and the Atonement as the ground of salvation, and on the disciple's charge, two paragraphs of the Design are counted, each marked borderline.

**Judgment belongs to Christ.** This teaching was measured on its floor alone: that judgment belongs to Christ. No tradition was found to claim His judgment seat in its own confession. The Article's fuller words, that we judge no soul to be saved or condemned in His place, were not measured; they stand as this ministry's own conviction.

## Article 11, Of Covenants and Commitments

Status: APPROVED by Aaron 19 Oct 2026, 20:24 ET ("Approve Article 11"). BUILD TEXT, subject to the three [VERIFY] items being confirmed by the build run before going live, and the [UPDATE] after Stage 2.

### What the measurement found

Besides sexual holiness, which has its own disclosure above, eight teachings of this Article were measured. All eight are held in common by the standard these Articles keep, and no tradition's adopted texts were found to teach otherwise on any of them. Where agreement rests on part of a teaching, or on a church's own way of reading it, these notes say so.

**God's offspring.** Some traditions confess that we are God's offspring in the words of Acts 17:28 itself: the Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Lutheran and Reformed churches, and the Methodist through Wesley's Notes. The others confess God as the maker of every person and the giver of each one's life and breath, and we count that as the same claim; six such readings are marked borderline. The Stone-Campbell churches have no adopted text on the point.

**Disciples by covenant.** Agreement here rests on Christ's command to make disciples of all nations, baptizing and teaching them (Matthew 28:19-20). The promise that God writes His laws in His people's hearts (Hebrews 8:10) is confessed in their own adopted texts by only a few: the Adventist through a voted proof text, the Catholic in sense, the Lutheran in Jeremiah's form, and the Methodist through Wesley's Notes.

**Love proven by obedience.** All sixteen confess that we are saved by grace through faith (Ephesians 2:8-9), and that carries the count. That faith works by love (Galatians 5:6) is confessed in their own words by the Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Anabaptist and Jehovah's Witness traditions, and in sense by the Anglican, Baptist and Holiness. Christ's words "If ye love me, keep my commandments" (John 14:15) are confessed in so many words by the Adventist, Latter-day Saint, Eastern Orthodox and Methodist traditions, and in sense by the Catholic, Anglican and Anabaptist.

**Born of water and of the Spirit.** Christ was baptized "to fulfil all righteousness" (Matthew 3:15). He commanded His apostles to teach all nations, baptizing them (Matthew 28:19), and those who gladly received the word were baptized (Acts 2:41). Christ's baptism and His command to baptize are both held in common by the standard these Articles keep. The Anglican, Reformed, Lutheran, Anabaptist, Pentecostal and Holiness traditions confess in their own adopted texts that Christ ordained baptism with water, and the Anglican, Lutheran and Pentecostal join it to the new birth He spoke of: "Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God" (John 3:5). Others confess the new birth as the Spirit's work and baptism as its sign; the texts that decide the Reformed, Baptist and Holiness counts on John 3:5 speak of the Spirit's work, and the Reformed and Baptist readings are marked borderline. The measurement counts what the churches confess. It does not settle what "water" signifies, or how baptism is related to the new birth. That is not leave to set either aside. Christ's example and His command stand, and every church and every believer is asked to search their own doctrine, pray for wisdom, and follow the Spirit of God in them. The Article's companion verse, that we should seek the Lord and feel after Him (Acts 17:27), was found in the adopted texts of the Catholic and Eastern Orthodox churches; not every family's texts were searched for it.

**The Salvation Army.** The Salvation Army, counted with the Holiness churches, does not practise water baptism or the Lord's Supper. In its own words, "No particular outward observance is necessary to inward grace," and "Christ is the one true Sacrament." Its official Handbook of Doctrine confesses Christ's command to make disciples, His baptism by John, and the gift of the Spirit to those who repent and believe. The Article does not settle whether baptism and the Supper are sacraments or ordinances, or who receives them, so the Army is not counted as disagreeing. Its practice is reported here as it is, beside Christ's example and His command.

**Repent and be baptized.** Peter's answer to "What shall we do?" is Christ's command carried out: "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost" (Acts 2:38). The Baptist churches confess repentance, faith and the baptism of believers, and hold that baptism follows faith and signifies the remission of sins. No Baptist text read in the measurement speaks Acts 2:38's own words, so their reading counts and is marked borderline. How baptism is related to the remission of sins, the measurement does not settle. That is not leave to set the command aside. Every church and every believer, our own included, is asked to search what the apostles meant by it, pray for wisdom, and obey as the Spirit leads.

**Promises some churches confine to part of their people.** The Jehovah's Witnesses hold that being born again belongs to the anointed, and they read "there is neither Jew nor Greek ... in Christ Jesus" (Galatians 3:28) of the "Israel of God." These readings count, because the Article does not settle who receives these promises, and each is marked in the record with its restriction named.

**The Jehovah's Witnesses' footing.** Their teaching is set in publications approved by their Governing Body, counted as the family's adopted teaching for now, while the rule for traditions without creeds is still being settled.

**The Stone-Campbell churches.** These churches hold no binding creed, by conviction, so where they have no adopted text on a point, that is not disagreement. Their lived practice is believer's baptism after the Good Confession of Jesus as the Christ, and Acts 2:38 is central to it, though no adopted text binds it. On the cost of discipleship and on God's welcome of all who come to Him, *The Design for the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)* and its Preamble are counted, each marked borderline.

**Wesley's Notes.** Several Methodist readings rest on John Wesley's *Explanatory Notes upon the New Testament*. The Global Methodist Church names them as a doctrinal standard, and The United Methodist Church includes them among its standards by long understanding and the rulings of its Judicial Council. Each such reading says so in the record.

**Still being confirmed.** Two Oriental Orthodox readings wait for a second edition, or the original language, of the rite they rest on, and are named beside those counts.

## Article 4, Of God's Word

Status: APPROVED by Aaron 19 Oct 2026, 20:28 ET ("Approve Article 4"), including "Two teachings measured twice". BUILD TEXT, subject to the three [VERIFY] items and the [UPDATE] after Stage 2. ### What the measurement found

Seven teachings of this Article were measured. All seven are held in common by the standard these Articles keep, and no tradition's adopted texts were found to teach otherwise on any of them. Where agreement rests on part of a teaching, or on a church's own way of reading it, these notes say so.

**God speaks today.** The Article says that God spoke in ages past, speaks today, and will yet speak. What was measured is that God speaks to His people today by His Spirit (Hebrews 1:1-2; John 16:13; Acts 2:17-18). Whether that speaking includes new revelation carrying doctrinal authority, and whether the canon of Scripture is open, the Article leaves to each tradition. The Westminster Confession and the Baptist Confession of 1689 hold that God's former ways of revealing His will have now ceased, which closes new revelation of that kind. This teaching does not claim it, and in their own words the same churches confess the Spirit's present work in His people, so they are counted as teaching it.

**Two teachings measured twice.** As first worded, the teaching on God's frail servants and the teaching that God's word is preserved for us fell short, only because too few traditions' adopted texts spoke to them; none taught otherwise. By our method, a teaching that falls short through silence alone goes back for review before anything is said of it. Each was drawn closer to the verses on which the Article rests: that we have this treasure in earthen vessels, and that all have sinned (2 Corinthians 4:7; Romans 3:23); and that what was written aforetime was written for our learning (Romans 15:4). Both were then measured again, and both are held in common. The Article's own words did not change.

**A verse that contains the teaching.** Some traditions count on these two teachings through a verse that contains them rather than a sentence that states them: the confession that all have sinned, which includes the servants through whom God works, or a prayer that the scriptures were written for our learning. Where a church's own text states the whole teaching, that text is cited instead. Where none was found, the reading counts and is marked borderline.

**Preserved, but not perfectly.** The Article says that what God has spoken plainly has been preserved for us. It leaves to each tradition whether Scripture is without error, how its text came down to us, and whether anything once written has been lost. Many Christians hold that God's word was preserved but not perfectly, and among them is The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, this ministry's own faith. Its scriptures teach that plain and precious things were taken from the Bible (1 Nephi 13:26-29), and that the Bible is the word of God as far as it is translated correctly (Articles of Faith 1:8). They also teach that the Lord's word shall not pass away but shall all be fulfilled (D&C 1:38; Joseph Smith—Matthew 1:35). Read on its own terms, that tradition teaches the floor, and it is counted.

**The Jehovah's Witnesses' footing.** Their teaching is set in publications approved by their Governing Body, counted as the family's adopted teaching for now, while the rule for traditions without creeds is still being settled.

**The Stone-Campbell churches.** These churches hold no binding creed, by conviction, so where they have no adopted text on a point, that is not disagreement. On Scripture as inspired and leading to Christ, a paragraph of *The Design for the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)* is counted, marked borderline.

## Article 2, Of God

Status: APPROVED by Aaron 19 Oct 2026, 20:30 ET ("Approve Article 2"), with the heading corrected to "and two still being confirmed" as offered. BUILD TEXT, subject to the three [VERIFY] items. On Article 2's Disclosure tab, after the Father of Spirits disclosure and before the method account.

### What the measurement found

Besides the teaching that God is the Father of our spirits, which has its own disclosure above, four teachings of this Article were measured. All four are held in common by the standard these Articles keep, and no tradition's adopted texts were found to teach otherwise on any of them. Where agreement rests on part of a teaching, or on a church's own way of reading it, these notes say so.

**Two teachings measured twice.** As first measured, the teaching that God's purposes are good and His love for His children without limit, and the teaching that no mortal knows the full nature of God, fell short only because too few traditions' adopted texts spoke to them; none taught otherwise. By our method, such a teaching goes back for review before anything is said of it. Review found that each, as first worded, did not test exactly what the Article says: the first did not yet state that God's purposes are good, and the second tested God's ways rather than His nature. Each was re-worded to ask no more and no less than the Article says (Romans 8:28; 1 Corinthians 13:9, 12) and measured again, and both are held in common. The Article's own words did not change.

**Known in part, for now.** Some traditions hold that God's being lies beyond the full knowledge of every creature forever; others hope that His children will come to know Him fully in the life to come. The teaching measured is the part they share: no one in mortality knows the full nature of God, and we know Him as far as He reveals Himself. Whether that limit is for this life only, or forever, the Article leaves to each tradition.

**God in the Highest.** Several traditions confess that God is the Most High by making the angels' song their own: "Glory to God in the highest" (Luke 2:14) stands in the Catechism of the Catholic Church, in the Great Doxology of the Eastern Orthodox churches, in the Coptic Liturgy of St Basil, and at the opening of the Qurbana of the Church of the East. A church that confesses a verse as its own, in its own worship or teaching, makes that verse's claim. The Bible alone, which every tradition receives, never carries a count by itself.

**The Salvation Army's Handbook.** The first part of each chapter of the Salvation Army's *Handbook of Doctrine*, which the Handbook itself describes as the Army's officially approved statement of its doctrine, is counted as the Army's teaching. Its sections "For further exploration", which the Handbook says are not part of that statement, are not.

**A posture, not a measured teaching.** The Article's sentence "Of the eternal functions and hierarchy of heavenly populations we remain carefully neutral and avoid speculation for the sake of interfaith unity" says how this ministry speaks, not what any church teaches, so it was not measured. It asks no tradition to set aside its own teaching on the Godhead; it only keeps this Article from deciding it.

**The Jehovah's Witnesses' footing.** Their teaching is set in publications approved by their Governing Body, counted as the family's adopted teaching for now, while the rule for traditions without creeds is still being settled.

**The Stone-Campbell churches.** These churches hold no binding creed, by conviction, so where they have no adopted text on a point, that is not disagreement. On God in the Highest, the doxology of the Preamble to *The Design for the Christian Church (Disciples of Christ)* is counted, marked borderline.

**Readings marked borderline, and two still being confirmed.** On God's goodness and love, the Adventist, Church of the East and Oriental Orthodox readings count and are marked borderline. For the Church of the East, two readings still wait for a second edition, or the original language, of the text they rest on, and are named beside those counts.
