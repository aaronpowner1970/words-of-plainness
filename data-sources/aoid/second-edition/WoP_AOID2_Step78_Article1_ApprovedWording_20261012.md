## 1. Article text (approved verbatim)

Title: Of Love for God and Neighbor

Paragraph 1:
**We love** the Lord our God with all our heart, and with all our soul, and with all our mind. **We love** our neighbor as ourselves. Jesus called these the first and great commandment and the second like unto it, and said, "On these two commandments hang all the law and the prophets." When a lawyer pressed Him, "And who is my neighbour?" He told of a Samaritan, a man outside the lawyer's own faith, who stopped and showed mercy on a wounded stranger. We are called to be neighbors to all, not only to those who share our faith.

Paragraph 2:
**We do** unto others as we would have them do unto us, for this is the law and the prophets. **We love** our enemies, bless those who curse us, pray for those who persecute us, and overcome evil with good. **We forgive** one another, even as God for Christ's sake has forgiven us, and we ask His forgiveness as we forgive others. **We put away** lying and speak truth with our neighbor.

Paragraph 3:
**We feed** the hungry, welcome the stranger, clothe the naked, and visit the sick and those in prison, for what we do for the least of these, we do for Him. **We do good** unto all, especially unto the household of faith. **We offer** hospitality without grudging, remembering that some have entertained angels unawares. **We pray** for the sick in faith, for "the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up," and we pray as Christ prayed, "Thy will be done." Jesus gave His disciples a new commandment, "That ye love one another; as I have loved you," and said, "By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another."

Reflection prompt (rjw):
Christ commands us to love our neighbors as ourselves. Do you count yourself among those whom God loves? How might recognizing His love for you help you better love yourself and even your most difficult neighbor?

## 4. Floors as amended and ratified (for the repository floor document)

### L8 (AMENDED AND RATIFIED by Aaron 12 Oct 2026)
> The Article confesses only what James 5:14-16 confesses: the disciple prays for the sick, and "the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up"; and he prays as Christ prayed, "Thy will be done" (Matthew 6:10; 26:39, 42). This is the floor. It does not settle whether anointing the sick is a sacrament or ordinance, who may administer it, which gifts of healing continue today, or why some who are prayed for in faith are not healed; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Restoration column (ratified): D&C 42:43-44; D&C 42:46-48.

### L11. A new commandment (RATIFIED by Aaron 12 Oct 2026; unscored)
> The Article confesses only what John 13:34-35 confesses: Christ gave His disciples a new commandment, that they love one another as He has loved them, and by this all men shall know that they are His disciples. This is the floor. It does not settle who counts as "one another," the bounds of the Church, or what visible unity among Christians requires; each tradition fills those in its own house.

Why the exclusion matters: ecclesiology is where traditions that understand themselves as the Church part ways (the Article 8 persona finding).
Restoration column (ratified): D&C 88:123; 4 Nephi 1:15-17; Mosiah 18:8-9.

## 5. L1 panel comment notes (after the floor paragraph) — APPROVED by Aaron 12 Oct 2026 ("Approve notes", 04:56 EDT)

The self-love question lives in L1's panel comment only. Under AOID2-TABS-MIDDLE-TAB, Article 1 has no disclosure of its own, so its Disclosure tab carries the standard generated method line.

Note 1 (self-love, ruling 1):
"As thyself" makes our own good the measure of our love for others. Whether Christ here assumes that measure, or also teaches that a right love of oneself is a duty, the Article does not settle. Scripture's witness that the worth of souls is great in the sight of God (D&C 18:10) is offered in the Restoration column; the reflection invites each reader to consider whether they count themselves among those God loves.

Note 2 (cross-reference, ruling 5):
Compare Article 11 s3-s4, where "If ye love me, keep my commandments" (John 14:15) is joined to the teaching that obedience is an expression of love, not the means of saving ourselves.

Note 3 (existing, from the floor document): the Prologue (A01 s19) uses Matthew 22:40 as the key to reading what Christ taught; here the passage is the commandment itself, and the repetition is intended.
