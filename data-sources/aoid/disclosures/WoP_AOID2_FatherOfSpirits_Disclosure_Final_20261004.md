## Part 1. The footnote under Article 2

One line, marked † at the close of the clause that carries A2-F1, "the Eternal Father of our spirits" (s1), so that the mark falls after "spirits" and before "whose purposes are good". It is not on "We believe in God" (s11), which every tradition confesses, nor on the sentence as a whole, whose other clauses (s12, s2) carry A2-F2 and reach the standard:

> † This teaching is set down as biblically anchored witness rather than as common ground. Of sixteen Christian traditions measured, nine teach in their own adopted texts that God is the Father of our spirits; none teaches otherwise; four hold no common position, and three are still being confirmed. That falls short of the more-than-four-in-five standard these Articles keep. See [*Of the Father of Spirits: A Disclosure of Witness*](/aoid/disclosures/father-of-spirits).

**Why it differs from the Sexual Holiness footnote in one place.** That footnote reports traditions *divided within themselves*. None is divided here, and codex C4(a)(ii) forbids calling silence disagreement, so this footnote says "none teaches otherwise" and reports the silent traditions as holding no common position. Every other word follows the earlier footnote's pattern.

## Part 2. The companion study

### Of the Father of Spirits: A Disclosure of Witness

*Address: /aoid/disclosures/father-of-spirits*

**What was measured.** These Articles keep a plain standard. A teaching is set down as held in common only when more than four in five of the sixteen Christian traditions measured teach it in their own adopted texts, which means at least thirteen of the sixteen. One teaching in Article 2 does not meet that standard. We say so here rather than let the Article imply otherwise.

Each tradition measured is counted as a family: a church, or a group of churches, whose adopted texts can be read. The sixteen are named as they appear on *The Living Tree of Christ-Believing Traditions*:

1. Eastern Orthodox
2. Catholic
3. Lutheran
4. Reformed
5. Anglican
6. Anabaptist
7. Baptist
8. Methodist
9. Pentecostal (with independent charismatic churches)
10. Stone-Campbell
11. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints
12. Adventist
13. Jehovah's Witnesses
14. Oriental Orthodox
15. Church of the East
16. Holiness

The Living Tree shows more branches than these sixteen. Which branches were measured, and which were not, is set out under "What the sixteen cover" at the end of this study.

The list reaches beyond the historic creeds on purpose, because the Articles are offered to everyone who confesses Jesus' name. It includes:

- **Two families outside the Nicene confession:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, this ministry's own faith, and the Jehovah's Witnesses.
- **One family that holds no creed by conviction:** the Stone-Campbell churches.

Including them does not change the result. Counted without The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, the teaching falls shorter still.

Each tradition stands in one of four places on the teaching:

- **Teaches it:** a church of the family teaches it in its own adopted text, and none officially teaches otherwise.
- **Divided:** its own churches, each through its own governing body, have adopted opposite teachings.
- **No common position:** no church in the family has adopted a text on the question either way. That is an absence of position, not a division, and never a disagreement.
- **Being confirmed:** a church of the family has a text that may speak to the point, but its adoption or its wording has not yet been shown. It is not counted until it is.

On the teaching that God is the Father of our spirits, the sixteen stand as follows: nine teach it; none is divided; four have no common position (Pentecostal, Stone-Campbell, Adventist and Holiness); three are being confirmed (Anabaptist, Oriental Orthodox and Church of the East).

**What "No common position" means here.** No tradition measured was found to deny that God is the Father of our spirits. The four that hold no common position are silent on it: their adopted texts confess God as Creator and Preserver of all things, and some confess that we depend on Him for "life and breath", but none speaks to the origin of the human spirit. Silence is reported as silence. Even if all three traditions still being confirmed are found to teach it, the count will be twelve, one short of thirteen. So the teaching cannot be counted as common ground by the standard these Articles keep, and we do not present it as such.

**What the Scriptures say plainly.** The Bible does not leave this teaching in shadow.

- **The Father of spirits:** "Furthermore we have had fathers of our flesh which corrected us, and we gave them reverence: shall we not much rather be in subjection unto the Father of spirits, and live?" ([Hebrews 12:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/12?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)).
- **The spirit returns to its Giver:** "Then shall the dust return to the earth as it was: and the spirit shall return unto God who gave it" ([Ecclesiastes 12:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/eccl/12?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).
- **The God of the spirits of all flesh:** Moses and Aaron cried, "O God, the God of the spirits of all flesh" ([Numbers 16:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/16?lang=eng&id=p22#p22); see [27:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/num/27?lang=eng&id=p16#p16)), and the LORD "formeth the spirit of man within him" ([Zechariah 12:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/zech/12?lang=eng&id=p1#p1)).

**Where the traditions differ.** Christians who confess God as the Father of their spirits do not all describe that fatherhood in the same way. Some confess that God creates each soul directly; some speak of the spirit as His gift, returning to Him at death; the Restoration speaks of His children as begotten sons and daughters. The Article does not settle the manner of that fatherhood. Each tradition fills it in its own house.

**Where these Articles stand.** These Articles stand with the plain reading of Hebrews 12:9. As a covenant of discipleship, they confess that God is the Father of our spirits, and they name no manner of that fatherhood beyond what the verse confesses.

This interfaith ministry makes this confession knowing it is not set down in the adopted texts of every church that confesses Jesus' name, and draws no boundary with it. The one line these Articles draw for fellowship among fellow believers is the confession of Christ's name ([1 Corinthians 12:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/12?lang=eng&id=p3#p3); [1 John 4:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-jn/4?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

**The witness of the Restoration.** For readers of the Restoration scriptures, the same teaching stands in latter-day revelation, and goes further:

- **Begotten of God:** "That by him, and through him, and of him, the worlds are and were created, and the inhabitants thereof are begotten sons and daughters unto God" ([D&C 76:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/76?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)).
- **Created spiritually first:** "For I, the Lord God, created all things, of which I have spoken, spiritually, before they were naturally upon the face of the earth" ([Moses 3:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/3?lang=eng&id=p5#p5)).
- **Returning home:** "the spirits of all men, whether they be good or evil, are taken home to that God who gave them life" ([Alma 40:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/40?lang=eng&id=p11#p11)).

These are amplifying witness, not the Article's claim. The fuller account of the premortal life of the spirit is set out plainly in Volume 2.

**A word to every reader.** To call God "Father" is the first word of the prayer the Lord taught ([Matthew 6:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/6?lang=eng&id=p9#p9)). Hebrews 12:9 takes that word further: the One we address as Father is the Father of the very spirit that prays. Whatever tradition a reader comes from, and whether or not its confession has set this down, the invitation is the same: to come to Him not as a stranger to a Creator, but as a child to a Father who corrects us "for our profit, that we might be partakers of his holiness" ([Hebrews 12:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/12?lang=eng&id=p10#p10)).

Where the Lord's people speak of this fatherhood in different ways, we choose to speak plainly and to love without condition. We leave judgment with the One who alone sees the heart.

**Method notes:**

- **The standard:** C4. A teaching is near-universal only when more than 80 per cent of the sixteen measured traditions, each counted as a family (at least thirteen), teach it in adopted texts (C4, as refined by AOID2-DENOMINATOR-EAST and AOID2-HOLINESS-FAMILY).
- **What counts as a position:** a church's position is any text its competent body has adopted (F.2, AOID2-F2-GENRES). The text must itself make the claim, in the church's own words; a broader or neighbouring claim does not count (C4(a)(i), codex v0.16). A liturgy line counts only when its wording is confirmed in a second independent edition or the original language (B2(f)).
- **Who decides a family's place (C4(a)(iv)).** Where not every church in a family has adopted a text on the point, the churches named in its row decide its place; the family's other churches were silent on the point or were not read.
- **Silence is not disagreement:** every count is reported as taught, contradicted and no position; silence is never described as dispute or division (C4(a)(ii), codex v0.16).
- **What the floor claims:** only what Hebrews 12:9 confesses, God as the "Father of spirits" set against the "fathers of our flesh". The manner of that fatherhood is left to each tradition (A4). The floor is kept at the Article's words and not widened (AOID2-A2F1-FLOOR).
- **A note on the Jehovah's Witnesses.** As in the Sexual Holiness disclosure: their teaching is set in publications approved by their Governing Body, counted as the family's adopted teaching for now, while the rule for traditions without creeds is still being settled. It cannot change the outcome here: without them the count is eight.

  Notes on the table:

  - **Evangelical and Dispensationalism are movements, not traditions of their own.** Each is a way of believing that runs across many churches rather than a church in itself. Evangelical Christians are found among Baptists, Reformed, Anglicans, Methodists, Pentecostals and non-denominational congregations; Dispensationalism, a way of reading biblical prophecy and history, is held chiefly in Baptist, Bible and independent churches and taught through schools and study Bibles. Neither has a governing body of its own that adopts doctrine binding on its churches. Associations such as the National Association of Evangelicals and the World Evangelical Alliance publish statements of faith, but they are fellowships that churches join; each member church's teaching is still set by its own governing body. These Articles therefore count Evangelical and Dispensationalist believers where their own churches stand. On this teaching, their voices are in the count among those who teach it through the Presbyterian Church in America and the Orthodox Presbyterian Church, which hold the Westminster Confession; through the Reformed Baptist churches that hold the Second London Confession of 1689; and through the Global Methodist Church, which holds Wesley's Sermons as a doctrinal standard. Where their churches have adopted no text on the point, as with the Assemblies of God and the other Pentecostal bodies read, they stand with their family as holding no common position. That is silence, not dissent.
  - **Non-denominational churches are not heard as a family, and that is a real gap.** Each congregation governs itself, with no body above it that adopts teaching, so a method built on adopted texts cannot read them at family scale. They are many, tens of millions in the United States alone, and their absence from the count is disclosed here rather than explained away.
  - **Holiness is measured as a family of its own (AOID2-HOLINESS-FAMILY, 1 Oct 2026).** The Holiness churches descend from Methodism and share Wesley's teaching on sanctification, but the Living Tree shows them as their own branch, and they govern themselves. The Free Methodist Church is counted with Holiness. Churches that are both Holiness and Pentecostal, such as the Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee), the Church of God in Christ and the International Pentecostal Holiness Church, are counted with Pentecostal. Two churches sometimes listed with Holiness are counted elsewhere: the Christian and Missionary Alliance calls itself an evangelical denomination and its teaching on sanctification is not Wesleyan, so it is counted as part of the Evangelical movement; the Brethren in Christ describe themselves as shaped by Anabaptist and Pietist roots and are counted with the Anabaptists.
  - **The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints means that Church alone.** Churches that separated from it, including Community of Christ, are distinct bodies and are not counted with it, as Catholic and Eastern Orthodox are not counted together.
  - **Eastern Orthodox is scored on its own adopted texts,** which speak for the Eastern churches only. The Oriental Orthodox churches and the Church of the East, separate since the fifth century, are not represented by them; they are measured as families of their own (AOID2-DENOMINATOR-EAST, 1 Oct 2026).

  Ruled 1 Oct 2026: the Oriental Orthodox churches and the Church of the East are added as families 14 and 15 (AOID2-DENOMINATOR-EAST), and the Holiness churches as family 16 (AOID2-HOLINESS-FAMILY). The other unmeasured branches stay outside, disclosed here, because they have no adopted texts the method can read.
