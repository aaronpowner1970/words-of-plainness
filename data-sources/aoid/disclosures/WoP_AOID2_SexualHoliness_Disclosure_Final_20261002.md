## Part 2. The companion study

### Of Sexual Holiness: A Disclosure of Division

*Address: /aoid/disclosures/sexual-holiness*

**What was measured.** These Articles keep a plain standard. A teaching is set down as held in common only when more than four in five of the sixteen Christian traditions measured teach it in their own adopted texts, which means at least thirteen of the sixteen. Two teachings in Article 11 do not meet that standard. We say so here rather than let the Article imply otherwise.

Each tradition measured is counted as a family: a church, or a group of churches, whose adopted texts can be read. The sixteen are named as they appear on *The Living Tree of Christ-Believing Traditions*:

1. Eastern Orthodox
2. Catholic
3. Lutheran
4. Reformed
5. Anglican
6. Anabaptist
7. Baptist
8. Methodist
9. Pentecostal (with independent charismatic churches)
10. Stone-Campbell
11. The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints
12. Adventist
13. Jehovah's Witnesses
14. Oriental Orthodox
15. Church of the East
16. Holiness

The Living Tree shows more branches than these sixteen. Which branches were measured, and which were not, is set out under "What the sixteen cover" at the end of this study.

The list reaches beyond the historic creeds on purpose, because the Articles are offered to everyone who confesses Jesus' name. It includes:

- **Two families outside the Nicene confession:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, this ministry's own faith, and the Jehovah's Witnesses.
- **One family that holds no creed by conviction:** the Stone-Campbell churches.

Including them does not change the result. Counted without The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, both teachings fall shorter still.

Each tradition stands in one of three places on each teaching:

- **Teaches it:** its churches teach it in their adopted texts, and none officially teaches otherwise.
- **Divided:** its own churches, each through its own governing body, have adopted opposite teachings.
- **No common position:** no church in the family has adopted a text on the question either way. That is an absence of position, not a division.

On the two teachings in Article 11, the sixteen stand as follows:

- **Within marriage** (that sexual relations belong within marriage): eleven teach it; four are divided (Lutheran, Reformed, Methodist and Baptist); one has no common position (Stone-Campbell).
- **A man and a woman** (that marriage is between a man and a woman): ten teach it; five are divided (Lutheran, Anglican, Reformed, Methodist and Baptist); one has no common position (Stone-Campbell).

**What "Divided" means.** A family is shown as divided when its own churches, each speaking through its own governing body, have adopted opposite teachings. It does not mean the family has no teaching, or that its people lack conviction. In every divided family named here, churches of the family hold the teaching plainly: among the Baptists, for example, the Southern Baptist Convention and most Baptist bodies whose texts were read. But another church of the same family, by its own adopted text, teaches otherwise, and so the family cannot be counted as one voice. These Articles do not choose between the churches of a divided family. They report the division as it stands. A reader whose own tradition is named here is invited to learn how that division came about in their tradition's history and governance, and to raise their questions where they can be answered: within their own community of worshipers.

**What the Scriptures say plainly.** The Bible does not leave this teaching in shadow.

- **On marriage and the bed:** "Marriage is honourable in all, and the bed undefiled: but whoremongers and adulterers God will judge" ([Hebrews 13:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/13?lang=eng&id=p4#p4)).
- **On holiness:** "For this is the will of God, even your sanctification, that ye should abstain from fornication" ([1 Thessalonians 4:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-thes/4?lang=eng&id=p3#p3)). "For God hath not called us unto uncleanness, but unto holiness" ([4:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-thes/4?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).
- **On marriage from the beginning:** when Jesus was asked about marriage, he answered from the beginning: "Have ye not read, that he which made them at the beginning made them male and female, And said, For this cause shall a man leave father and mother, and shall cleave to his wife: and they twain shall be one flesh? Wherefore they are no more twain, but one flesh. What therefore God hath joined together, let not man put asunder" ([Matthew 19:4–6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/19?lang=eng&id=p4-p6#p4); see [Genesis 2:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/gen/2?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)).

**Where Christians divide.** Christians who love these same Scriptures still disagree. The disagreement is not over whether these words stand in the Bible. It is over how far they reach. Two questions carry it:

- **How wide is the word?** Does the Bible's word for sexual immorality bind every sexual relationship outside marriage, or a narrower set of wrongs?
- **Pattern or definition?** Does the Lord's appeal to "male and female" define marriage for every time and place, or describe the pattern he was asked about?

Most of the families answer that these words reach as far as their plain sense carries them. In some families, member churches have answered differently through their own governing assemblies. Some now speak of fidelity "in marriage and other covenanted relationships," or of faithfulness to a "spouse or partner," while some of these churches still keep, as an aspiration, that their members abstain until married. Others now marry two men or two women by their own rites.

We report this division as it stands. We do not settle it here, and we count no one outside Christ's reach because of it.

**Where these Articles stand.** These Articles stand with the plain reading. As a covenant of discipleship, they confess two things:

- **Within marriage:** sexual relations belong within marriage.
- **Ordained of God:** marriage is the union of a man and a woman, ordained of God.

This interfaith ministry makes this confession knowing it is not shared by every church that confesses Jesus' name, and draws no boundary with it. The one line these Articles draw for fellowship among fellow believers is the confession of Christ's name ([1 Corinthians 12:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/12?lang=eng&id=p3#p3); [1 John 4:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-jn/4?lang=eng&id=p2#p2)).

**The witness of the Restoration.** For readers of the Restoration scriptures, the same teaching stands in latter-day revelation:

- **Fidelity:** "Thou shalt love thy wife with all thy heart, and shalt cleave unto her and none else" ([D&C 42:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/42?lang=eng&id=p22#p22)).
- **Marriage ordained:** "Marriage is ordained of God unto man… and they twain shall be one flesh" ([D&C 49:15–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/49?lang=eng&id=p15-p16#p15)). The restored account of the creation repeats the one-flesh union ([Moses 3:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/3?lang=eng&id=p24#p24)).
- **The heart:** the risen Lord, teaching at the temple, set the standard in the heart itself: "whosoever looketh on a woman, to lust after her, hath committed adultery already in his heart" ([3 Nephi 12:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/12?lang=eng&id=p27-p28#p27)).
- **Beyond adultery alone:** the commandment against adultery reaches to "anything like unto it" ([D&C 59:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/59?lang=eng&id=p6#p6)), and the Lord declares, "I, the Lord God, delight in the chastity of women. And whoredoms are an abomination before me" ([Jacob 2:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/2?lang=eng&id=p28#p28)).

**A word to every reader.** This is a hard truth, and it falls on real lives. Many who read these words have walked another road, or love someone who has.

The covenant described here is an invitation into holiness, not a verdict on any soul. No soul finishes in a day. Christ meets each of us where we are and calls us higher. His grace is not a license; it is a power, and it is sufficient for every step along His path.

The home this covenant hopes for is a simple one: "their husbands love their wives, and their wives love their husbands; and their husbands and their wives love their children" ([Jacob 3:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/3?lang=eng&id=p7#p7)).

The divisions in Christendom are real, and they will not be settled by a research count. Every disciple of Christ will one day give account before God, and so each of us owes the Lord an honest reckoning now, with the Scriptures, with the history and governance of our own tradition, and with the reasons our brothers and sisters have come to differ. If something here gives offence, let the offence fall where it belongs: on any unacceptable degree of certainty, our own included, that could render our worldview misguided or harmful to ourselves or to those around us.

Where the Lord's people disagree, we choose to speak plainly and to love without condition. We leave judgment with the One who alone sees the heart.

**Method notes:**

- **The standard:** C4. A teaching is near-universal only when more than 80 per cent of the sixteen measured traditions, each counted as a family (at least thirteen), teach it in adopted texts (C4, as refined by AOID2-DENOMINATOR-EAST and AOID2-HOLINESS-FAMILY). A family whose member churches officially disagree does not count.
- **What counts as a position:** a church's position is any text its competent body has adopted (F.2, AOID2-F2-GENRES). Each body is tested against its own definition of marriage (A4(c)).
- **Who decides a family's place (C4(a)(iv)).** Where not every church in a family has adopted a text on the point, the churches named in its row decide its place; the family's other churches were silent on the point or were not read.
- **A note on the Jehovah's Witnesses.** The Jehovah's Witnesses have no creed or confession; their teaching is set in publications approved by their Governing Body. These Articles count such publications as the family's adopted teaching for now, while the rule for traditions without creeds is still being settled. If that rule changes, their row will be scored again. It cannot change the outcome here: with or without them, both teachings fall short of the standard.
- **What the sixteen cover.** *The Living Tree of Christ-Believing Traditions* is a devotional map, not an institutional genealogy. Its rows group traditions by historical setting, theological affinity, cultural relationship, revival influence or restorationist self-understanding. A row is a grouping for the eye, not a measured family. Each family measured is a church, or group of churches, whose adopted texts can be read.

  Notes on the table:

  - **Evangelical and Dispensationalism are movements, not traditions of their own.** Each is a way of believing that runs across many churches rather than a church in itself. Evangelical Christians are found among Baptists, Reformed, Anglicans, Methodists, Pentecostals and non-denominational congregations; Dispensationalism, a way of reading biblical prophecy and history, is held chiefly in Baptist, Bible and independent churches and taught through schools and study Bibles. Neither has a governing body of its own that adopts doctrine binding on its churches. Associations such as the National Association of Evangelicals and the World Evangelical Alliance publish statements of faith, but they are fellowships that churches join; each member church's teaching is still set by its own governing body. These Articles therefore count Evangelical and Dispensationalist believers where their own churches stand: their voices are already in the count, through the Southern Baptist Convention, the Presbyterian Church in America, the Anglican Church in North America, the Assemblies of God and others.
  - **Non-denominational churches are not heard as a family, and that is a real gap.** Each congregation governs itself, with no body above it that adopts teaching, so a method built on adopted texts cannot read them at family scale. They are many, tens of millions in the United States alone, and their absence from the count is disclosed here rather than explained away.
  - **The Metropolitan Community Churches are not counted with any family (AOID2-MCC-PLACEMENT, 8 Oct 2026).** They govern themselves through their own General Conference, and their adopted texts provide a rite of holy matrimony for "the spiritual joining of two persons." Their own Statement of Faith does not place them within the Pentecostal family or any other family measured here, so they are placed by their own description and stand outside the sixteen. Had they been counted with the Pentecostal churches, that family would be divided on the second teaching, and 29b would fall to 9 of 16.
  - **Holiness is measured as a family of its own (AOID2-HOLINESS-FAMILY, 1 Oct 2026).** The Holiness churches descend from Methodism and share Wesley's teaching on sanctification, but the Living Tree shows them as their own branch, and they govern themselves. The Free Methodist Church is counted with Holiness. Churches that are both Holiness and Pentecostal, such as the Church of God (Cleveland, Tennessee), the Church of God in Christ and the International Pentecostal Holiness Church, are counted with Pentecostal. Two churches sometimes listed with Holiness are counted elsewhere: the Christian and Missionary Alliance calls itself an evangelical denomination and its teaching on sanctification is not Wesleyan, so it is counted as part of the Evangelical movement; the Brethren in Christ describe themselves as shaped by Anabaptist and Pietist roots and are counted with the Anabaptists. Both teach both teachings in their own adopted texts, so neither placement changes a count.
  - **The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints means that Church alone.** Churches that separated from it, including Community of Christ, are distinct bodies and are not counted with it, as Catholic and Eastern Orthodox are not counted together.
  - **Eastern Orthodox is scored on the Holy and Great Council (Crete 2016),** which speaks for the Eastern churches only. The Oriental Orthodox churches and the Church of the East, separate since the fifth century, are not represented by it; they are measured as families of their own (AOID2-DENOMINATOR-EAST, 1 Oct 2026).

  Ruled 1 Oct 2026: the Oriental Orthodox churches and the Church of the East are added as families 14 and 15 (AOID2-DENOMINATOR-EAST), and the Holiness churches as family 16 (AOID2-HOLINESS-FAMILY). The other unmeasured branches stay outside, disclosed here, because they have no adopted texts the method can read.
